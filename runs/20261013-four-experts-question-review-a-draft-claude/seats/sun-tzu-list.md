# 孫子 / 孫武 (Sun Tzu) — final list

## Changes from the top 12

1. **#1 Count before you commit: corrected, now #1.** I kept the five matters, the seven comparisons and the temple reckoning. I removed "three arms, then a personnel decision", the duration cap and "if the count fails, go", and fixed the daily cost to 「日費千金」 (codex row 1). The cost-and-duration half is its own item again (#8). Codex's note on review #4 is right that the text calls for speed (「兵聞拙速」, 「兵之情主速」), so it is not just a line inside the count. I added 軍形's chain 「地生度，度生量，量生數，數生稱，稱生勝」 (new). Because the source itself gives that order, "algorithm" is a fair kind for this item.
   **Partial disagreement with codex:** "leave if the count fails" was not invented. It is 曹操's note on the line: 「不能定計，則退而去也」 (十一家注·計篇). But the main text ties 留/去 (stay/leave) to whether 吾計 (my plan) is heeded, and the commentators split on who stays or leaves. 孟氏 says the subordinate general is kept or removed. 陳皥, 梅堯臣, 王晳 and 張預 say Sunzi is telling the king he himself will stay or leave. So the line is out of the rule.
2. **#2 Decide whether you can fight: corrected, split into #4 and #13.** Every point of codex row 2 that I checked holds:
   - 「吾卒之可以擊」 means our troops *can attack*.
   - 「殆」 means danger, not death. Ames 1993 n.129 also prefers 殆 over the variant 敗.
   - 「一勝一負」 means one win, one loss, not a coin-flip probability.
   - The third half-victory is about not knowing the terrain.
   - The text gives no ordered grading.

   I also removed "you are protected for it". Codex is right that 「退不避罪」 means the general does not avoid blame.
3. **#3 Make yourself unbeatable: corrected, now #2.** I removed "work only on your half" (the words have no "only") and the corollary "nothing for the enemy to study" (codex row 3). Codex is also right that 「不能使敵之必可勝」 does not forbid acting on the enemy (兵勢 has 「以利動之」). I kept the merge with 九變, quoted with 「待之」. I added 「勝兵先勝而後求戰」 from the same 軍形 paragraph.
4. **#4 Escalate down a cost ladder: corrected, now #3.** 上/其次/其下 (best / next / lowest) is a ranking, not a sequence of options to try in turn. The text says nothing about ranking "damage before difficulty". 距闉 is the earthen siege mound, not closing a breach. 「毀人之國而非久也」 means destroying a state *without a long war* (codex row 4).
5. **#5 Deceive deliberately: corrected, now #7. The concealment and adaptation half is split out to #10.**
   - I fixed two reversals. 「形人而我無形」 means *they* are made to show form while I show none. 「人皆知我所以勝之形，而莫知吾所以制勝之形」 says everyone *does* know the visible form of the win; what no one knows is the form by which it was controlled.
   - Only the first four clauses show the opposite of the truth.
   - "If legible you lose it" is removed (codex row 5).
   - I dropped 「其疾如風…」 from the rule because it describes how the army moves, not a decision.
6. **#6 Own the tempo: corrected, now #9.** 勞 means weary. 乖其所之 means to divert or thwart their course: 曹操 says 「乖，戾也。戾其道，示以利害，使敵疑也」. No enemy "orders" are in the text (codex row 6). I added 九地's 「先奪其所愛，則聽矣」.
7. **#7 Change the conditions, not the people: corrected, now #12, with 奇正 (codex "Missing") added.** The source joins the two itself: 「戰勢不過奇正」. I removed "disorder produces panic", "calm produces courage" and "character is an output", and kept 擇人 (choosing people) (codex row 7).
   **Small disagreement with codex:** codex reads 「亂生於治」 as apparent disorder born of real order. That is the reading of 曹操, 杜牧, 梅堯臣 and 張預. 李筌 and 賈林 read it as relying on order breeding disorder (十一家注·勢篇). Since the meaning is disputed, that line is out of the rule. I kept 「勇怯，勢也」, which says the same under both readings. 分數/形名 is left out for the cap (see Gaps).
8. **#8 Move only on advantage, and finish it: corrected, now #5.** I removed "worse than a defeat", "you hold nothing" and "burden of proof" (codex row 8).
   **Beyond codex:** the meaning of 「不修其功」 is itself disputed, so 費留 stays in the words and out of the rule. The readings of 修:
   - Most commentators read it as rewarding merit promptly: 李筌, 杜牧 (「修者，舉也」), 王晳 and 張預.
   - 梅堯臣 reads it as acting on the advantage instead of sitting on it.
   - 曹操 glosses 費留 as 「若水之留，不復還也」.

   So neither the identity's "bring the work to completion" nor the top 12's "carried through" can be stated as his rule.
9. **#9 Match their state, not their strength: corrected, now #18.** I fixed the first check: avoid keen spirit, strike when it flags. The text gives no order of checks, and the closing list is **eight** prescriptions, not "nine refusals"; 「圍師必闕」 is a positive one (codex row 9). 朝/晝/暮 is read as time of day by 杜牧, and as the start, middle and end of a campaign by 孟氏, 梅堯臣 and 王晳 (十一家注·軍爭篇). The rule does not choose between them.
10. **#10 Read which situation you are in: corrected, now #19.** I removed three additions (codex row 10):
    - "one prescribed thing and nothing else";
    - "a second nine for the invader" — the invader passage defines six situations, and the nine 「吾將…」 dispositions that follow add to the first list rather than replace it;
    - "exactly when to advance".

    I changed the kind to decision rule: it is a table from conditions to responses, with no steps. I joined the troops passage because the chapter joins them: 「九地之變，屈伸之利，人情之理，不可不察也」.
11. **#11 Probe, then read the signs: corrected, now #17.** I removed "probe *then* read, *before* you judge anything". 動靜 means movement and stillness, and 死生之地 does not say the ground is already killing them (codex row 11). I changed the kind to decision rule.
12. **#12 Interleave harm with benefit: corrected, split into #6 and #15.** The printed quote had foreign strings inside it ("DISTRACTION erectile", "SPDHH"), so I re-quoted it from the source. I removed "two passes", "columns", "virtues" and "already dissolved" (codex row 12). I joined 五危 to 地形's 六敗 because the text names both 「將之過也」 (the general's faults).

## Final list

Ranking is my reading. Line numbers refer to `runs/20261013-four-seats/sources/sun-tzu/孫子兵法.txt` (Wikisource raw text) and `史記_卷065.txt`.

### 1. Reckon before battle

- **Kind:** algorithm
- **Rule:** Before fighting, lay the war out by five matters (the Way, heaven, ground, the general, law) and compare the two sides on seven questions. Whoever comes out ahead in the reckoning before battle wins, whoever has less does not, and having none is worse still. A second passage gives the reckoning as an ordered chain: the ground gives measurement, measurement gives quantity, quantity gives number, number gives the weighing of the sides, and the weighing gives victory.
- **Their words:** 「兵者，國之大事，死生之地，存亡之道，不可不察也。故經之以五事，校之以七計，而索其情：一曰道，二曰天，三曰地，四曰將，五曰法。」…「道者，令民與上同意，可與之死，可與之生，而不畏危也。」…「故校之以七計而索其情，曰：主孰有道？將孰有能？天地孰得？法令孰行？兵眾孰強？士卒孰練？賞罰孰明？吾以此知勝負矣。」…「夫未戰而廟算勝者，得算多也；未戰而廟算不勝者，得算少也。多算勝，少算不勝，而況於無算乎？」 — "War is a great matter of state, the ground of life and death, the way to survival or ruin; it must be examined. So lay it out by five matters, compare it by seven reckonings, and search out the true situation: the Way, heaven, ground, the general, law… The Way is what makes the people one in intent with their superior, so that they will die with him or live with him and not fear danger… Compare by the seven reckonings: Which ruler has the Way? Which general has ability? Who has heaven and ground? Whose laws and orders are carried out? Whose troops are stronger? Whose officers and men are better trained? Whose rewards and punishments are clearer? By this I know victory and defeat… One who wins the temple reckoning before battle has gained more 算; one who does not has gained fewer. More wins, fewer does not, how much less none at all?"
  「兵法：一曰度，二曰量，三曰數，四曰稱，五曰勝。地生度，度生量，量生數，數生稱，稱生勝。故勝兵若以鎰稱銖，敗兵若以銖稱鎰。」 — "In the method of war: first measurement, second quantity, third number, fourth weighing, fifth victory. Ground gives rise to measurement, measurement to quantity, quantity to number, number to weighing, weighing to victory. So a winning army is like a *yi* weighed against a *zhu*, a losing army like a *zhu* against a *yi*."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》始計第一 (l.12–18); 軍形第四 (l.59).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #1 (corrected); 軍形 chain is new (my research).

### 2. Make yourself unbeatable first; win, then seek battle

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** First make yourself unbeatable, then wait for the enemy to become beatable. Being unbeatable is in your own hands, being beatable is in the enemy's, and you cannot make the enemy certain to be beatable; so the winning army wins first and then seeks battle. Do not rely on the enemy not coming or not attacking; rely on having the means to meet them and on having what cannot be attacked.
- **Their words:** 「昔之善戰者，先為不可勝，以待敵之可勝。不可勝在己，可勝在敵。故善戰者，能為不可勝，不能使敵之必可勝。故曰：勝可知，而不可為。」…「古之所謂善戰者，勝於易勝者也。」…「故善戰者，立於不敗之地，而不失敵之敗也。是故勝兵先勝而後求戰，敗兵先戰而後求勝。」 — "The skilled fighters of old first made themselves unbeatable and waited for the enemy to become beatable. Being unbeatable lies with oneself; being beatable lies with the enemy. So the skilled fighter can make himself unbeatable but cannot make the enemy certain to be beatable. Hence: victory can be known but cannot be made… Those the ancients called skilled fighters won where winning was easy… The skilled fighter stands where he cannot be defeated and does not miss the enemy's defeat. So the winning army wins first and then seeks battle; the losing army fights first and then seeks victory."
  「故用兵之法，無恃其不來，恃吾有以待之；無恃其不攻，恃吾有所不可攻也。」 — "Do not rely on their not coming; rely on having the means to meet them. Do not rely on their not attacking; rely on having what cannot be attacked."
- **Source:** 軍形第四 (l.51, l.57); 九變第八 (l.109).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #3 (corrected)

### 3. Whole over broken; strike plans first, walled cities last

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Prefer keeping things whole to breaking them, at every scale from the state to the five-man squad. Subduing the other side's army without battle is the best of the best; a hundred victories in a hundred battles is not. Rank the targets as follows: plans highest, then alliances, then armies, and attacking walled cities lowest, as the method for when there is no alternative.
- **Their words:** 「凡用兵之法，全國為上，破國次之；全軍為上，破軍次之；全旅為上，破旅次之；全卒為上，破卒次之；全伍為上，破伍次之。是故百戰百勝，非善之善者也；不戰而屈人之兵，善之善者也。故上兵伐謀，其次伐交，其次伐兵，其下攻城。攻城之法，為不得已。修櫓轒轀，具器械，三月而後成；距闉，又三月而後已。將不勝其忿，而蟻附之，殺士三分之一，而城不拔者，此攻之災也。故善用兵者，屈人之兵而非戰也，拔人之城而非攻也，毀人之國而非久也，必以全爭於天下，故兵不頓而利可全，此謀攻之法也。」 — "In using troops, a whole state is best, a broken one next; a whole army best, a broken one next; and so for brigade, company and squad. So a hundred victories in a hundred battles is not the best of the best; to subdue the other's army without battle is the best of the best. So the highest use of troops strikes plans, the next strikes alliances, the next strikes troops, the lowest attacks walled cities. Attacking cities is the method for when there is no alternative: three months to make shields, assault carts and equipment, three more for the earthen mounds; if the general cannot master his anger and sends the men swarming up like ants, a third of them are killed and the city still stands — this is the calamity of attack. So the skilled user of troops subdues the other's army without battle, takes the other's city without attacking it, destroys the other's state without a long war; he contends for all under heaven by keeping things whole, so his troops are not blunted and the gain can be whole."
- **Source:** 謀攻第三 (l.34–38).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #4 (corrected)

### 4. Know whether you can fight; know the other side and yourself

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:**
  - Five things tell you who will win:
    - knowing when you can and cannot fight;
    - knowing how to use large and small forces;
    - superiors and subordinates wanting the same thing;
    - being prepared and waiting for the unprepared;
    - an able general whom the ruler does not interfere with.
  - Knowing the other side and yourself, a hundred battles hold no danger. Knowing yourself but not them, you win one and lose one. Knowing neither, every battle is dangerous.
  - Knowing whether your troops can attack and whether the enemy can be attacked, but not whether the ground permits battle, is still only half a victory.
- **Their words:** 「故知勝有五：知可以戰與不可以戰者勝，識衆寡之用者勝，上下同欲者勝，以虞待不虞者勝，將能而君不御者勝。此五者，知勝之道也。故曰：知彼知己，百戰不殆；不知彼而知己，一勝一負；不知彼不知己，每戰必殆。」 — "There are five ways to know victory: one who knows when he can fight and when he cannot wins; one who knows how to use many and few wins; one whose superiors and subordinates want the same thing wins; one who, prepared, waits for the unprepared wins; one whose general is able and whose ruler does not interfere wins. These five are the way to know victory. Hence: know the other and know yourself, and in a hundred battles you are not in danger; not knowing the other but knowing yourself, one win, one loss; knowing neither, in every battle you are in danger."
  「知吾卒之可以擊，而不知敵之不可擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，而不知吾卒之不可以擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，知吾卒之可以擊，而不知地形之不可以戰，勝之半也。…故曰：知彼知己，勝乃不殆；知天知地，勝乃可全。」 — "Knowing our troops can attack but not that the enemy cannot be attacked is half a victory; knowing the enemy can be attacked but not that our troops cannot attack is half a victory; knowing the enemy can be attacked and our troops can attack, but not that the ground does not permit battle, is half a victory… Hence: know the other and yourself, and victory is not in danger; know heaven and ground, and victory can be whole."
- **Source:** 謀攻第三 (l.46–48); 地形第十 (l.135).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #2 (corrected; the authority half is now #13)

### 5. Move only when it accords with advantage; never from anger

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Do not move without advantage, do not use troops without gain, do not fight unless in danger; a ruler must not raise an army out of anger, nor a general give battle out of resentment. Move when it accords with advantage and stop when it does not. Anger can turn back into joy, but a destroyed state cannot be restored and the dead cannot be brought back.
- **Their words:** 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』。故曰：明主慮之，良將修之，非利不動，非得不用，非危不戰。主不可以怒而興師，將不可以慍而致戰。合於利而動，不合於利而止。怒可以復喜，慍可以復悅，亡國不可以復存，死者不可以復生。故明主慎之，良將警之，此安國全軍之道也。」 — "To win battles and take objectives without 修 the achievement is ill-omened; it is called 費留. Hence the enlightened ruler considers it and the good general attends to it: do not move without advantage, do not use troops without gain, do not fight unless in danger. A ruler must not raise an army out of anger; a general must not give battle out of resentment. Move when it accords with advantage; stop when it does not. Anger can become joy again, resentment can become pleasure again; a destroyed state cannot exist again, the dead cannot live again. So the enlightened ruler is careful of it and the good general is wary of it: this is the way to keep the state secure and the army whole." On 修 and 費留, the commentators split; see Changes #8.
  The same gate is in 九地: 「合於利而動，不合於利而止。」
  The bamboo 〈見吳王〉 gives the same stance in dialogue. It is in English only here; I did not open a Chinese transcription. Ames translates: "Using the military is to gain the advantage; it is not a matter of being fond of it… it is not a matter of sport."
- **Source:** 火攻第十二 (l.159); 九地第十一 (l.140). For 〈見吳王〉: Roger T. Ames, *Sun-tzu: The Art of Warfare* (Ballantine, 1993), Part II ch.5, from the 1985 銀雀山 edition.
- **Words are:** `quoted` (火攻, 九地). The 〈見吳王〉 line is `attributed`: it is a narrated dialogue, and Ames judges that both it and the 史記 drill story are later than the core text.
- **From:** top-12 #8 (corrected)

### 6. In deliberating, mix harm into benefit

- **Kind:** mindset
- **Rule:** The wise always mix benefit and harm together when they deliberate. With benefit mixed in, the undertaking can be carried out; with harm mixed in, trouble can be resolved. Whoever does not fully know the harm of using troops cannot fully know its benefit.
- **Their words:** 「是故智者之慮，必雜於利害，雜於利而務可信也，雜於害而患可解也。」 — "So the wise one's deliberation always mixes benefit and harm: mixed with benefit, the undertaking can be carried out (杜牧: 「信，申也」); mixed with harm, trouble can be resolved." 「故不盡知用兵之害者，則不能盡知用兵之利也。」 — "Whoever does not fully know the harm of using troops cannot fully know its benefit."
- **Source:** 九變第八 (l.107); 作戰第二 (l.21).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #12 (corrected; first half)

### 7. War is the way of deception

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** War is the way of deception:
  - When able, appear unable; when using troops, appear not to; when near, appear far; when far, appear near.
  - Act on the enemy's condition: lure, take in disorder, prepare against the solid, avoid the strong, provoke the angry, make the humble arrogant, tire the rested, split the close-knit.
  - Attack where they are unprepared, and come out where they do not expect.

  War stands on deceit, moves by advantage, and changes by dividing and combining.
- **Their words:** 「兵者，詭道也。故能而示之不能，用而示之不用，近而示之遠，遠而示之近。利而誘之，亂而取之，實而備之，強而避之，怒而撓之，卑而驕之，佚而勞之，親而離之。攻其無備，出其不意。此兵家之勝，不可先傳也。」 — "War is the way of deception. So when able, show inability; when using, show non-use; when near, show far; when far, show near. With advantage, lure them; in disorder, take them; solid, prepare against them; strong, avoid them; angry, stir them; humble, make them arrogant; rested, tire them; close, separate them. Attack where they are unprepared; come out where they do not expect. These are the strategist's victories; they cannot be handed down in advance." 「故兵以詐立，以利動，以分合為變者也。」 — "So war is established by deceit, moved by advantage, and varied by dividing and combining."
- **Source:** 始計第一 (l.16); 軍爭第七 (l.96).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #5 (corrected; concealment and adaptation are now #10)

### 8. Value victory, not duration; speed; supply from the enemy

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** In war, value victory and not duration. A long campaign blunts the troops, exhausts strength and leaves the state short, and no state has ever benefited from a long war: clumsy speed is heard of, skilful prolongation has never been seen. Do not levy twice or haul grain three times; take provisions from the enemy, for one measure of his grain equals twenty of one's own.
- **Their words:** 「凡用兵之法，馳車千駟，革車千乘，帶甲十萬，千里饋糧，…日費千金，然後十萬之師舉矣。其用戰也，貴勝，久則鈍兵挫銳，攻城則力屈，久暴師則國用不足。…故兵聞拙速，未睹巧之久也。夫兵久而國利者，未之有也。」…「善用兵者，役不再籍，糧不三載；取用於國，因糧於敵，故軍食可足也。」…「故智將務食於敵，食敵一鍾，當吾二十鍾。」…「故兵貴勝，不貴久。」 — "A thousand fast chariots, a thousand armoured carts, a hundred thousand armoured men, grain carried a thousand *li*… a thousand gold a day, and only then can an army of a hundred thousand be raised. In using it to fight, value victory: long, and it blunts the troops and dulls their edge; attacking cities, strength is spent; the army long in the field, the state's means fall short… So in war one hears of clumsy speed, but has not seen skill that is prolonged. A long war that benefits the state has never been… The skilled user of troops does not levy twice nor haul grain three times; he takes equipment from his own state and provisions from the enemy, so the army's food is enough… So the wise general strives to eat from the enemy: one *zhong* of the enemy's food equals twenty of mine… So in war value victory, not duration."
  「故兵之情主速，乘人之不及，由不虞之道，攻其所不戒也。」 — "The nature of war is to rely on speed: take advantage of the other's not being in time, go by roads they do not anticipate, attack where they are not on guard."
- **Source:** 作戰第二 (l.21, 23, 27, 31); 九地第十一 (l.140).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #1 (split back out; it was top-10 #4)

### 9. Bring the other side to you; do not be brought

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Whoever is first on the field and awaits the enemy is fresh; whoever comes later and hurries into battle is weary. So the skilled fighter brings others and is not brought by them: he draws them with advantage and keeps them off with harm.
  - When he wants battle, he attacks what they must rescue.
  - When he does not, he diverts them from their course.
  - Against a large, ordered enemy, he first seizes what they cherish, and they comply.
- **Their words:** 「凡先處戰地而待敵者佚，後處戰地而趨戰者勞。故善戰者，致人而不致於人。能使敵自至者，利之也；能使敵不得至者，害之也。」…「故我欲戰，敵雖高壘深溝，不得不與我戰者，攻其所必救也；我不欲戰，雖畫地而守之，敵不得與我戰者，乖其所之也。」 — "Whoever occupies the field first and awaits the enemy is at ease; whoever occupies it later and hurries to battle is weary. So the skilled fighter brings others and is not brought by others. What makes the enemy come of himself is advantage; what keeps him from coming is harm… When I want battle, though the enemy has high walls and deep moats, he must fight me, because I attack what he must rescue; when I do not want battle, though I only draw a line on the ground and hold it, he cannot fight me, because I divert him from where he is going."
  「敢問：敵衆整而將來，待之若何？曰：先奪其所愛，則聽矣。」 — "May I ask: the enemy, many and in order, is about to come; how do I meet him? Answer: first seize what he cherishes, and he will comply."
- **Source:** 虛實第六 (l.75–81); 九地第十一 (l.140).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #6 (corrected)

### 10. Avoid the solid, strike the empty; have no constant form

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Make the enemy show its form while you show none: then you are concentrated and they are divided, and an enemy that prepares everywhere is thin everywhere. Like water, which avoids the high and runs to the low, an army avoids the solid, strikes the empty and shapes its victory by the enemy. It has no fixed 勢 and no constant form, and its victories are not repeated.
- **Their words:** 「故形人而我無形，則我專而敵分。…無所不備，則無所不寡。寡者，備人者也；衆者，使人備己者也。」…「人皆知我所以勝之形，而莫知吾所以制勝之形。故其戰勝不復，而應形於無窮。」…「夫兵形象水，水之行，避高而趨下；兵之勝，避實而擊虛。水因地而制行，兵因敵而制勝。故兵無成勢，無恒形，能因敵變化而取勝者，謂之神。」 — "So I make others take form while I have none; then I am concentrated and the enemy divided… Preparing everywhere, he is few everywhere. Being few is preparing against others; being many is making others prepare against you… Everyone knows the form by which I won, but no one knows the form by which I controlled the victory. So the victories are not repeated, and the response to forms is without end… The army's form is like water: water in its course avoids the high and hastens to the low; an army in its victory avoids the solid and strikes the empty. Water shapes its course by the ground; an army shapes its victory by the enemy. So an army has no fixed 勢 and no constant form; one who can take victory by changing with the enemy is called divine."
- **Source:** 虛實第六 (l.83, 87, 89).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #5 (split out) together with codex "Missing" (adaptation without a fixed form)

### 11. Foreknowledge must come from people; pay for it

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Foreknowledge cannot be had from spirits, from likeness to past events, or from measurement; it must be got from people who know the enemy's situation. It is the height of inhumanity to keep a war going for years, at a ruinous daily cost, for a victory decided in one day, yet grudge ranks, salaries and a hundred pieces of gold and so not know the enemy's situation. Of the five kinds of spy, knowledge rests on the converted spy, so he must be treated most generously.
- **Their words:** 「凡興師十萬，出征千里，百姓之費，公家之奉，日費千金，…相守數年，以爭一日之勝，而愛爵祿百金，不知敵之情者，不仁之至也，非人之將也，非主之佐也，非勝之主也。故明君賢將，所以動而勝人，成功出於眾者，先知也。先知者，不可取於鬼神，不可象於事，不可驗於度，必取於人，知敵之情者也。」…「五間之事，主必知之，知之必在於反間，故反間不可不厚也。」 — "Raising a hundred thousand and marching a thousand *li*, the people's spending and the state's outlay come to a thousand gold a day… To hold out for several years to contend for one day's victory, yet begrudge ranks, salaries and a hundred gold and so not know the enemy's situation, is the height of inhumanity: no general of men, no aid to a ruler, no master of victory. What lets the enlightened ruler and the wise general move and defeat others, with achievements beyond the crowd, is foreknowledge. Foreknowledge cannot be taken from ghosts and spirits, cannot be got by analogy with events, cannot be verified by measurement (度; 李筌: 「度，數也」); it must be taken from people who know the enemy's situation… The ruler must know the work of the five kinds of spy; knowing it rests on the converted spy, so the converted spy must be treated generously."
- **Source:** 用間第十三 (l.162, 168).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** new (my research; also codex "Missing"; the identity research quoted the 先知者 line inside its #1)

### 12. Seek it in 勢, made of the regular and the irregular, not from the people

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Battle 勢 is no more than the regular (正) and the irregular (奇): engage with the regular, win with the irregular, and their combinations cannot be exhausted. The skilled fighter seeks victory in 勢 and does not demand it of his people, so he can choose his people and rely on 勢; courage and cowardice are a matter of 勢.
- **Their words:** 「三軍之衆，可使必受敵而無敗者，奇正是也。」…「凡戰者，以正合，以奇勝。…戰勢不過奇正，奇正之變，不可勝窮也。奇正相生，如循環之無端，孰能窮之哉？」…「勇怯，勢也」…「故善戰者，求之於勢，不責於人，故能擇人而任勢。任勢者，其戰人也，如轉木石。木石之性，安則靜，危則動，方則止，圓則行。」 — "That the whole army can take the enemy's attack without defeat is the work of 奇 and 正… In all battle, engage with the regular and win with the irregular… The 勢 of battle are no more than irregular and regular, yet their changes cannot all be exhausted. Irregular and regular give rise to each other like a ring without end; who can exhaust them?… Courage and cowardice are a matter of 勢… So the skilled fighter seeks it in 勢 and does not demand it of people; so he can choose people and rely on 勢. One who relies on 勢 fights with men as one rolls logs and stones: by nature logs and stones are still on level ground and move on a slope; square, they stop; round, they roll." 勢 is defined in 始計 as 「勢者，因利而制權也」: "勢 is shaping one's leverage by advantage."
- **Source:** 兵勢第五 (l.62, 64, 68, 72); 始計第一 (l.16).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #7 (corrected) with codex "Missing" (奇正) joined by the source's own 「戰勢不過奇正」

### 13. Not every road is taken, and not every command of the ruler is accepted

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** There are roads not to be taken, armies not to be struck, cities not to be attacked, ground not to be contested, and commands of the ruler not to be accepted. When the way of battle means sure victory and the ruler says do not fight, fighting is permissible; when it means no victory and the ruler says fight, not fighting is permissible. The general advances without seeking fame and retreats without avoiding blame, protecting only the people.
- **Their words:** 「途有所不由，軍有所不擊，城有所不攻，地有所不爭，君命有所不受。」 — "There are roads one does not take, armies one does not strike, cities one does not attack, ground one does not contest, ruler's commands one does not accept."
  「故戰道必勝，主曰無戰，必戰可也；戰道不勝，主曰必戰，無戰可也。是故進不求名，退不避罪，唯民是保，而利合於主，國之寶也。」 — "So when the way of battle means sure victory, though the ruler says do not fight, fighting is permissible; when the way of battle does not mean victory, though the ruler says fight, not fighting is permissible. So one who advances not seeking fame and retreats not avoiding blame, protecting only the people and whose gain accords with the ruler's, is the treasure of the state."
  謀攻 names three ways the ruler brings trouble on his army: 「不知軍之不可以進而謂之進，不知軍之不可以退而謂之退，是為縻軍；不知三軍之事，而同三軍之政，則軍士惑矣；不知三軍之權，而同三軍之任，則軍士疑矣。」 — "ordering advance or retreat without knowing whether the army can, which is hobbling the army; sharing in the army's administration without knowing its affairs, which confuses the soldiers; sharing in its command without knowing its expedients, which makes them doubt."
  The 史記 drill story shows him acting on this: 「臣既已受命為將，將在軍，君命有所不受。」 — "Having received the command as general, while the general is with the army there are commands of the ruler he does not accept."
  The bamboo 〈四變〉 explains the clause. It is in English only here (Ames): "That there are commands from the ruler not to be obeyed means that if the commands of the ruler are contrary to these four contingencies, do not obey them."
- **Source:** 九變第八 (l.105); 地形第十 (l.131); 謀攻第三 (l.44); 《史記》卷六十五 孫子吳起列傳 (l.13); 〈四變〉 in Ames 1993, Part II ch.2.
- **Words are:** `quoted` (九變, 地形, 謀攻). The 史記 line is `attributed` (司馬遷's narrative). 〈四變〉 is `attributed`: Ames reads it as commentary on the core text.
- **From:** top-12 #2 (the authority half, corrected); 九變, 史記 and 〈四變〉 are new (my research).

### 14. Choose the action by the ratio of forces

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** With ten to one, surround; five to one, attack; two to one, divide; equal, you can fight; fewer, you can hold (a variant reads "slip away"); outmatched, you can avoid. A small force that holds stubbornly is taken by a large one. Numbers are not valued for their own sake: it is enough not to advance recklessly, to concentrate strength, size up the enemy and take men, and whoever lacks forethought and slights the enemy is sure to be captured.
- **Their words:** 「故用兵之法，十則圍之，五則攻之，倍則分之，敵則能戰之，少則能守之，不若則能避之。故小敵之堅，大敵之擒也。」 — "So in using troops: ten to one, surround them; five to one, attack them; double, divide them; equal, be able to fight them; fewer, be able to hold; not a match, be able to avoid them. So the stubbornness of a small force is the capture of a large one."
  「故兵非貴益多也，惟無武進，足以併力、料敵、取人而已。夫惟無慮而易敵者，必擒於人。」 — "So in war more is not what is valued; only do not advance rashly; enough to combine strength, size up the enemy and take men. Only one who lacks forethought and slights the enemy is sure to be captured by others."
  Readings differ:
  - 十一家注 reads 「少則能逃之」.
  - On 倍則分之, 曹操 reads "divide your own force into regular and irregular" (「以二敵一，則一術爲正，一術爲奇」), 王晳 reads it as splitting into two forces so the enemy is hit front and back, and 陳皥 as sending part of the force at what the enemy must rescue, so that the enemy divides.
  - Ames 1993 (n.125) swaps the 倍 and 敵 clauses by emendation.
- **Source:** 謀攻第三 (l.40); 行軍第九 (l.122); 十一家注孫子·謀攻篇 (Wikisource).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** new (my research)

### 15. The general's faults: five dangers, six defeats

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Examine the general for five dangers, each a handle for the enemy:
  - one set on dying can be killed;
  - one set on living can be captured;
  - the quick-tempered can be provoked by insult;
  - the scrupulous about honour can be shamed;
  - one who loves the people can be harried.

  The six kinds of defeat (flight, slackness, sinking, collapse, disorder, rout) are not disasters from heaven but the general's faults, and the text names the cause of each.
- **Their words:** 「故將有五危︰必死，可殺也﹔必生，可虜也﹔忿速，可侮也﹔廉潔，可辱也﹔愛民，可煩也。凡此五者，將之過也，用兵之災也。覆軍殺將，必以五危，不可不察也。」 — "The general has five dangers: set on dying, he can be killed; set on living, he can be captured; quick to anger, he can be insulted; scrupulous, he can be shamed; loving the people, he can be troubled. These five are the general's faults, the calamities of using troops. The overturned army and the slain general come from the five dangers; they must be examined."
  「故兵有走者、有弛者、有陷者、有崩者、有亂者、有北者。凡此六者，非天之災，將之過也。夫勢均，以一擊十，曰走；卒强吏弱，曰弛；吏强卒弱，曰陷；大吏怒而不服，遇敵懟而自戰，將不知其能，曰崩；將弱不嚴，教道不明，吏卒無常，陳兵縱橫，曰亂；將不能料敵，以少合衆，以弱擊強，兵無選鋒，曰北。」 — "Armies have flight, slackness, sinking, collapse, disorder and rout. These six are not calamities from heaven but the general's faults. With 勢 equal, striking ten with one is flight; strong men and weak officers is slackness; strong officers and weak men is sinking; senior officers angry and insubordinate, fighting on their own from resentment when they meet the enemy, with the general not knowing their ability, is collapse; a weak, lax general, unclear instruction, officers and men without constancy and ranks every which way is disorder; a general who cannot size up the enemy, meets many with few and strong with weak, and has no picked vanguard, is rout."
- **Source:** 九變第八 (l.111); 地形第十 (l.129).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #12 (the 五危 half, corrected); 六敗 is new (my research).

### 16. Orders clear first, then enforced; care that cannot command is useless

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** If orders are not clear and not drilled in, it is the general's fault; if they are clear and still not followed, it is the officers' fault. Punish troops before they are attached to you and they will not submit, but once they are attached, if punishment is not carried out, they cannot be used. Care without the power to direct and command makes soldiers like spoiled children: useless.
- **Their words:** 「約束不明，申令不熟，將之罪也；既已明而不如法者，吏士之罪也。」 — "If the rules are not clear and the orders not made familiar, it is the general's fault; if they are already clear and are not followed as the law requires, it is the officers' fault." In the story he says the first sentence alone after the first failure, then repeats the orders before the second.
  The bamboo 〈見吳王〉 has the same rule. It is in English only here (Ames): "If one does not order them, or if one's orders are not understood, it is the fault of the commander. If one has already issued orders and has explained them, it is the fault of the field officers."
  「卒未親附而罰之，則不服，不服則難用也。卒已親附而罰不行，則不可用也。故令之以文，齊之以武，是謂必取。」 — "Punish soldiers before they are attached and they will not submit, and if they do not submit they are hard to use. Once they are attached, if punishment is not carried out, they cannot be used. So command them with civility and keep them uniform with martial discipline: this is called sure success."
  「視卒如嬰兒，故可以與之赴深溪；視卒如愛子，故可與之俱死。厚而不能使，愛而不能令，亂而不能治，譬若驕子，不可用也。」 — "Regard the soldiers as infants and they will go into deep valleys with you; regard them as beloved sons and they will die with you. Generous but unable to direct, loving but unable to command, disorderly and unable to control: they are like spoiled children, of no use."
- **Source:** 《史記》卷六十五 (l.13); 〈見吳王〉 in Ames 1993, Part II ch.5; 行軍第九 (l.124); 地形第十 (l.133).
- **Words are:** The 史記 and 〈見吳王〉 lines are `attributed`: narrated, and per Ames both versions are later than the core text. The 行軍 and 地形 lines are `quoted`.
- **From:** new (my research; also codex "Missing" for the 行軍/地形 half)

### 17. Learn the enemy by probing and by signs

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Learn the enemy by acting on him:
  - reckon him (策) to know the gains and losses of his plans;
  - stir him (作) to learn the pattern of his movement and stillness;
  - make him take form (形) to know his ground of life and death;
  - test him in contact (角) to know where he has more and where less.

  Read his intent from what can be seen. Humble words with more preparation mean he will advance; strong words with a forward push mean he will withdraw; asking for peace without a pledge means a plot; half advancing and half withdrawing means a lure. The chapter lists many more signs.
- **Their words:** 「故策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處。」 — "So reckon them to know the calculation of gain and loss; stir them to know the pattern of movement and stillness; give them form to know the ground of death and life; test them to know where there is surplus and shortage."
  「敵近而靜者，恃其險也；遠而挑戰者，欲人之進也；…辭卑而益備者，進也；辭強而進驅者，退也；…無約而請和者，謀也；…半進半退者，誘也；杖而立者，饑也；…見利而不進者，勞也；鳥集者，虛也；夜呼者，恐也；軍擾者，將不重也；旌旗動者，亂也；吏怒者，倦也；…數賞者，窘也；數罰者，困也。」 — "Near and quiet: they rely on their strong ground. Far off and challenging: they want you to advance… Humble words and more preparation: they will advance. Strong words and pressing forward: they will withdraw… Asking for peace without a pledge: a plot… Half advancing, half withdrawing: a lure. Leaning on their weapons as they stand: hungry… Seeing advantage and not advancing: weary. Birds gathering: the camp is empty. Calling out at night: afraid. The camp in uproar: the general lacks weight. Banners moving: disorder. Officers angry: exhausted… Frequent rewards: in straits. Frequent punishments: in distress."
- **Source:** 虛實第六 (l.87); 行軍第九 (l.120).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #11 (corrected)

### 18. Spirit, heart, strength and change: meet theirs with yours

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** An army's spirit can be taken and a general's heart can be taken. Four management rules follow:
  - **Managing spirit:** avoid the enemy's spirit when it is keen, and strike when it flags and turns homeward.
  - **Managing the heart:** await disorder with order, and clamour with calm.
  - **Managing strength:** await the far-come while near, the weary while rested, the hungry while fed.
  - **Managing change:** do not intercept well-ordered banners or strike an imposing array.

  Eight prescriptions:
  1. Do not attack up a high hill.
  2. Do not meet head-on an enemy with a hill at its back.
  3. Do not follow a feigned flight.
  4. Do not attack crack troops.
  5. Do not take bait.
  6. Do not block an army going home.
  7. Leave a gap for a surrounded army.
  8. Do not press a desperate enemy.
- **Their words:** 「三軍可奪氣，將軍可奪心。是故朝氣銳，晝氣惰，暮氣歸。故善用兵者，避其銳氣，擊其惰歸，此治氣者也；以治待亂，以靜待嘩，此治心者也；以近待遠，以佚待勞，以飽待饑，此治力者也；無邀正正之旗，無擊堂堂之陣，此治變者也。」 — "The army's spirit can be taken; the general's heart can be taken. So morning spirit is keen, midday spirit slack, evening spirit turns homeward. So the skilled user of troops avoids keen spirit and strikes the slack and homeward: this is managing spirit. With order await disorder, with stillness await clamour: this is managing the heart. Near, await the distant; at ease, await the weary; fed, await the hungry: this is managing strength. Do not intercept orderly banners, do not strike an imposing array: this is managing change."
  「故用兵之法，高陵勿向，背丘勿逆，佯北勿從，銳卒勿攻，餌兵勿食，歸師勿遏，圍師必闕，窮寇勿迫，此用兵之法也。」 — "So the method of using troops: a high hill, do not face up it; a hill at their back, do not oppose; feigned flight, do not follow; crack troops, do not attack; bait troops, do not swallow; an army going home, do not block; a surrounded army, always leave a gap; a desperate enemy, do not press."
  The commentators read 朝/晝/暮 two ways: 杜牧 takes them as times of day, while 孟氏, 梅堯臣 and 王晳 take them as the start, middle and end of a campaign.
- **Source:** 軍爭第七 (l.100, 102); 十一家注孫子·軍爭篇.
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #9 (corrected)

### 19. Name the ground, act as prescribed; troops with no way out fight

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Identify the ground by the conditions the chapter defines, then act as prescribed:
  - scattered ground: do not fight;
  - light ground: do not halt;
  - contested ground: do not attack;
  - open-crossing ground: do not get cut off;
  - crossroads ground: join allies;
  - heavy ground: plunder;
  - broken ground: keep moving;
  - enclosed ground: use stratagem;
  - dead ground: fight.

  Troops deep in hostile land with nowhere to go fight to the death without being ordered. The chapter says the variations of the nine grounds and the pattern of human feelings must be examined.
- **Their words:** 「是故散地則無戰，輕地則無止，爭地則無攻，交地則無絕，衢地則合交，重地則掠，圮地則行，圍地則謀，死地則戰。」 — "So on scattered ground, do not fight; on light ground, do not halt; on contested ground, do not attack; on crossing ground, do not be cut off; on crossroads ground, join in alliances; on heavy ground, plunder; on broken ground, keep going; on enclosed ground, scheme; on dead ground, fight." (Each ground is defined in l.138, e.g. 「疾戰則存，不疾戰則亡者，為死地」 — "ground where you survive if you fight fast and perish if you do not is dead ground.")
  「投之無所往，死且不北。死焉不得，士人盡力。兵士甚陷則不懼，無所往則固，深入則拘，不得已則鬥。」…「投之亡地然後存，陷之死地然後生。夫衆陷於害，然後能為勝敗。」…「九地之變，屈伸之利，人情之理，不可不察也。」 — "Throw them where there is nowhere to go and they will die rather than flee; facing death, what can they not achieve? Officers and men give their full strength. Soldiers deeply trapped do not fear; with nowhere to go they hold firm; deep in, they cling together; with no choice, they fight… Throw them into ground where they would perish, and they survive; sink them into dead ground, and they live. Only when the mass has fallen into harm can it decide victory or defeat… The variations of the nine grounds, the advantage of drawing in or extending, the pattern of human feelings: these must be examined." The same chapter gives a second list for each ground of what the commander himself does (「散地，吾將一其志…死地，吾將示之以不活」, l.148).
- **Source:** 九地第十一 (l.138, 142, 146, 148, 150).
- **Words are:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-12 #10 (corrected)

### 20. Which house falls first: judge by the land unit, the tax, and what they breed

- **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** The King of Wu asked which of the six houses dividing Jin would fall first. Sunzi named Fan and Zhonghang first, then Zhi, then Han and Wei, while Zhao, keeping its old laws, would hold and Jin would go to it. His reason, given house by house (Ames's translation), had two sides:
  - **The houses that fell:** small land units plus the tax gave full coffers and many officials, so the ruler grew arrogant and the ministers wasteful, and they fought often for exploits.
  - **Zhao:** the largest unit and no new taxes gave empty coffers and few officials, a frugal ruler and humble ministers over a prosperous people, so it held.
- **Their words:** 「吴王问孙子曰："六将军分守晋国之地，孰先亡、孰固成？"孙子曰："范、中行是(氏)先亡。""孰为之次？""智是(氏)为次。""孰为之次？""韩魏为次，赵毋失其故法，晋国归焉。"」 (simplified characters, as printed by 何炳棣) — "The King of Wu asked Master Sun: 'Of the six commanders who divided and held the land of Jin, which will perish first, and which will hold firm?' Master Sun said: 'Fan and Zhonghang will perish first.' 'Who next?' 'Zhi next.' 'Who next?' 'Han and Wei next. Zhao has not lost its old laws, and the state of Jin will go to it.'"
  The reasoning is in English only here (Ames): "Fan and Chung-hang took eighty square paces as a yuan, and took one hundred and sixty square paces as a chen, and then made five households their basic tax unit. The land area was small and the officials in office were many. With five households as the basic tax unit, the public coffers prospered. With the public coffers prospering and the officials in office many, the ruler became arrogant and his ministers wasteful. And in pursuit of great exploits they embarked on frequent wars. Thus I say they were the first to perish… Chao took one hundred and twenty square paces as a yuan, and took two hundred and forty square paces as a chen, and so there were no new taxes forthcoming for the public coffers. With the public coffers empty and the officials in office few, the ruler was frugal and his ministers humble in their management of what was a prosperous people. Thus I say that he held on to his lands."
  The closing line is **the king's, not Sunzi's**: 「王者之道明矣，厚爱其民者也」 — "The way of the true king is clear: it is to love his people generously."
- **Source:** 銀雀山漢簡〈吳問〉. Chinese as quoted in 何炳棣〈中國現存最古的私家著述《孫子兵法》〉 (https://www.aisixiang.com/data/101665.html). English from Ames 1993, Part II ch.1 and n.230. Ames reads 伍稅 as "five households as the tax unit"; 何炳棣, in his own words rather than the slips', describes Fan and Zhonghang as 「亩最小，税最重」 (smallest unit, heaviest tax).
- **Words are:** `attributed` (a narrated dialogue in the bamboo text)

- **What `quoted` means here.** It means the main text of the thirteen chapters, which speaks in his voice under 「孫子曰」. Whether the historical 孫武 wrote it is not established: 葉適's objection from the 左傳's silence; Warring-States dating; 何炳棣 argues for 512 BC (the 2016 aisixiang reprint). I cite commentators (十一家注) only to show where a gloss is disputed, never as his words.
- **Bamboo texts not opened in Chinese.** For the 〈吳問〉 reasoning, 〈四變〉 and 〈見吳王〉 I used Ames's English (*Sun-tzu: The Art of Warfare*, 1993, read from a PDF copy of the book, Part II, translating the 1985 銀雀山 edition). I did not open a Chinese transcription, so no Chinese is quoted for those parts.
  - ctext.org returned a Cloudflare challenge to every automated request.
  - Web searches (WebSearch, Chinese Wikisource and Chinese Wikipedia) for phrases of the 〈吳問〉 reasoning returned nothing. The only Chinese I found was 何炳棣's quotation of the opening and of the king's closing line.
  - 銀雀山漢墓竹簡〔壹〕 itself was not available.
- **The thirteen chapters were not collated against the bamboo text.** Ames's notes record these differences that touch this list:
  - 軍形 「守則不足，攻則有餘」 is reversed in the strips (n.133; not used here).
  - 軍形 「無智名，無勇功」: the strips add a clause Ames renders "never an exceptional victory" (n.134). I kept 勝於易勝 but left this line out of #2.
  - 虛實's water passage and 「無恒形…謂之神」 are worded differently in the strips (n.157–159; #10). The meaning is close, but I quote the received text.
  - 謀攻 「每戰必殆」 has the variant 「必敗」 (n.129; #4).
- **Readings left out of rules because the words are disputed:**
  - 「將聽吾計…留之…去之」 (#1)
  - 「修其功」 and 「費留」 (#5)
  - 「亂生於治，怯生於勇，弱生於強」 (#12)
  - 虛實's 「勝可擅也」 (l.85; Wikisource marks 為 as a variant for 擅), which sits against 軍形's 「勝可知，而不可為」 in #2. I quote 軍形 and do not resolve the tension.
- **答話 not used.** The 吳王問孫武 exchanges after 用間 are later citations: Ames places them in his Part III, "recovered from later works", from 杜佑's 通典, and the Wikisource text's own editors doubt parts of it. So 「爭地之法，讓之者得，爭之者失」 and 「困而不謀者窮，窮而不戰者亡」 are left out.
- **Left out for the cap, not for sourcing:**
  - 分數/形名 and 金鼓旌旗 (organising a mass "as a few"; 兵勢 l.62, 軍爭 l.98)
  - 迂直之計 (軍爭 l.92, 96)
  - 率然 coordination (九地 l.144)
  - 地形's six terrains (l.127)
  - the five fire attacks (火攻)
  - 「王徒好其言，不能用其實」 (史記: the king likes the words but cannot use their substance), which is a judgement, not a rule
