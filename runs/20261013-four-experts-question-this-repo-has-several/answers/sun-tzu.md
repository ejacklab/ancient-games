# 孫子 (Sun Tzu) — one official set of trust tags

## Answer

Keep the repo's existing four labels — `verified`, `reported`, `inferred`, `unknown` — as the single official set, write their definitions once in one new canon file, and have every template, table, template, script and draft agent point at it; drop `unproven`, `gap`, `confirmed`, `fact` and `disputed` as separate tags because each is one of the four seen from another angle. If EJ would rather not add a file, then the canon can live in `docs/workflow-templates/research-file.md` and everything else points there — but that file is a template, so a new `docs/EVIDENCE_LABELS.md` is the cleaner home.

The four, one job each (the tag says only *how the evidence stands to the finding*; whether the claim is true goes in the Finding text):

- **`verified`** — I read the primary document myself, and it states what the row says (including the narrow claim "the document says X").
- **`reported`** — the row carries a claim that some source states, without the primary being read; a secondary account, or a primary account of someone else's claim.
- **`inferred`** — no source states it; it follows from other rows, which the row must name, with the reason.
- **`unknown`** — what I looked for and did not find, or a claim for which no source was found; the row states what was searched.

Rule for when two tags seem to fit: **the tag follows the relation between the evidence and the finding, not the reliability of the source; if two still seem to fit, the finding is two findings — split it, do not pick or average.** Never upgrade a label: when new evidence arrives, add a row.

## How I decided

1. **Count the pieces before choosing.** (my #1: lay the question out, compare, and only then commit.) Five definitions exist (brief facts 1–5) and they split into two different measures: `verified`/`reported` grade *the source*, while `inferred` and `gap` grade *the claim*. The five-reviewer finding says exactly this (brief, "problems already raised"). One word cannot hold both measures; so the first thing to decide is which measure the tag names.
2. **Do not take the reckoning from measurement or omens.** (my #11: "Foreknowledge cannot be taken from ghosts and spirits, cannot be got by analogy with events, cannot be verified by measurement … it must be taken from people who know the enemy's situation.") So the canon's source is named and dated: the repo's own files (the brief's facts) plus the established schemes below, not my taste.
3. **What the established schemes do.** *This is my reading, from web sources, not from my list.* The Admiralty/NATO System "comprises a two-character notation assessing the reliability of the source and the assessed level of confidence on the information", and — the operative line for this question — "Each descriptor is considered in isolation to ensure that the reliability of the source does not influence the assessed accuracy of the report" ([Admiralty code, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_code)). Its two ratings are source reliability A–F and accuracy 1–6, rated separately. The repo's `verified`/`reported` is a source rating; its `inferred`/`gap`/`unproven` is an information rating. So the established answer to "which wins when a vendor blog is both `reported` and `unproven`" is: neither — they are answers to two questions, and the failure is asking one field to hold both.
4. **Refuse to add structure that was not shown to be needed.** (my reading; also my #3 "keep whole, break last": prefer the whole set to five broken ones.) I considered literally importing a second column or an A–F code set. The repo's actual usage (brief counts) is overwhelmingly one axis per row (`verified`/`reported`/`unknown`) and run 2 was accepted on that set. So the change is the *minimum* that resolves the stated defect: one single-valued tag, defined so that each tag answers one question, plus the split rule.
5. **Resolve the collision by the claim boundary, not by force.** (my #6, mix harm into benefit; my #17, learn the enemy by probing and by signs — test the rule on the case that was raised.) Vendor blog says "60% faster" with no harness. Two rows, each labeled by the evidence relation: row A, "the vendor's post states the system is 60% faster" — `verified` (I read the primary); row B, "the system is 60% faster" — `reported` (the source states it; the missing harness is said in the finding, not encoded in the tag). If a writer still cannot choose, the tag is not the problem — the row is double. So the rule is: one tag, source-relation; two tags → two rows.
6. **Put the rule in the ground, not in people.** (my #12: "The skilled fighter seeks victory in 勢 and does not demand it of people"; "Courage and cowardice are a matter of 勢.") Do not ask every writer to remember five half-copies. One canon file, and the one script that checks findings reads its list from that file, so the ground enforces it.
7. **Speak plainly enough to be followed.** (my #16: "If the rules are not clear and the orders not made familiar, it is the general's fault.") So the canon gives a one-line definition per tag, the split rule, and the mapping of the retired labels.
8. **Say whether `unproven` is lost.** (my #6 again — name the harm before committing.) `unproven` ("claimed without published evidence", brief fact 4) becomes `unknown`; `gap` ("looked for, not found", brief fact 3) also becomes `unknown`; `confirmed` becomes `verified`; `fact` becomes `verified`; `disputed` is not a tag at all — the house rule already gives both rows. Nothing in the accepted run-2 vocabulary disappears from the official set; it was already `verified/reported/unknown/unproven`.
9. **Keep the two neighboring tag sets separate.** (my #3 "prefer keeping things whole"; my reading.) Explorer's `read`/`ran` tag *code behaviour and method* (read vs. run), and the main agent's *quoted / my reading / not measured* tag *whose conclusion it is* — neither is a research-finding source relation. Merging them would re-create the same one-word-two-jobs defect.
10. **Where it lives and what changes.** (my #12 "choose people and rely on 勢" — a mechanism, not a reminder.) One new file `docs/EVIDENCE_LABELS.md` holds the four definitions, the collision rule, the no-upgrade rule, the mapping of retired labels, and the pointer list. Then, point at it: `docs/workflow-templates/research-file.md` (replace the inline definition); `docs/TASK_TYPES.md` §? `research and reports` check (replace `CONFIRMED/INFERRED/GAP/UNPROVEN`); `docs/FEEDBACK_TEMPLATES.md` rows 9 and 14 (replace `FACT/INFERRED/GAP/Disputed` and `CONFIRMED/INFERRED/GAP/UNPROVEN` with the four, keeping any `Disputed` line as a note, not a tag); `runs/20261011-agent-memory-graph/check-findings.py` (make its `LABELS` default read `docs/EVIDENCE_LABELS.md` instead of hard-coding the four); `.claude/agents/researcher.md` (draft, not adopted); and one retag pass over `docs/research/*/FINDINGS.md`, which is the only place the old words are actually read.

## Costs and risks

- **The canon is one more file to keep true** (my #3: prefer whole, but it says nothing about a free lunch). If it drifts from what writers do, it becomes a fifth half-copy. Price: the checker must read the tag list from the canon, or the canon is decorative.
- **Retagging old files is a real cost and cannot be hidden** (my #6: name the harm). `gap`→`unknown` and `unproven`→`unknown` will lose the distinction those words carried in the whale and agent-memory files; the Finding text keeps it, but a word-census will no longer show it. This is the accepted price of one field.
- **A tag that names the evidence relation does not by itself say the claim is true** (my #2: being unbeatable lies with me, and I "cannot make the enemy certain to be beatable" — the same limit applies to a label). `reported` will still sit next to a claim that is unbenchmarked; the reader must read the Finding text. My proposal moves that duty to the text deliberately, not by accident.
- **My external facts are my reading, not quoted from my list.** The Admiralty lines are quoted from the Wikipedia article, which itself relies on a single source ([Admiralty code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_code)); I did not read FM 2-22-3 or JWP 2-00. The `GRADE` comparison in the brief's wording I did not verify beyond search results, so I have not rested any step on it.
- **This is advice, not the decision** (house rule: EJ decides). The tag names, the home, and the retag scope are the parts that need EJ's word.
- **Merging Explorer's `read`/`ran` or the main agent's `quoted`/`my reading`/`not measured` into the finding set is refused on purpose**, to keep each tag from doing two jobs.

## How we would know I was right

- **The decider's test.** Give the same three findings — the vendor-blog claim, a claim inferred from two rows, and a fact never found — to two writers who have not seen this answer, with the canon at hand. If they agree on one tag each (Row A `verified`, Row B `reported`, the inference `inferred` with both rows named, the not-found `unknown` with the search), the canon is doing its job. If they split on the vendor case, the definition is still doing two jobs.
- **The file test, run against the repo's own checker.** `check-findings.py` (its `LABELS` read from `docs/EVIDENCE_LABELS.md`) passes every new findings file and **fails one where a row is seeded with `gap` or `unproven`** as a tag. That failure is the observable that the single set is enforced. Stated plainly: this check is one I am proposing and would help write, so passing it is not independent evidence that the canon is *right* — only that it is applied.
- **The migration census.** After the retag pass, a grep over `docs/research/*/FINDINGS.md` finds no row labeled `gap`, `unproven`, `confirmed`, `fact` or `disputed`; the same grep over the next three research runs stays clean. That is a count, not a judgement, and I would report it as such.
- **The honest failure condition.** If in two months a fifth tag has been added in a sixth place, my #12 was wrong — the fix was put in people and not in the ground — and the next move is to make the checker read the canon before adding any more writers.
