# lao-tzu — review of design.md
Question: What can we remove from this design?
## Findings
1. Line 36 vs line 34 — "a small task may need only Linus", but the order starts with 范蠡, so every task pays for a 范蠡 pass. Remove 范蠡 as the sizer: default to Linus alone, and add seats on a stated trigger. — principle: "為道日損" (ch.48) — label: my reading
2. Lines 7–8, 17–18 — the quoted problem is skipped steps ("logic is 1+1 = 2"). Linus answers that, and 范蠡's "what is the goal" answers line 4. No quoted problem line asks for 孫子's or 老子's question, so these two are the first candidates to remove. — principle: "為學日益，為道日損" (ch.48) — label: my reading
3. Line 7 — EJ began with "3 person". The fourth seat came from "or maybe we can have 4". — principle: "為道日損" (ch.48) — label: quoted (line 7)
4. Lines 40–41 — the sentence keeping 孫子 ("How would this fail?") apart from Linus ("does it add up?") is there because the two overlap. They may be one seat. — principle: "有無相生，難易相成" (ch.2) — label: my reading
5. Whole design — it has no "no seats" form. In the opposite design, the worker writes out its facts, its common sense and the objective it chose (lines 17–21). Without that baseline, nobody can tell what the seats add. — principle: "有無相生" (ch.2) — label: not measured
6. Line 34 — four passes in a row change one plan four times. 老子 may remove what 孫子 just added. — principle: "治大國若烹小鮮" (ch.60) — label: my reading
7. Lines 29, 36 — no seat has a point where its job is done and it stops. — principle: "功遂身退，天之道" (ch.9) — label: my reading

## Verdict
The design holds up only in part. Linus and the goal question tie to EJ's quoted problem. 孫子, 老子, and 范蠡 as the gate on every task have not yet shown what they add, and a baseline with no seats would show it.
