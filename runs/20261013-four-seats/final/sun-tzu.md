## Final list

1. **Kind:** algorithm
- **Rule:** Assess warfare through five fundamental factors and compare them across seven calculations to foresee victory or defeat. Retain the commander who heeds the calculations and dismiss the one who does not.
- **Their words:** 「故經之以五事，校之以七計，而索其情：一曰道，二曰天，三曰地，四曰將，五曰法。…故校之以七計而索其情，曰：主孰有道？將孰有能？天地孰得？法令孰行？兵眾孰強？士卒孰練？賞罰孰明？吾以此知勝負矣。將聽吾計，用之必勝，留之；將不聽吾計，用之必敗，去之。…多算勝，少算不勝，而況於無算乎？」 ("Regulate it by five factors, compare by seven calculations, and seek their reality: first, the Way; second, Heaven; third, Earth; fourth, the General; fifth, Law... Compare them by seven calculations to seek their reality: Which ruler has the Way? Which general has ability? Who gains Heaven and Earth? Whose laws and orders are executed? Whose forces are stronger? Whose soldiers are trained? Whose rewards and punishments are clear? By these I know victory and defeat. If a general heeds my calculations, employ him and he will surely win—retain him; if he does not heed my calculations, employ him and he will surely lose—dismiss him... Many calculations win, few calculations lose, how much more so none!")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·始計第一》

2. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Preserving an enemy's state, army, or unit intact is superior to destroying it. The highest form of military action strikes the enemy's plans, the next strikes alliances, the next strikes troops, and assaulting fortified cities is the lowest resort.
- **Their words:** 「凡用兵之法，全國為上，破國次之；全軍為上，破軍次之；全旅為上，破旅次之；全卒為上，破卒次之；全伍為上，破伍次之。是故百戰百勝，非善之善者也；不戰而屈人之兵，善之善者也。故上兵伐謀，其次伐交，其次伐兵，其下攻城。攻城之法，為不得已。…故善用兵者，屈人之兵而非戰也，拔人之城而非攻也，毀人之國而非久也，必以全爭於天下，故兵不頓而利可全，此謀攻之法也。」 ("In the method of using troops, keeping the state intact is best, breaking the state is next; keeping the army intact is best, breaking the army is next; keeping the brigade intact is best, breaking the brigade is next; keeping the company intact is best, breaking the company is next; keeping the squad intact is best, breaking the squad is next. Hence winning a hundred victories in a hundred battles is not the best of the best; subduing the enemy's troops without battle is the best of the best. Therefore the highest army attacks plans; next attacks alliances; next attacks troops; lowest assaults cities. Besieging cities is done only when unavoidable... The skilled user of troops subdues the enemy's troops without battle, takes cities without assaulting them, and overthrows states without prolonged operations, contending for all under Heaven with wholeness intact.")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·謀攻第三》

3. **Kind:** mindset
- **Rule:** Make yourself invincible first, then wait for the enemy to become vulnerable. Invincibility resides within oneself, whereas vulnerability resides with the enemy; victory can be recognized, but cannot be forced into existence.
- **Their words:** 「昔之善戰者，先為不可勝，以待敵之可勝。不可勝在己，可勝在敵。故善戰者，能為不可勝，不能使敵之必可勝。故曰：勝可知，而不可為。…古之所謂善戰者，勝於易勝者也。故善戰者之勝也，無智名，無勇功。故其戰勝不忒。不忒者，其所措必勝，勝已敗者也。故善戰者，立於不敗之地，而不失敵之敗也。是故勝兵先勝而後求戰，敗兵先戰而後求勝。」 ("Ancient skilled warriors first made themselves invincible, and waited for the enemy to become vulnerable. Invincibility lies with oneself; vulnerability lies with the enemy. Therefore the skilled warrior can make himself invincible, but cannot ensure that the enemy will necessarily be vulnerable. Hence it is said: victory can be known, but cannot be manufactured... Those ancient skilled warriors conquered those who were easy to conquer. Thus their victories brought neither reputation for wisdom nor merit for courage. Their victories were without deviation because their measures were destined to succeed, conquering an enemy already defeated. Thus the victorious army wins first and then seeks battle; the defeated army battles first and then seeks victory.")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·軍形第四》

4. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Victory can be known by five criteria: knowing when to fight and when not to fight, understanding how to use large and small forces, unity of purpose across ranks, meeting the unprepared with preparation, and having a capable general unhindered by the ruler. When the principles of war dictate certain victory, engage even if the ruler commands otherwise; when they dictate defeat, refrain from battle even if the ruler commands to fight.
- **Their words:** 「故知勝有五：知可以戰與不可以戰者勝，識衆寡之用者勝，上下同欲者勝，以虞待不虞者勝，將能而君不御者勝。此五者，知勝之道也。故曰：知彼知己，百戰不殆；不知彼而知己，一勝一負；不知彼不知己，每戰必殆。」…「故戰道必勝，主曰無戰，必戰可也；戰道不勝，主曰必戰，無戰可也。是故進不求名，退不避罪，唯民是保，而利合於主，國之寶也。」 ("There are five ways to know victory: one who knows when he can fight and when he cannot wins; one who understands how to use large and small forces wins; one whose superiors and inferiors share the same desire wins; one who awaits the unprepared with preparation wins; one whose general is capable and not interfered with by the ruler wins. These five are the way to know victory. Hence it is said: know the other and know yourself, and in a hundred battles you will not be endangered; not knowing the other but knowing yourself, one victory and one defeat; knowing neither the other nor yourself, in every battle you will be imperiled... When the way of warfare dictates certain victory, though the ruler says do not fight, you may fight; when the way of warfare dictates defeat, though the ruler says fight, you may refrain from fighting. Thus advancing without seeking fame and retreating without avoiding blame, solely protecting the people and matching the ruler's advantage, is the treasure of the state.")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·謀攻第三》 (five ways to know victory) & 《孫子兵法·地形第十》 (authority rule)

5. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Act only when it accords with advantage, and cease when it does not. Rulers must not launch armies out of anger, nor commanders fight out of resentment, for human emotions can turn to joy again, but a ruined state cannot be revived and the dead cannot be brought back to life.
- **Their words:** 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』。故曰：明主慮之，良將修之，非利不動，非得不用，非危不戰。主不可以怒而興師，將不可以慍而致戰。合於利而動，不合於利而止。怒可以復喜，慍可以復悅，亡國不可以復存，死者不可以復生。故明主慎之，良將警之，此安國全軍之道也。」 ("To win in battle and take objectives but fail to consolidate the achievements is inauspicious, termed 'wasteful lingering' [*feiliu*]. Therefore the enlightened ruler deliberates it and the good general cultivates it: move not unless there is advantage, act not unless there is gain, fight not unless there is danger. A ruler may not raise an army in anger; a general may not join battle in resentment. Move when it accords with advantage; halt when it does not. Anger can return to happiness, resentment can turn back to joy; but a destroyed state cannot be preserved again, and the dead cannot be brought back to life.")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·火攻第十二》 (reiterated in 《孫子兵法·九地第十一》)

6. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Foreknowledge cannot be obtained from ghosts and spirits, nor deduced from analogy with past events, nor verified by astronomical calculations; it must be obtained from people who know the enemy's situation. Employ the five kinds of spies—local, internal, converted, doomed, and surviving—with converted enemy spies serving as the foundation.

7. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Warfare is based on deception: when capable, appear incapable; when using forces, appear not to use them; when near, make it appear far; when far, appear near. 9. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** The skilled commander brings the enemy to the field and is not brought by them. To force an unwilling enemy to fight, strike what they must rescue; to avoid battle when unwilling, divert or frustrate where they are heading.

10. **Kind:** mindset
- **Rule:** Seek victory from the strategic momentum rather than demanding it of individuals, selecting men to fit the momentum. Regulating large numbers is identical to regulating few through organizational divisions; fighting large numbers is identical to fighting few through signals and banners.

16. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Gauge the enemy using four diagnostic probes: calculate to learn their plans of gain and loss, provoke them to learn their rhythms of action and stillness, shape them to discover their positions of life and death, and skirmish with them to measure where their strength is surplus or deficient. Verify enemy intent through observable signs rather than words, such as humble words accompanied by increased defense signaling an advance, or aggressive words accompanied by forward advances signaling a retreat.
- **Their words:** 「故策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處。」…「辭卑而益備者，進也；辭強而進驅者，退也；輕車先出，居其側者，陣也；無約而請和者，謀也；奔走而陳兵者，期也；半進半退者，誘也；杖而立者，饑也；汲而先飲者，渴也；見利而不進者，勞也；鳥集者，虛也；夜呼者，恐也；軍擾者，將不重也；旌旗動者，亂也。」 ("Probe them to know their plans of gain and loss; stir them to know the patterns of their movement and rest; reveal their disposition to know their grounds of life and death; test them to know where they have surplus and deficiency... Humble words accompanied by increased preparations mean they are advancing; forceful words and pressing forward in columns mean they are retreating; light chariots deploying first on the flanks indicate a battle array; suing for peace without a treaty indicates a plot; troops scurrying about deploying arms indicate a coordinated hour; half-advancing and half-withdrawing is a lure; leaning on staffs indicates hunger; water-drawers drinking first indicates thirst; seeing advantage but not advancing indicates exhaustion; birds flocking indicates an empty camp; night shouting indicates fear; disorder in camp indicates an unrespected commander; fluttering banners indicate confusion.")
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法·虛實第六》 (probes) & 《孫子兵法·行軍第九》 (signs)

17. **Kind:** decision rule
- **Rule:** Discern the terrain and apply the matching response: in scattered ground do not fight; in light ground do not halt; in contentious ground do not attack; in open ground do not cut off routes; in intersecting ground join with allies; in heavy ground forage; in difficult ground advance; in encircled ground plan; in deadly ground fight. When troops are cast into deadly ground where there is no escape, they will fight desperately and survive.
- **Their words:** 「是故散地則無戰，輕地則無止，爭地則無攻，交地則無絕，衢地則合交，重地則掠，圮地則行，圍地則謀，死地則戰。…投之亡地然後存，陷之死地然後生。夫衆陷於害，然後能為勝敗。」 ("Therefore, in dispersive ground do not fight; in shallow ground do not halt; in contentious ground do not attack; in communicative ground do not sever connections; in intersecting highways join alliances; in deep ground plunder resources; in treacherous ground march on; in encircled ground devise schemes; in deadly ground fight... Cast them into fatal ground and they will survive; plunge them into deadly ground and they will live. Only when the multitude is trapped in peril can they bring about victory.")

## Gaps
- **〈吳問〉 and bamboo slip variants:** The excavated Yinqueshan (銀雀山) bamboo slips include lost chapters such as 〈吳問〉 where Sun Tzu converses with King Helü. However, authoritative line-by-line collated readings could not be directly verified against primary tomb slips in this environment without risking unverified conjecture. Consequently, no items were manufactured from bamboo-only fragments.
- **The 《答話》 (Questions and Answers) appendix:** Found in the Song edition received text of 《孫子兵法》 following Chapter 13. As noted by Song editors, these dialogues contain external attributions and potential interpolations (e.g. from 《孫子兵法論》 or later commentators). To preserve strict quote fidelity, they have been excluded from the core list of Sun Tzu's own mindsets and rules.
- **Biographical anecdotes in 《史記》:** Sima Qian's famous biography (drilling the palace concubines) reflects narrative historiography written several centuries later rather than Sun Tzu's direct strategic and tactical rules, and was therefore omitted from the decision rules.
- **Excluded overly modern interpretations:** Paraphrases claiming that "character is purely an output", "deception creates an uncopyable signature", or "siege ladder steps must be sequentially attempted" were excluded because the primary text does not state those modern systematic guarantees.
