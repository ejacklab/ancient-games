# 孫武 / Sun Tzu — top 12, reviewed

I re-fetched the full received text (`zh.wikisource.org/wiki/孫子兵法`, raw wikitext, all 13 chapters + 答話 + the editorial notes) and verified every clause below against it directly, rather than relying on the research's quotations.

---

## Review of the top 10

**1. Count before you commit — change.** This is the book's own opening gate and it is correctly identified, but it is two things merged: a calculation procedure (五事 + 七計) and an epistemology of where knowledge may come from (用間's 「先知者…必取於人」). Split them. The five factors and seven comparisons are an ordered procedure and should be labelled *algorithm*; the 用間 clause is a separate sourcing rule and is better kept as a short corollary, since 用間's real substance — five kinds of spy, funded, staffed, and protected — went unused. Ranking at #1 is right; this is chapter one and everything else is downstream. Strongest available quote is already the one used.

**2. Subdue without battle; the four-tier escalation — keep, but widen.** Correctly identified, correctly ranked in the first ten, and the 攻城 cost-pricing (「三月而後成」, 「殺士三分之一，而城不拔者，此攻之災也」) is the right supporting evidence. But the rule as written starts at 「是故百戰百勝」, which skips 謀攻's *first* sentence — 「全國為上，破國次之；全軍為上，破軍次之…」 — the preservation ladder that ranks intact over broken at five levels of scale. That is the underlying principle, not an illustration of it. Dropped from #2 to #4 only because the two gates above it (can I fight? can I be beaten?) fire earlier in any real decision.

**3. Make yourself unbeatable first — keep, high.** Distinct, precisely stated, and the quote is the strongest Sun Tzu has for it. One defect to fix: the gloss reads "unb defeatable" / "defeatable-able" seven times. It should read *unbeatable* / *beatable*. That typo will propagate straight into any artifact built from this. Merge in 九變's 「無恃其不來，恃吾有以待之」 — that is the same orientation stated again, and it costs nothing.

**4. Prefer quick over thorough — change (this is the weakest rule in the ten).** As stated it is contradicted by two other chapters.軍爭第七 is entirely about the price of *march speed* (「百里而爭利，則擒三軍將」), and 九地 says flatly 「故兵之情主速，乘人之不及」. So the received text does not say "be quick." It says: duration is a cost you must price and cap (「故兵貴勝，不貴久」), and speed of manoeuvre is mandatory and carries its own casualty rate. What the chapter is really for is the **cost ledger** — 「日費千金，然後十萬之師舉矣」, 「百姓之費，十去其七」 — i.e. price the campaign in money and months *before* starting. That belongs inside rule 1 as the calculation's third arm, not as a standalone tempo rule.

**5. Change the conditions, not the people — keep.** 「求之於勢，不責於人」 is the most portable single rule in the book for anyone managing anyone. Two fixes: the clause 「形之，敵必從之；予之，敵必取之。以利動之，以卒待之」 is *Sun Tzu manipulating the enemy*, and is misfiled here — it belongs with the deception rule. And the strongest supporting quote is missing: 「亂生於治，怯生於勇，弱生於強。治亂，數也；勇怯，勢也；強弱，形也。」 — courage, cowardice, strength and weakness are *outputs of position*, which is the rule's actual claim, stated as arithmetic.

**6. Make them come to you — change.** Two rules merged: (a) own the tempo (「致人而不致於人」, 「我欲戰…攻其所必救也」), and (b) be invisible (「形人而我無形」, 「微乎微乎，至於無形」). (b) is not distinct from top-10 #7 and largely duplicates it. Split: keep (a), move (b) into #7. Ranked #6 → #6, unchanged in place but half its size.

**7. Deceive deliberately — keep, high.** 「兵者，詭道也」 in chapter one, twelve named tells, and 「兵以詐立」 as method in 軍爭 — this is genuinely load-bearing and the quote is the strongest available. Upgrade it: three strong lines were left unused — 「形人而我無形，則我專而敵分」, 「人皆知我所以勝之形，而莫知吾所以制勝之形」 (win in a shape nobody can copy), and the tempo quartet 「其疾如風，其徐如林，侵掠如火，不動如山」.

**8. Test against harm as well as benefit — keep, but recast as two, and the second is the better one.** 「必雜於利害」 is a general principle that overlaps rule 1. The 五危 list is the sharpest thing in the chapter and it was relegated to a supporting note: *every virtue is a lever against you* — 「必死，可殺也；必生，可虜也；忿速，可侮也；廉潔，可辱也；愛民，可煩也」. Promote it to the centre of the item. Ranked #8 → #12, but for the opposite reason: it is narrow, one chapter, one check — not because it is weak.

**9. Never rely on their restraint — keep, but merge.** 「無恃其不來，恃吾有以待之」 is the same rule as 軍形's 「不可勝在己，可勝在敵」 and belongs beside it. Standalone it is thin. Also: **the quote as printed is wrong by one character.** The received text reads 「恃吾有以待**之**」, not 「以待也」 — the quote-check caught this and the research did not correct it. Fix before use.

**10. Don't let anger start a war — change, and promote.** Correctly identified, and the placement note is a genuine catch that I confirmed: 「主不可以怒而興師」 closes **火攻第十二** in the received text, not 謀攻 where it is usually cited. But the rule is too narrow as framed. Two unused pieces make it a top-eight item: (a) 「合於利而動，不合於利而止」 appears **twice** — 火攻第十二 *and* 九地第十一 — which makes it not a line in one chapter but his standing go/no-go gate; (b) 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』」 — victory won and then not carried through is *worse* than a plan that failed. The research noticed 費留 and did not rank it.

## What the top 10 missed

**The knowledge-to-authority procedure (謀攻第三 + 地形第十).** 「知彼知己，百戰不殆；不知彼而知己，一勝一負；不知彼不知己，每戰必殆」 is the most quoted line in the book and it is absent from all ten. More useful is what surrounds it, because it is a graded procedure: five preconditions for a fight worth having (「知可以戰與不可以戰者勝，識衆寡之用者勝，上下同欲者勝，以虞待不虞者勝，將能而君不御者勝」), then a partial-victory scale — knowing your troops can be struck but not that the enemy cannot is *half* a victory, three times over (地形第十) — and then the authority clause the top10 never noticed: 「故戰道必勝，主曰無戰，必戰可也；戰道不勝，主曰必戰，無戰可也。」 The assessment overrules the stated wish, and you are protected for it. Rule 1 counts; this decides and then licenses.

**A systematic situation→response grammar, in five places.** The top 10 has no lookup-table item at all, which is strange, because this is the most mechanical content in the book: 九變's five postures (「圮地無舍，衢地合交，絕地無留，圍地則謀，死地則戰」); 九地's nine postures each with its one prescribed response (「散地則無戰，輕地則無止，爭地則無攻…死地則戰」) *and* a second, different answer to the same posture when you are the guest rather than the defender; 軍爭's four ordered pairs (治氣 / 治心 / 治力 / 治變) plus nine explicit "don'ts" (「佯北勿從…圍師必闕，窮寇勿迫」); 行軍's tell-table; 地形's six terrains, six defeats and six named causes (「將不能料敵，以少合衆，以弱擊強，兵無選鋒，曰北」). It is the same grammar at five scales. Three of these become items below.

**The four probes (虛實第六).** 「策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處」 — an ordered procedure for reading an opponent, each probe aimed at a different question. Combined with 行軍's sign table (「辭卑而益備者，進也；辭強而進驅者，退也；半進半退者，誘也；杖而立者，飢也；鳥集者，虛也」) this is his observation algorithm, and it is the single most mechanically useful passage in the book for anyone building a decision procedure.

**Courage is a variable of the hour (軍爭第七).** 「三軍可奪氣，將軍可奪心。是故朝氣銳，晝氣惰，暮氣歸。」 — morale is not a property of men, it is a function of time since the last engagement. This is the sharpest possible support for rule 5's real claim, and it belongs there.

**Benevolence without firmness produces a spoiled child (地形第十).** 「視卒如嬰兒，故可以與之赴深溪；視卒如愛子，故可與之俱死。厚而不能使，愛而不能令，亂而不能治，譬若驕子，不可用也。」 Care is not sufficient; care without the ability to command yields someone useless. Genuinely his own thought and in no list.

**Near misses, for the record.** 「凡治衆如治寡，分數是也；鬥衆如鬥寡，形名是也」 plus 「令之以文，齊之以武」 (軍爭, 行軍) — build the control system so it works without heroism, distinct from 任勢. 用間's five kinds of spy, 「非仁義不能用間」, and 「間事未發而先聞者，間與所告者皆死」 — intelligence as a funded, staffed, *protected* system. 九地's 「將軍之事，靜以幽，正以治。能愚士卒之耳目，使之無知…帥與之期，如登高而去其梯」 and 「犯之以事，勿告以言」 — commit by deed, not by telling. 「凡戰者，以正合，以奇勝」 — hold the front straight, win with the irregular. 謀攻's 「君之所以患於軍者三」 — the three ways a superior ruins an army from above. 「故善用兵者，譬如率然」 — coordination without micromanagement.

## Top 12

**1. Count before you commit — and if the count fails, go.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Three arms, then a personnel decision. (1) Name the five factors — 道 / 天 / 地 / 將 / 法. (2) Run the seven comparisons — whose way, whose general, whose terrain, whose law, whose numbers, whose troops, whose rewards. (3) Price it: chariots, horses, the 100,000 levy, a thousand *li* of supply, ten thousand gold a day — and cap the duration. If the general will listen, keep him; if not, he will lose and you leave. Three chapters in, Sunzi says the *tempo* rule is the output of this gate, not a separate preference: value victory, not duration.
- **Their words:** 「兵者，國之大事，死生之地，存亡之道，不可不察也。故經之以五事，校之以七計，而索其情：一曰道，二曰天，三曰地，四曰將，五曰法。」…「故校之以七計而索其情，曰：主孰有道？將孰有能？天地孰得？法令孰行？兵眾孰強？士卒孰練？賞罰孰明？吾以此知勝負矣。」…「將聽吾計，用之必勝，留之；將不聽吾計，用之必敗，去之。」…「多算勝，少算不勝，而況於無算乎？」 — "War is a great matter of state… it must not go unexamined. Examine it by five things and compare it by seven calculations… Whose way is right? Whose general is capable? Who has heaven and earth? Whose laws are carried out? Whose numbers are stronger? Whose troops are trained? Whose rewards and punishments are clear? By this I know victory and defeat. If the general will listen to my plan, he will surely win — keep him; if he will not, he will surely lose — leave him. More calculation wins, less calculation loses, how much more so *none*."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》始計第一 (五事, 七計, 留去, 多算); cost ledger and 「故兵貴勝，不貴久」 from 作戰第二.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #1 (changed) + top-10 #4 folded in

**2. Decide whether you can fight — and let the decision overrule the wish.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Five preconditions for a fight worth having: you know whether it can be fought, you can use what you have, ruler and soldiers want the same thing, you are ready and they are not, and your general can act without interference. Then grade your knowledge: knowing both sides is safe, knowing only yourself is a coin-flip, knowing neither is death — and knowing one side but not the other is *half* a victory, whichever side you know. Then act on the assessment regardless of what was ordered: if the way says you can win, you fight even if the ruler says do not; if it says you cannot, you do not fight even if he says you must.
- **Their words:** 「故知勝有五：知可以戰與不可以戰者勝，識衆寡之用者勝，上下同欲者勝，以虞待不虞者勝，將能而君不御者勝。」…「故曰：知彼知己，百戰不殆；不知彼而知己，一勝一負；不知彼不知己，每戰必殆。」…「知吾卒之可以擊，而不知敵之不可擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，而不知吾卒之不可以擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，知吾卒之可以擊，而不知地形之不可以戰，勝之半也。」…「故戰道必勝，主曰無戰，必戰可也；戰道不勝，主曰必戰，無戰可也。是故進不求名，退不避罪，唯民是保，而利合於主，國之寶也。」 — "There are five ways to know you will win: know whether it can be fought and cannot; know how to use your numbers; have ruler and people wanting the same; be prepared against the unprepared; and have a general the ruler does not interfere with… Know the enemy and know yourself and a hundred battles leave no danger; not knowing the enemy but knowing yourself, one win one loss; knowing neither, every battle is fatal… Knowing your troops can be struck but not that the enemy cannot be struck is half a victory… Therefore when the way of war says you can win, though the ruler says do not fight, fight — and when it says you cannot, though he says you must, do not. So advance seeking no glory, retreat declining no charge, protect only the people — and let the gain accord with the ruler's. This is a country's treasure."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》謀攻第三 (五知勝, 知彼知己); 地形第十 (three half-victories, 戰道必勝).
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** new

**3. Make yourself unbeatable; never build on their restraint.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** Split the question of victory in two. Being unbeatable lies with you, being beatable lies with them — so you work only on your half and wait for theirs. You can make yourself unbeatable; you cannot manufacture their error. Corollary: beat what is easy to beat, and let the victory leave no signature — no reputation for cleverness, no reputation for courage, nothing for the enemy to study.
- **Their words:** 「昔之善戰者，先為不可勝，以待敵之可勝。不可勝在己，可勝在敵。故善戰者，能為不可勝，不能使敵之必可勝。故曰：勝可知，而不可為。」…「古之所謂善戰者，勝於易勝者也。故善戰者之勝也，無智名，無勇功。故其戰勝不忒。」…「故無恃其不來，恃吾有以待之；無恃其不攻，恃吾有所不可攻也。」 — "The ancients' good commanders first made themselves unbeatable and waited for the enemy to become beatable. Being unbeatable lies with yourself; being beatable lies with the enemy. Hence the skilled commander can make himself unbeatable; he cannot make the enemy necessarily beatable. Victory can be known; it cannot be manufactured… Those the ancients called good commanders defeated those who were easy to defeat. So the skilled commander's victories bear no name for cunning and no credit for courage… Therefore do not rely on their not coming — rely on your having a preparation; do not rely on their not attacking — rely on your having what cannot be attacked."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》軍形第四 (先為不可勝, 勝於易勝者); the second quotation is 九變第八, not 軍形 — quote the received text as 「恃吾有以待**之**」.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #3 (kept) + top-10 #9 merged in

**4. Escalate down a cost ladder, and rank intact above broken.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** At every level — state, army, brigade, battalion, squad — keeping it whole beats breaking it, so rank options by damage before you rank them by difficulty. Then work down a ladder: attack plans, then alliances, then troops, and walls last. Siege is the resort of the desperate and he prices it: three months of equipment, three more to close the breach, a third of your soldiers dead and the city still standing.
- **Their words:** 「凡用兵之法，全國為上，破國次之；全軍為上，破軍次之；全旅為上，破旅次之；全卒為上，破卒次之；全伍為上，破伍次之。是故百戰百勝，非善之善者也；不戰而屈人之兵，善之善者也。故上兵伐謀，其次伐交，其次伐兵，其下攻城。攻城之法，為不得已。」…「故善用兵者，屈人之兵而非戰也，拔人之城而非攻也，毀人之國而非久也，必以全爭於天下，故兵不頓而利可全，此謀攻之法也。」 — "For preserving the whole state is best, breaking it next; preserving the whole army is best, breaking it next; and so down… A hundred battles won is not the best of the good; to subdue the enemy without battle is the best of the good. Hence the best army attacks plans; the next, alliances; the next, troops; the lowest, walls. Besieging a city is a last resort… The skilled wielder of troops subdues without battle, takes the city without assaulting it, and does not wear down the state — contending for all under heaven, so his troops never tire and his gain is kept whole. This is the method of 謀攻."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》謀攻第三, whole chapter.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #2 (kept, widened to open on 全國為上)

**5. Deceive deliberately, in twelve named ways; win in a shape nobody can copy.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** Not a trick but the foundation — if your real capability is legible, you lose it. Advertise the opposite of each capability, consistently enough to be acted on. Then let them see your form while yours stays invisible, and pick your tempo to suit the intent. Finally, the best victory is the one that leaves nothing to copy: everyone knows the shape in which you won; nobody knows the shape in which you won.
- **Their words:** 「兵者，詭道也。故能而示之不能，用而示之不用，近而示之遠，遠而示之近。利而誘之，亂而取之，實而備之，強而避之，怒而撓之，卑而驕之，佚而勞之，親而離之。攻其無備，出其不意。」…「故兵以詐立，以利動，以分合為變者也。故其疾如風，其徐如林，侵掠如火，不動如山。」…「故形人而我無形，則我專而敵分。」…「人皆知我所以勝之形，而莫知吾所以制勝之形。」 — "War is the way of deception. When able, appear unable; when using, appear not to be using; when near, appear far; when far, appear near. Lure them where advantage lies and take them in disorder; when solid, be ready; when strong, avoid; when angry, harry; when humble, make them overconfident; when rested, tire them; when close, divide them. Attack the unprepared, emerge from the unexpected… War stands on deception, is moved by advantage, varies by dividing and combining. So be swift as wind, quiet as forest, ravaging as fire, immovable as mountain… Let them show their form while yours stays formless, and you are concentrated while they are spread. Everyone knows the shape in which I won; none knows the shape in which I made it win."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》始計第一 (twelve tells); 軍爭第七 (詐立, four tempos); 虛實第六 (形人我無形, 制勝之形).
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #7 (kept, widened)

**6. Own the tempo: let the conditions decide whether and where the contest happens.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** Whoever arrives and waits has the advantage; whoever comes chasing has laboured for nothing. So decide before you engage whether *you* want the fight at all, and if you do, go where they cannot avoid coming — attack what they must rescue; if you do not, go where their orders forbid them to come. Move toward what they are not drawn to, away from what they expect.
- **Their words:** 「凡先處戰地而待敵者佚，後處戰地而趨戰者勞。故善戰者，致人而不致於人。」…「出其所不趨，趨其所不意。」…「故我欲戰，敵雖高壘深溝，不得不與我戰者，攻其所必救也；我不欲戰，雖畫地而守之，敵不得與我戰者，乖其所之也。」 — "Whoever is first in position and waits is at ease; whoever comes later and presses has laboured. Hence the skilled commander draws the enemy in and is not drawn in… Move by what they are not drawn toward; approach by what they do not expect… When I want to fight, the enemy, though behind high ramparts and deep moats, must fight me — I attack what they must rescue; when I do not want to fight, though I draw a line and hold, the enemy cannot fight me — I go where their orders carry them."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》虛實第六.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #6 (changed — the invisibility half moved to #5)

**7. Change the conditions, not the people.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** When output is short, do not push harder on the people — change the shape of the situation until the right behaviour is the easy behaviour. And accept that character is an output: order produces disorder and disorder produces panic, calm produces courage and courage produces weakness — because these are functions of count, of situation, and of formation. Build the command system so that large numbers behave like a few (division and rank), and bodies engage like single men (labels and signals).
- **Their words:** 「故善戰者，求之於勢，不責於人，故能擇人而任勢。任勢者，其戰人也，如轉木石。木石之性，安則靜，危則動，方則止，圓則行。」…「亂生於治，怯生於勇，弱生於強。治亂，數也；勇怯，勢也；強弱，形也。」…「凡治衆如治寡，分數是也；鬥衆如鬥寡，形名是也。」 — "The skilled commander seeks it in the situation and does not blame the men; being able to select men and put them in the situation. One who commands situation fights as one who rolls wood and stone: the nature of wood and stone is, at rest still, on a slope moving; square they stop, round they roll… Disorder is born of order, cowardice of courage, weakness of strength. Order and disorder are a matter of numbers; courage and cowardice of situation; strength and weakness of formation… To command a mass as a few: division and rank. To fight a mass as a few: labels and signals."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》兵勢第五, whole chapter.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #5 (kept;形之/予之 removed — that clause manipulates the enemy and belongs to #5)

**8. Move only on advantage; and finish it, or lose it.**
- **Kind:** decision rule
- **In plain English:** One gate, stated twice in the received text: move when it accords with advantage, stop when it does not. The corollary is about reversibility — anger can become joy again and resentment can become pleasure again, but a destroyed state cannot be restored and the dead cannot come back — so the burden of proof sits with whoever proposes to act. And the second half: a victory won and not carried through to completion is worse than a defeat, because you paid for it and hold nothing. He has a name for it — 費留, "wasted leave."
- **Their words:** 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』。故曰：明主慮之，良將修之，非利不動，非得不用，非危不戰。主不可以怒而興師，將不可以慍而致戰。合於利而動，不合於利而止。怒可以復喜，慍可以復悅，亡國不可以復存，死者不可以復生。」 — "To win battles and take cities and then not complete the work is bad; it is called *feiliu*, wasted leave. Therefore do not move without advantage, do not act without gain, do not fight without necessity. A ruler may not raise an army in anger; a general may not go to battle in resentment. Move when it accords with advantage; stop when it does not. Anger can become joy again; resentment can become pleasure again; a destroyed state cannot be restored; the dead cannot be brought back."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》火攻第十二 (closing passage — *not* 謀攻, where it is commonly cited; verified in this base text). 「合於利而動，不合於利而止」 also closes the relevant movement in 九地第十一, which is why I rank it as a standing gate rather than a chapter line.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #10 (changed — broadened to the advantage gate and 費留)

**9. Match their state, not their strength.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Four checks, in order: match their spirit with yours ordered, theirs disordered; match their agitation with yours calm; match their resources with yours better supplied; match their battle with yours one that is not a set-piece. Underneath all four lies the observation that morale is a clock — sharpest at dawn, slack by noon, spent by dusk. Then the nine refusals: do not attack up a slope, do not pursue a feigned retreat, do not attack fresh troops, do not block a returning army, do not close the gap of a surrounded enemy, do not press a cornered one.
- **Their words:** 「三軍可奪氣，將軍可奪心。是故朝氣銳，晝氣惰，暮氣歸。故善用兵者，避其銳氣，擊其惰歸，此治氣者也；以治待亂，以靜待嘩，此治心者也；以近待遠，以佚待勞，以飽待饑，此治力者也；無邀正正之旗，無擊堂堂之陣，此治變者也。」…「高陵勿向，背丘勿逆，佯北勿從，銳卒勿攻，餌兵勿食，歸師勿遏，圍師必闕，窮寇勿迫，此用兵之法也。」 — "The army's spirit can be taken, the general's heart can be taken. Morning spirit is sharp, midday spirit slack, evening spirit spent. Hence the skilled commander avoids their sharp spirit and strikes their spent return — this is managing spirit; waits order with disorder, stillness with uproar — this is managing the heart; meets near with far, ease with labour, fed with hunger — this is managing strength; does not intercept an army in array, does not strike a formation in order — this is managing change… Do not attack up a slope, do not meet one against a ridge, do not follow a feigned retreat, do not attack fresh troops, do not take bait, do not block a returning army, leave a gap to a surrounded enemy, do not press a cornered foe."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》軍爭第七.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** new

**10. Read which situation you are in, then do the one prescribed thing.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Nine postures, each with a fixed response and nothing else: do not fight / do not halt / do not attack / do not sever / join / forage / advance / plan / fight. Then run it again from the other side — the same posture as an invader has a different answer, which is the part almost everyone misses. Only after the identification comes the move he is famous for: when there is no way back, there is no longer anything to fear, and that is exactly when to advance.
- **Their words:** 「是故散地則無戰，輕地則無止，爭地則無攻，交地則無絕，衢地則合交，重地則掠，圮地則行，圍地則謀，死地則戰。」…「故兵之情：圍則禦，不得已則鬥，過則從。」…「投之亡地然後存，陷之死地然後生。夫衆陷於害，然後能為勝敗。」 — "Therefore: in scattered ground, do not fight; in light ground, do not halt; on contested ground, do not attack; in interlocked ground, do not sever; on thoroughfare ground, join with allies; in deep ground, forage; in broken ground, advance; in encirclement, plan; in dead ground, fight… The nature of troops: surrounded, they resist; with no choice, they fight; past it, they follow… Throw them where there is no going, and they survive; drop them into dead ground, and they live. A force trapped in harm is only then able to win or lose."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》九地第十一. Note the same chapter gives a *second* nine for the invader (「入深者，重地也」…) and then nine dispositions of the commander to match.
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** new

**11. Probe, then read the signs, before you judge anything.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Four probes, each aimed at a different question — probe to learn the balance of advantage, provoke to learn their tempo, shape to learn where the ground is killing them, and measure to learn where they are thin. Then a tell-table: infer intent from observable behaviour before you accept their account. Polite deference that is increasing preparation means they are advancing; aggressive speech with the columns pressing forward means they are withdrawing; a request for negotiation with no accord means a trap.
- **Their words:** 「故策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處。」…「敵近而靜者，恃其險也；遠而挑戰者，欲人之進也。」…「辭卑而益備者，進也；辭強而進驅者，退也；輕車先出，居其側者，陣也；無約而請和者，謀也；奔走而陳兵者，期也；半進半退者，誘也；杖而立者，飢也；汲而先飲者，渴也；見利而不進者，勞也；鳥集者，虛也；夜呼者，恐也；軍擾者，將不重也；旌旗動者，亂也。」 — "Therefore probe them and learn the balance of gain and loss; move them and learn the rhythm of their movement; shape them and learn where death lies; measure them and learn where surplus and shortage are… They are near and quiet: they rely on their ground. Far off and provoking: they want you to advance. Their words humble and their preparations increase: they are advancing. Their words strong and they press forward in formation: they are withdrawing. Light chariots come out first and take the flank: a battle-array. They ask for terms without accord: a plot. They run about and deploy: a date is set. Half advancing, half withdrawing: a lure. They stand on their staffs: they are hungry. They draw water and drink first: they are thirsty. They see advantage and do not advance: they are weary. Birds gather: it is empty. Night calls: they are afraid. The camp is in uproar: the general is not held in respect. The banners move without order: they are disordered."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》虛實第六 (four probes); 行軍第九 (sign table; the same chapter's terrain rules — 「絕山依谷，視生處高，戰隆無登」 — are the movement half of the same procedure).
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** new

**12. Interleave harm with benefit — and audit your own virtues as levers.**
- **Kind:** algorithm
- **In plain English:** Two passes before committing. First: mix the harm column into the benefit column as you think, so the plan is trusted for its upside and the trouble is already dissolved. Second: run yourself against five checks, each of which is a virtue that an opponent can use against you — the need to die, the need to live, anger and haste, incorruptibility, love of your people. Every one is a handle.
- **Their words:** 「是故智者之慮，必雜於利害，雜於利而務可信也，雜於害而患可解也。」…「故將有五危︰必死，可殺也﹔必生，可虜也 DISTRACTION erectile:忿速，可侮也SPDHH;:廉潔，可辱也SPDHH;:愛民，可煩也SPDHH:。凡此五者，將之過也，用兵之災也。覆軍殺將，必以五危，不可不察也。」 — "Therefore the wise person's deliberation must be mixed with benefit and harm: mixed in with benefit, the affair can be trusted; mixed in with harm, the affliction can be dissolved… The general has five dangers: the habit of seeking death can be used to kill him; the habit of seeking life can be used to capture him; anger and haste can be used to insult him; incorruptibility can be used to shame him; love of the people can be used to trouble him. These five are the general's faults, the disasters of using troops. Overrunning the army and killing the general comes of the five dangers, and one must not fail to examine them."
- **Source:** 《孫子兵法》九變第八 (both passages).
- **Label:** `quoted`
- **From:** top-10 #8 (changed — 利害 demoted to a pass, 五危 promoted to the centre)

## Gaps

- **〈吳問〉 — not sourced, and I should correct an expectation here.** The research pointed at 〈吳問〉 as the place to look for Sun Tzu's own voice in the oldest text. I checked: it is **not** in the Wikisource received text. The 答話 section that text does carry is entirely the 吳王問孫武 exchanges on the nine terrains (散地, 輕地, 爭地, 交地, 衢地, 重地, 圮地, 圍地), plus the 吳王/subjects dialogue about entering郢, and it ends with the Song editors' note 「何氏引此文，亦云『兵法曰』…」. 〈吳問〉 belongs to the 曹操 edition; its title is on the 銀雀山 篇題木牍 per 李學勤 as quoted by Wang Jiaxiang. I could not obtain its wording. **An 吳問 item would be `attributed` at best, and I did not verify a single clause of it, so I have not put one in the twelve.**
- **One base text only, as in the research.** Every quotation above is verified against zh.wikisource 孫子兵法 (received, Wikisource's 宋本-based text) — I read the raw wikitext of all thirteen chapters plus 答話. I did **not** collate against 曹操's edition, the 十一家本, or the 銀雀山 bamboo text. I therefore cannot certify that any of these twelve wording-wise in the oldest witness. That caveat is inherited and unchanged.
- **One variant inside my own item set.** The base text reads 「故曰：勝可<另>擅|為</別>也。敵雖衆，可使無鬥」 in 虛實第六, flagged in Wikisource as 勝可擅一作『為』也 — which sits awkwardly against 軍形's 「勝可知，而不可為」 that item 3 turns on.
