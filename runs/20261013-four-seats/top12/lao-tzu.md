# 老子 — top 12, reviewed

## Review of the top 10

Overall: the manuscript notes are the best part of this research and they are reliable — I re-checked every silk chapter number and every Guodian claim against 老子 (帛書校勘版) and the Murphy-derived Guodian table, and **all ten are correct**. Eight of ten rules cite the received text, which the research itself says is "demonstrably later and re-edited"; ranking should prefer what all three witnesses carry. Three rules are mis-framed (1, 3, 6), two are merged (2, 6), one is two rules (9), one is a contents list (10).

1. **Rule 1 (無為) — change.** The anchor is the book's most famous line, and it is the one passage where the manuscript disagrees: 帛書 81 (无名, = 王弼 37) reads 道恆无名，侯王若守之，萬物將自化 with no 無為. But the note's conclusion is wrong: "that formula is a received-text feature" is false of the silk, which has損之又損，以至於無為，**無為而無不為** verbatim at 帛書 48 (= 王弼 48). The formula is early; the *ch. 37 placement* is late. Re-anchor on ch. 48 and make it an ordered procedure.
2. **Rule 2 (timing, Shiji) — change.** Correct to label `attributed` and correct to call it the only recorded decision. But the rule as written merges three: timing (得其時則駕), concealed display (深藏若虛／盛德容貌若愚), and stripping the addressee's appetites (去子之驕氣與多欲). Keep only the timing, and note what the research skipped: 吾所以告子，若是而已 — the counsel itself is "this is all I have to tell you," which is the rule.
3. **Rule 3 (曲則全) — change.** Two faults. "Less-travelled" is nowhere in the text, and 少則得／多則惑 is about *quantity held*, not about choosing the smaller option — that is a famous-line misreading. And the plain English drops what the manuscript has: 帛書 67 (執一, = 王弼 22) runs straight on 不自視故彰，不自見故明，不自伐故有功，弗矜故能長，夫唯不爭，故莫能與之爭. The earliest witness makes this one passage. Merge with ch. 24.
4. **Rule 4 (上善若水) — change.** Keep the manuscript note verbatim — 帛書 52 reads 上善**治水**…水善利萬物而**有靜**, not 若水／不爭 — it is the sharpest single finding in the file. But change the rule: the text's decision content is the **eight 善** (居善地…動善時), a checklist the research dropped entirely, and the silk changes the third rung (予善**天**, not 與善**仁**). "Take the low useful position" is a fair gloss of one clause, not of the chapter.
5. **Rule 5 (功遂身退) — keep.** A real trigger ("the work is done") and an action ("leave"), attested at Guodian 甲 unit 5 and 帛書 53. Two refinements: the silk's 功**述**身**芮** is a good catch, but 遂 is not a lost emendation — the silk keeps 成功**遂**事 at 帛書 61 and 78; and the silk reads 成**功**成而弗居 where Wang Bi has 功**成**而弗居.
6. **Rule 6 (為之於未有) — change.** Two rules in one ("act before it breaks" and "hold the last step"), and it omits the chapter that states the first better: ch. 63's 圖難於其易，為大於其細 (Guodian 甲 unit 1) and 輕諾必寡信，多易必多難. Its own finding is right and should stay load-bearing: Guodian has ch. 64 only at beginning and end, so the two most-quoted images (千里之行, 起於累土) have no fourth-century-BCE attestation and the silk reads 百仞之高／羸土.
7. **Rule 7 (天下神器) — keep.** Right kind, right trigger, and the silk's sharper 非可為者也 earns the rank. Two additions: the silk's third prescription is 去甚，去**大**，去楮 (drop the grand scale), which is the text's own version of Rule 3; and ch. 60's 治大國若烹小鮮 — the operational form of the same rule, fully in the silk at 帛書 23 — is never used, although this project's own design review leans on it.
8. **Rule 8 (自知者明) — change.** Under-quoted: the silk keeps seven rungs (帛書 77盡己) and the research used two. The dropped rungs are the operative ones — 知足者富，強行者有志，不失其所者久. "Do not lose your ground" is a decision rule with a trigger and it is absent from the whole top 10.
9. **Rule 9 (柔弱勝剛) — change, into Rule 8's neighbour.** The silk joins this chapter to the reversal procedure: 帛書 80 (微明) = 將欲翕之必固張之…柔弱勝強，魚不可脫於淵. So standalone it is half a rule. Also drop "stand down and give way" as the whole gloss — the chapter's own sting is 天下莫不知莫能行: a principle everyone can recite and nobody applies is the one to verify before relying on it.
10. **Rule 10 (三寶) — drop.** As written it is a contents list, not a rule; the decision line "when you cannot keep all three" is invented to fit; and 不敢為天下先 duplicates ch. 8's 不爭 and the功遂身退 mood. The one durable part is 慈故能勇，夫慈以戰則勝，以守則固 — a claim about courage that the plain English never states. The research's variant work here is good and should not be lost: 帛書 32 writes 一曰**茲**、二曰**檢**, and 天將**建**之，如以茲垣之 against Wang Bi's 天將**救**之，以慈衛之.

## What the top 10 missed

- **ch. 13, the root cause of error:** 吾所以有大患者，為吾有身，及吾無身，吾有何患 (王弼 13; 帛書 57 寵辱; Guodian 乙 unit 1). Nothing in the top 10 ever names what makes this seat's own advice wrong. Now #2.
- **ch. 24, do not answer to your own display:** 自見者不明，自是者不彰，自伐者無功，自矜者不長 (王弼 24; 帛書 66 弗居, which has 自**視**者不彰 and 炊者不立, and 有**欲**者弗居). Now folded into #4, because the silk already joins it to ch. 22.
- **ch. 36, the reversal procedure:** 將欲弱之，必固強之；將欲廢之，必固興之；將欲奪之，必固與之 (王弼 36; 帛書 80 微明, which also carries 柔弱勝強 and gives a fourth pair — 將欲去之，必固與之). The only genuine *algorithm* of political timing in the book, and it has a manuscript for the shape. Now #8.
- **ch. 71, what the research cut and flagged:** 知不知上，不知知病 (王弼 71; 帛書 36 知病 reads 知不知 尚矣 **不知不知** 病矣). Now #9.
- **ch. 8's eight 善:** 居善地，心善淵，與善仁，言善信，正善治，事善能，動善時 — a checklist, and the silk's third rung is 予善**天**, not 與善**仁**. Now #11.
- **ch. 11, where the usefulness lives:** 三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用…故有之以為利，無之以為用 (王弼 11; 帛書 55 玄中; Guodian 甲 unit 5). The book never says what to *optimise for*; this does — the empty part is the part that works.
- **ch. 68, an actual conflict procedure:** 善為士者不武，善戰者不怒，善勝敵者弗與，善用人者為之下 (王弼 68; 帛書 33 不爭, where the last line is 是謂用人 against Wang Bi's 是謂用人之力). Four ordered checks, no famous line.
- **ch. 20, the one other triggerless attitude:** 眾人皆皆有以，我獨頑似鄙…吾欲獨異於人，而貴食母 (王弼 20; 帛書 64 食母; Guodian 乙 unit 1). This is the mindset the top 10 most obviously lacks; it did not make the 12 only because #6 was the better-attested one.
- **ch. 60,** 治大國若烹小鮮 (王弼 60; 帛書 23 居位) — never used.
- **ch. 12,** 為腹不為目 (王弼 12; 帛書 56; Guodian 甲 unit 4) — never used.
- **ch. 44,** 名與身孰親…知止不殆，可以長久 (王弼 44; 帛書 44 立戒; Guodian 甲 unit 5) — your standing and your stopping point, which rules #2 and #9 both require and the top 10 never supplies.
- **ch. 81 / 帛書 31,** 信言不美，美言不信；善者不辯，辯者不善 — the closing test for your own words; Guodian 丙 unit 3 has the end of ch. 31.
- **ch. 5 is not actually open.** The research listed 多聞數窮 (帛書 49 用中) vs 多言數窮 as an unresolved gap; Guodian 甲 unit 2 preserves part of ch. 5. Only the wording is contested, not the rule's existence.

## Top 12

1. **Kind:** algorithm. **In plain English:** Learning and the Way pull in opposite directions — for the Way, every act of doing is a loss. So: add to your knowledge; subtract from your doing; subtract again until there is no action left; and at that point nothing is left undone. The procedure is subtraction run to its end, not a stance of passivity. **Their words:** 為學日益，為道日損。損之又損，以至於無為。無為而無不為。取天下常以無事，及其有事，不足以取天下。 — "Learning grows daily; the Way loses daily. Lose and lose again, until there is no doing. No doing, and nothing is left undone. Taking the world is always by having no business; and when business appears, it is not enough to take the world." **Source:** 道德經 四十八章 (王弼本); 帛書校勘版 第四十八章 无为, which reads 為學者日益，聞道者日損 (聞道 not為道) and carries 無為而無不為; Guodian 乙 unit 1 preserves the beginning. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #1 (changed).
2. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** Find your own stake before you trust your own advice, because the stake is the defect — not the situation, and not the other person. **Their words:** 吾所以有大患者，為吾有身，及吾無身，吾有何患？ — "My great troubles come from having a body of my own; once I have no self of my own, what trouble have I?" **Source:** 道德經 十三章 (王弼本); 帛書 第五十七章 寵辱 (為吾有身也…及吾无身有何患), whose closing swaps the two allegories — 貴為身於為天下…愛以身為天下 — against Wang Bi's 貴以身為天下…愛以身為天下; Guodian 乙 unit 1. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** new.
3. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** Never take hold of the whole system. Aim at a part, leave the outcome unheld, and cut the three excess magnitudes — the severe, the grand, the extravagant. **Their words:** 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已。天下神器，不可為也，為者敗之，執者失之。是以聖人去甚，去奢，去泰。 — "Whoever wants to seize the world and work on it, I see him fail to get it. The world is a sacred vessel; it is not to be worked on. To work on it ruins it; to grip it loses it. So the sage drops the severe, the extravagant, the excessive." **Source:** 道德經 二十九章 (王弼本); 帛書 第七十三章 自然, with the sharper 天下神器也，**非可為者也** and its own third prescription 去甚，去**大**，去楮. Absent from Guodian. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #7 (kept).
4. **Kind:** algorithm. **In plain English:** Stay in the bent, worn, uncredited position — then take your own display away, in this order: don't look at yourself, don't show yourself, don't claim credit for what you did, don't stand on your achievement. The gain and the restraint are one procedure. **Their words:** 曲則全，枉則正，洼則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑。是以聖人執一以為天下牧，不自視故彰，不自見故明，不自伐故有功，弗矜故能長，夫唯不爭，故莫能與之爭。 — "Bent, it is whole; strained, it is upright; hollow, it is full; worn, it is new. Less, it is gained; more, it is confused. So the sage holds to the one and shepherds the world: not looking at himself, he is manifest; not showing himself, he is clear; not claiming his work, he has merit; not standing on it, he endures. Because he does not contend, none can contend with him." **Source:** 王弼本二十二章 + 二十四章; 帛書 第六十七章 執一 (= 王弼 22 + 24, with 枉則**正** and 為天下**牧**; full ch. 24 at 帛書 66: 炊者不立…自**視**者不彰…有**欲**者弗居). Neither chapter is in Guodian. **Label:** `quoted`; the joining of the two is the manuscript's, not mine. **From:** top-10 #3 (changed).
5. **Kind:** algorithm. **In plain English:** The two cheap moments are the unformed beginning and the nearly-finished end; the expensive moment is the middle. Act before disorder starts, plan the hard thing while it is still easy, do the great thing while it is still small — and do not relax one step before the last. **Their words:** 圖難於其易，為大於其細…夫輕諾必寡信，多易必多難。是以聖人猶難之，故終無難矣。…為之於未有，治之於未亂。…民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。慎終如始，則無敗事。 — "Take the difficulty at its easy; do the great at its small… Promise lightly and credit is short; take things lightly and difficulty is plentiful. So the sage still treats them as difficult, and so he has no difficulty at the end… Do the thing before it exists; govern it before it is out of order… People in the middle of a task most often fail as it nears completion. Be as careful at the end as at the start, and nothing fails." **Source:** 王弼本 六十三章 + 六十四章; 帛書 第二十六章 无難 + 第二十七章 輔物 (圖難乎亓易也…為之於亓未有也…恆於亓成而敗之，故慎終若始); Guodian 甲 unit 1 has ch. 63 at beginning and end and ch. 64 only at beginning and end, so the famous middle (千里之行, 起於累土) is unattested — the silk reads 百仞之高 /羸土. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #6 (changed).
6. **Kind:** mindset. **In plain English:** Standing attitude — on the right work, the result looks wrong from inside it. The Way-lit looks dim; progress looks like retreat; level ground looks rough; the highest good looks like a valley; the clearest white looks like disgrace; the great vessel is late-made; the greatest tone is faint; the greatest form has no form. Judge the direction by what it produces, never by how it looks while you are in it. **Their words:** 明道若昧，進道若退，夷道若纇，上德若谷，大白若辱，廣德若不足，質真如渝，大方無隅，大器晚成，大音希聲，大象無形。 — "Where the Way is lit it looks dim; where progress goes it looks like retreat; where the Way is level it looks uneven; the highest good looks like a valley; plain white looks like disgrace; broad good looks like want; plain truth looks like adulteration. Great square has no corner; the great vessel is late-completed; great tone is faint sound; great form has no shape." **Source:** 王弼本 四十一章; 帛書 第三章 闻道, which reads 明道如**費**, 上德如**浴**, 大器**免**成, **天**象无形, 道**殷**无名 — 免/晚 and 殷/隱 are the silk's standing loan substitutions, so 大器晚成 is a restored word; 費 and 昧 are not homophones, so that image is not secured. Guodian 乙 unit 2 preserves ch. 41. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** new.
7. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** When the work is done and running without you, stop attending to it and take no standing credit — and precisely because you don't dwell, the result doesn't leave. **Their words:** 功遂身退，天之道。…萬物作而不辭，生而不有，為而不恃，功成而弗居。夫唯弗居，是以不去。 — "When the work is accomplished, withdraw — that is heaven's Way… All things arise and it does not refuse them; beget them and does not own them; do them and does not rely on them; accomplish and does not dwell on them. Precisely because it does not dwell, it does not depart." **Source:** 王弼本 九章 + 二章; 帛書 第五十三章 持盈 reads 功**述**身**芮** (芮 for 退, cf. 帛書 51 芮亓身而身先 against 王弼 7 退其身), and 帛書 46 觀徼 reads 成**功**而弗居. 遂 survives in the silk at 成功遂事 (帛書 61, 78), so the received wording is an emendation, not a invention. Guodian 甲 unit 5 has ch. 9. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #5 (kept).
8. **Kind:** algorithm. **In plain English:** To contract something, widen it first; to weaken it, strengthen it first; to retire it, promote it first; to take it, give it first. Then stop and let the thing move against you. **Their words:** 將欲翕之，必固張之；將欲弱之，必固強之；將欲去之，必固與之；將欲奪之，必固予之。是謂微明。 — "To close it up, you must first spread it open; to weaken it, first make it strong; to take it away, first give it away; to take it, first bestow it. This is called subtle clarity." **Source:** 王弼本三十六章 (with 將欲廢之，必固興之); 帛書 第八十章 微明, which adds a fourth pair and then runs straight into 柔弱勝強，魚不可脫於淵，邦利器不可以示人 — the silk has this procedure and the weak-over-strong claim in one chapter. 陸德明《經典釋文》 on the opening word: 簡文作歙，又作洽。河上本作㬛也 (I do not know what he means by 簡文). Absent from Guodian, whose 70–81 may not yet exist. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** new.
9. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** Admitting you don't know is the higher performance; performing knowledge you don't have is the illness. And the cure is the same move as the disease: treat the illness as illness and you are not ill. **Their words:** 知不知上，不知知病。夫唯病病，是以不病。聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病。 — "Knowing that one does not know is the higher; not knowing that one knows is the illness. Only by treating the illness as illness is one not ill. The sage is not ill, because he treats the illness as illness, and so is not ill." **Source:** 王弼本 七十一章; 帛書 第三十六章 知病 reads 知不知 尚矣 **不知不知** 病矣 — praise attaches to the first clause, blame to a *doubled* negation, so the two witnesses differ in sense, not wording. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** new (the research flagged and cut this in its Gaps).
10. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** The ladder runs from judging others to judging yourself to knowing when enough is enough to not losing your ground. Self-measurement is the harder reading, and it is the one that lasts. **Their words:** 知人者智，自知者明。勝人者有力，自勝者強。知足者富。強行者有志。不失其所者久。 — "To know others is knowledge; to know oneself is clarity. To overcome others is strength; to overcome oneself is real strength. To know enough is richness. To force one's way is to have a purpose. Not to lose one's ground is to last." **Source:** 王弼本 三十三章; 帛書 第七十七章 尽己, which keeps all seven rungs and reads 知人者**知**也 (知 not智), 不失亓所者久也, 死**不忘**者壽也. Absent from Guodian. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #8 (changed).
11. **Kind:** algorithm. **In plain English:** Go where the crowd finds distasteful, benefit everything, and do not contend — then run the eight checks in order: choose your ground, keep your depth, treat people well, keep your word, keep things in order, do the job well, and move at the right time. **Their words:** 上善若水。水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。居善地，心善淵，與善仁，言善信，正善治，事善能，動善時。夫唯不爭，故無尤。 — "The highest good is like water. Water benefits all things and does not contend; it dwells where the crowd finds distasteful — so it is close to the Way. Dwell well in your ground, be deep of heart, be humane toward others, keep faith in your word, set things in order, do the work well, move at the right time. Because it does not contend, it is without blame." **Source:** 王弼本 八章; 帛書 第五十二章 治水 reads 上善**治水**…水善利萬物而**有靜** (the virtue named is stillness; 不爭 survives only in the closing line) and **予善天** for the third rung. Not in Guodian at all. **Label:** `quoted`. **From:** top-10 #4 (changed).
12. **Kind:** decision rule. **In plain English:** Check the timing before anything else. If the time has come, drive out and give one short answer; if it has not, wander off, unpressured. And when you do speak, show less appetite than you feel. **Their words:** 且君子得其時則駕，不得其時則蓬累而行。吾聞之，良賈深藏若虛，君子盛德容貌若愚。去子之驕氣與多欲，態色與淫志，是皆無益於子之身。吾所以告子，若是而已。 — "A gentleman, when his time comes, drives out; when it does not, he drifts along, bundled, and goes. I have heard that the skilled merchant hides his goods deep as though his store were empty; a man of great virtue wears the face of a fool. Your arrogance and your many desires, your proud bearing and your wanton aims — none of these serve your body. This is all I have to tell you." **Source:** 史記 卷六十三老子韓非列傳, wikisource 史記/卷063 — Sima Qian's report of Laozi speaking to Confucius, not a text of the 道德經; nothing to collate. **Label:** `attributed`. **From:** top-10 #2 (changed — timing only).

## Gaps

- **I could not read a single word of Guodian.** Wikisource still has no Guodian transcription; I used the chapter list only (en.wikipedia Bamboo Texts of Guodian, citing Murphy 2006). It confirms every Guodian claim in the research — ch. 9, 37 and 64 present with 64 in fragments; 3, 8, 22, 29, 33, 67, 78 absent — and it is why items3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11 above carry no fourth-century-BCE attestation. I have added no Guodian readings, and for ch. 48 I can say Guodian preserves the beginning but not which words.
- **陸德明's 簡文 remains unidentified.** I confirmed his note at ch. 37 (簡文作不) and added his note at ch. 36 (簡文作歙，又作洽), but the term predates the excavation by two millennia and cannot be equated with Guodian. Not used as evidence anywhere above.
- **No third collation.** ctext.org still denies automated access; I did not work around it. Everything above rests on the 王弼本 page, 帛書校勘版, and 史記 卷063 — the same three witnesses the research used. A published apparatus (高明, 裘錫圭, 郭店楚簡校讀記) would likely surface variants in the lines I quote, especially 帛書 第三章's loan characters.
- **One philological judgment I could not close.** I read 明道如**費** (王弼 明道若昧) as not a loan pair — 費 and 昧 are not homophones — so the received image of a Way that looks dim may be an editorial smoothing, whereas 大器**免**成／晚成 and 道**殷**无名 are the silk's familiar loan substitutions and are secure. I did not find a source that adjudicates 費, so #6's wording is weaker than the rest of the item.
- **The date of the text stays open.** Guodian sets c. 300 BCE for parts, Mawangdui c. 168 BCE for the whole; I saw nothing that places the received text's composition more precisely than "compiled over centuries."
- **Two good items did not fit and are not padding-worthy substitutes:** ch. 11's 有之以為利，無之以為用 and ch. 20's 我獨頑似鄙 (the one other pure mindset). Both are in "What the top 10 missed" with sources; take ch. 20 as #13 if the list ever runs to thirteen.
