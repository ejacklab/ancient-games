# Identity brief — who is this person, and their top 10 decision rules

You are researching ONE person (named in your seat brief) for EJ's Ancient Games project. The person will become a
reviewer "seat" built from their written principles — so what matters is how they actually decided, in their own
words, not their reputation or style.

Use the web (search and fetch) as well as what you know. Prefer primary sources: the person's own writing, speeches,
letters, or the oldest text that records them.

## Write exactly this (Markdown, no preamble)

# <Name> — who they are and how they decided

## Who they are
5–8 sentences: when, where, what they did, what they are known for deciding. Cite sources.

## Top 10 decision rules
For each, numbered 1–10, most important first:
- **Rule (plain English):** one sentence, phrased as a rule you could apply: "When X, do Y" or "Prefer A over B because C".
- **Their words:** the original text, in its original language (Chinese for Chinese sources), plus an English gloss.
- **Source:** book + chapter / letter / talk + date / URL. Precise enough that someone can find it.
- **How it decides:** what goes in (the situation) → what comes out (the choice).
- **Label:** `quoted` (their own words, source checked) · `attributed` (recorded by someone else, e.g. 史記 about
  范蠡, or a teacher's words credited to them) · `my reading` (a pattern you infer from what they did, no direct quote).

## Sources used
List every URL or text you actually opened.

## Gaps
Anything you could not source well. **If you cannot find 10 well-sourced rules, give fewer and say so here. Do not
pad the list, invent a quote, or round a paraphrase up to a quote.** A wrong quote is worse than a missing one.
