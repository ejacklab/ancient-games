# Review brief — top 12 mindsets, decision rules and algorithms

You are reviewing ONE person's research for EJ's Ancient Games project. Read these files first:
- your person's research: `runs/20261013-four-seats/identity/<seat>.md` (named in your seat brief)
- the quote check: `runs/20261013-four-seats/identity/quote-check.md` (which quotes were checked against the source text)

The research gave a top 10 decision rules. Your job: review it critically, then give the person's **top 12 mindsets,
decision rules or algorithms**. You may use the web to check or to find what the first list missed.

The three kinds, so each item can be labelled:
- **mindset** — a standing attitude they bring to every situation (no trigger)
- **decision rule** — "when X, do Y" or "prefer A over B"
- **algorithm** — an ordered procedure: steps, or a set of checks run in order

## Write exactly this (Markdown, no preamble)

# <Name> — top 12, reviewed

## Review of the top 10
One line per rule 1–10: **keep / change / drop**, and why. Ask of each: is this really how they decided, or a famous
line? Is it one thing, or two merged? Is it ranked right? Is the quote the strongest one for it?

## What the top 10 missed
Mindsets or algorithms the first list left out, if any, with why they matter.

## Top 12
Numbered 1–12, most important first. For each:
- **Kind:** mindset · decision rule · algorithm
- **In plain English:** one or two sentences. For an algorithm, the steps in order.
- **Their words:** original text in its original language, plus an English gloss.
- **Source:** precise enough to find.
- **Label:** `quoted` · `attributed` · `my reading` (same meaning as in the research).
- **From:** `top-10 #n` (kept or changed) or `new`.

## Gaps
What you could not source. **If you cannot find 12 well-sourced items, give fewer and say so. Do not pad the list,
invent a quote, or round a paraphrase up to a quote.** A wrong quote is worse than a missing one.
