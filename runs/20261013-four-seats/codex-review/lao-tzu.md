## Claims elsewhere in the file that the sources contradict

- **“All ten” manuscript notes are correct.** The supplied silk edition places 無為／received ch. 48 at **silk chapter 11**, not 48. Silk chapter 48 is 道用／received ch. 4. Likewise, received ch. 44 is **silk chapter 7**, not the “帛書 44 立戒” cited under omissions; silk chapter 44 is 右介／received ch. 79.

- **Silk chapter 67 equals received “22 + 24,” and “the joining of the two is the manuscript's.”** The supplied text labels silk chapter 67 **(22)** and chapter 66 **(24)**. Received ch. 22 already contains the affirmative self-restraint clauses. Their presence in silk ch. 67 does not establish that it incorporates ch. 24.

- **The silk reversal chapter “adds a fourth pair.”** Both witnesses have four pairs: 王弼 ch. 36 has 歙／張、弱／強、廢／興、奪／與; silk ch. 80 has 翕／張、弱／強、去／與、奪／予. The pairs differ; their count does not.

- **The earlier weak-over-strong rule must be joined to the reversal chapter.** Silk ch. 80 does contain 柔弱勝強, but silk ch. 43 independently contains 水之勝剛，弱之勝強 and 天下莫弗知，而莫能行也 (`silk.txt:455–457`). The review mixes these separate passages when describing a single chapter's “own sting.”

- **Chapter 8 supplies “eight 善” checks.** The quoted 居善地…動善時 list has seven clauses in both supplied witnesses.

- **The three treasures are merely a contents list rather than a rule.** 王弼 ch. 67 explicitly says 持而保之 and warns 今舍慈且勇，舍儉且廣，舍後且先，死矣. Silk ch. 32 likewise supplies the warning (`silk.txt:350`). An invented “when you cannot keep all three” trigger should be removed, but the source itself contains prescriptions.

- **The Shiji counsel is the “only recorded decision.”** The same biography records another decision: 見周之衰，乃遂去—seeing Zhou's decline, Laozi departed (`史記_卷063.txt:15`). This is the historian's narration, not Laozi's direct speech.

- **Silk reads 成功成而弗居.** Its actual ch. 46 wording is 成功而弗居也 (`silk.txt:500`). Item #7 gives this correctly; the earlier review adds an extra 成.

- Guodian coverage, manuscript dates, exhaustive verification of earlier research, and the proposed loan-character identifications are **not checkable here**. The supplied folder contains neither Guodian text nor its chapter table, dating evidence, or a philological apparatus establishing those identifications.

## Missing

- **Knowing when to stop.** 王弼 ch. 44: 知足不辱，知止不殆，可以長久; silk ch. 7 repeats it (`silk.txt:87`). #10 mentions sufficiency but does not retain the explicit stopping rule.

- **Usefulness through absence.** 王弼 ch. 11: 故有之以為利，無之以為用. The wheel, vessel, and room examples explain this principle; it appears in the file's omissions but remains outside the twelve.

- **Restraint in using force.** 王弼 ch. 31: 不得已而用之…勿美也; 戰勝以喪禮處之. Silk ch. 75 also contains these prescriptions. The twelve omit this explicit limit on military action and its treatment of victory.

- **Compassion, frugality, and refusing precedence.** 王弼 ch. 67 and silk ch. 32 explicitly prescribe preserving these and warn against their abandonment. Removing the invented trigger does not justify excluding the underlying rule.

- **Leadership through taking the lower position.** 王弼 ch. 66: 欲上民，必以言下之；欲先民，必以身後之. Silk ch. 29 preserves the same conditional structure (`silk.txt:319`). This is a more explicit leadership decision rule than several constructed procedures in the twelve.

## Overall

**My reading:** The principal failure is unsupported conversion of passages into procedures: ordered stages, added conditions, and modern evaluation methods repeatedly appear under a `quoted` label. **My reading:** The review also introduces source-location errors and mistaken claims about chapter mergers and checklist counts. **Not measured:** This is my comparison against the three supplied texts, not an independent manuscript collation, verification of historical authorship, or evidence for the exact ranking.
