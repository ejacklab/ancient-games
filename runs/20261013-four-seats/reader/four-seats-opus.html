Final list

1. Keep to the time: Heaven's time and human affairs together

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: While Heaven's time has not arisen, do not be the one who attacks; while human affairs have not arisen, do not be the one who starts. Success needs human affairs joined with Heaven and Earth. When one had come and the other had not, he told the king to wait.
  • Their words: 「夫聖人隨時以行，是謂守時。天時不作，弗為人客；人事不起，弗為之始。」 — "The sage moves with the time; this is called keeping to the time. When Heaven's time has not arisen, do not be the guest [the attacker]; when human affairs have not arisen, do not be the one who begins." / 「人事至矣，天應未也，王姑待之。」 — "Human affairs have arrived; Heaven's response has not. For now, wait." / 「天應至矣，人事未盡也，王姑待之。」… 「夫人事必將與天地相參，然後乃可以成功。」 — "Heaven's response has arrived; human affairs are not yet at their end. For now, wait." … "Human affairs must be joined with Heaven and Earth as a three; only then can success be achieved." (韋昭: 攻者為客; 先動為始; 人事 = 怨叛、逆亂之萌; 參，三也.)
  • Source: 國語·越語下 (Wikisource 國語/卷21): §范蠡進諫句踐持盈定傾節事 (國語21:3). There he tells the king that attacking now 「逆於天而不和於人」; the king attacked and lost (21:5). Also §范蠡謂人事至而天應未至 (21:15), on Wu's internal decay, and §范蠡謂人事與天地相參乃可以成功 (21:19).
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: top-12 #1 (split), with top-10 #1's quotes restored

2. Three kinds of state affairs, three things to go by

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: A state's affairs are keeping what is full, steadying what is tilting, and regulating affairs. Keeping the full goes with Heaven, steadying the tilting goes with people, and regulating affairs goes with Earth. Heaven's way, in his words, is full without overflowing, flourishing without arrogance, and labouring without boasting of its work.
  • Their words: 「夫國家之事，有持盈，有定傾，有節事。」/「持盈者與天，定傾者與人，節事者與地。」/ 「天道盈而不溢，盛而不驕，勞而不矜其功。」 — "In the affairs of a state there is keeping the full, steadying the tilting, and regulating affairs." / "Keeping the full goes with Heaven; steadying the tilting goes with people; regulating affairs goes with Earth." / "Heaven's way is full but does not overflow, flourishes but is not arrogant, labours but does not boast of its work." (韋昭: 持，守也; 盈，滿也; 傾，危也; 節，制也; 與天，法天也.)
  • Source: 國語21:3. He repeats it after the defeat at 21:7 (「君王其忘之乎？持盈者與天…」) and fills in two of the three: 與人 at 21:7 (→ #15) and 與地 at 21:9 (→ #3). 史記·越王句踐世家 41:18 has the variant 「持滿者與天，定傾者與人，節事者以地」.
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: top-12 #1 (split)

3. While the time has not come

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: When the time has not come, growth cannot be forced, and when a matter has not run its course, completion cannot be forced. Stay as you are, take the measure of the world, wait for what comes and set it right, and keep the people working, fed and not idle. When the time turns and an opening appears, one must know the constant measures of Heaven and Earth; if there is no opening and no turn, care for and protect the people and wait.
  • Their words: 「時不至，不可彊生；事不究，不可彊成。自若以處，以度天下，待其來者而正之，因時之所宜而定之。 同男女之功，除民之害，以避天殃。田野開闢，府倉實，民眾殷。無曠其眾，以為亂梯。時將有反，事將有閒， 必有以知天地之恆制，乃可以有天下之成利。事無閒，時無反，則撫民保教以須之。」 — "When the time has not come, growth cannot be forced; when the matter has not run its course, completion cannot be forced. Stay as you are and take the measure of the world; wait for what comes and set it right; settle it by what the time makes fitting. Join the work of men and women, remove the people's harms, and so avoid Heaven's punishment. Fields opened, storehouses full, people numerous. Do not leave the people idle — that becomes a ladder to disorder. When the time is about to turn and affairs are about to show an opening, one must have the means to know the constant measures of Heaven and Earth; then one can have the world's full benefit. If affairs show no opening and the time does not turn, care for the people, protect and teach them, and wait." (韋昭: 自如，無妄動也; 待其來 = 不先唱; 究，窮也.)
  • Source: 國語21:9, his answer to 「節事奈何？」 after returning from Wu.
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: top-12 #2

4. When the time comes, go; having it, finish

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Follow the time the way you fight a fire or chase a runaway: hurry, afraid only of arriving late. Having got the time, do not slacken. What Heaven gives and is not taken, or a time that is got but not carried through, turns back on you as calamity.
  • Their words: 「微君王之言，臣故將謁之。臣聞從時者，猶救火、追亡人也，蹶而趨之，唯恐弗及。」 — "Had you not said it, I was about to ask for it. I have heard: one who follows the time is like one fighting a fire or chasing a runaway — run and hurry, fearing only not to get there." / 「得時無怠，時不再來，天予不取，反為之災。」 — "Having got the time, do not slacken; the time does not come again; what Heaven gives and you do not take turns into disaster." / 「得時不成，天有還形。天節不遠，五年復反，小凶則近，大凶則遠。」 — "If you get the time and do not complete it, Heaven has a way of turning it back. Heaven's period is not far: in five years it comes round — near for a small misfortune, far for a great one." (韋昭: 小凶 = 危敗, 大凶 = 死滅; near = five years, far = ten or twenty.) / 史記: 「天與弗取，反受其咎。」 — "If Heaven gives and you do not take, you receive the blame in return."
  • Source: 國語21:21 (玄月, the ninth month per 韋昭; the king says the year is late, and the army marches). 21:23: said while advising against accepting Wu's five challenges in one day. 21:28: said against accepting Wu's first peace mission. The same 「得時不成，反受其殃」 appears at 21:13. 史記41:32: said against accepting Wu's last peace mission. Finish: after the king handed him the reply (21:30), 「范蠡不報於王，擊鼓興師以隨使者…遂滅吳」 (21:32, narration).
  • Words are: quoted (the march at 21:32 is attributed — narration)
  • From: top-12 #4

5. The plan does not shift

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: A plan made at court should not be lost on the battlefield. Advance and retreat turn into each other and Heaven's rhythm changes; only the plan does not shift. Years of planning are not to be thrown away in a morning.
  • Their words: 「夫謀之廊廟，失之中原，其可乎？王姑勿許也。…贏縮轉化，後將悔之。天節固然，唯謀不遷。」 — "To plan it in the temple hall and lose it on the central plain — can that be right? For now, do not accept. … Advance and retreat change into each other; later you would regret it. Heaven's rhythm is like that; only the plan does not move." (韋昭: 贏縮，進退也; 固然，有轉化也; 謀必素定，不可遷易.) / 「夫十年謀之，一朝而棄之，其可乎？王姑勿許，其事將易冀已。」 — "Ten years of planning, thrown away in one morning — can that be right? For now, do not accept; the matter will then be easy to hope for." / 史記: 「謀之二十二年，一旦而棄之，可乎？」
  • Source: 國語21:23 (Wu challenged to battle five times in a day and the king wanted to accept); 21:30 (Wu's envoy came back humbler and the king wanted to accept peace); 史記41:32. A later witness: 越絕書·請糴內傳 (卷五) 「君王圖之廊廟，失之中野，可乎？謀之七年，須臾棄之。」
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: top-12 #6

6. Leaving

  • Kind: mindset
  • Rule: A servant labours when his ruler is anxious and dies when his ruler is disgraced. He had stayed alive after 會稽 only for this task, so when it was done he asked for the 會稽 punishment and refused the share of the state. He warned 文種 that when the birds are gone the bow is put away, and that Goujian could share hardship but not joy. At the peak in Qi he said that holding an honoured name for long is unlucky.
  • Their words: 「臣聞之，為人臣者，君憂臣勞，君辱臣死。昔者君王辱於會稽，臣所以不死者，為此事也。今事已濟矣， 蠡請從會稽之罰。」…「臣聞命矣。君行制，臣行意。」 — "I have heard: a servant labours when his ruler is anxious, and dies when his ruler is disgraced. When you were disgraced at 會稽, the reason I did not die was this task. Now the task is done; I ask to receive the punishment of 會稽." … [to the threat: share the state, or die with wife and children] "I have heard your command. You act by your law; I act by my intent." (韋昭: 制，法也; 意，志也. 史記41:56 reads 君行令.) / 「蜚鳥盡，良弓藏；狡兔死，走狗烹。越王爲人長頸鳥喙，可與共患難，不可與共樂。 子何不去？」 — "When the flying birds are gone, the good bow is put away; when the cunning hare is dead, the hunting dog is cooked. The King of Yue has a long neck and a bird's beak; one can share hardship with him, not joy. Why don't you leave?" / 「居家則致千金，居官則至卿相，此布衣之極也。久受尊名，不祥。」 — "At home I have reached a thousand in gold; in office, chief minister — the limit for a man in plain cloth. To hold an honoured name long is unlucky."
  • Source: 國語21:34 (§范蠡乘輕舟以浮於五湖); 史記41:56 (the same duty line in his letter to Goujian); 史記41:36 (his letter to 文種, from Qi); 史記41:58 (Qi, followed by the narration 「乃歸相印，盡散其財」).
  • Words are: quoted. The narrator's reasons at 史記41:56 (「范蠡以爲大名之下，難以久居，且句踐爲人可與同患，難與處安」) and the departures are attributed.
  • From: top-12 #5

7. Do not plan early

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: While the Lord on High has not brought it to completion, wait for the time to turn; seeking it by force is unlucky. There is taking back, giving and not giving, so do not plan early. Wu is already the king's, and planning for it early would make the outcome unknowable again.
  • Their words: 「未可也。蠡聞之，上帝不考，時反是守，彊索者不祥。得時不成，反受其殃。失德滅名，流走死亡。有奪，有予， 有不予，王無蚤圖。夫吳，君王之吳也，王若蚤圖之，其事又將未可知也。」 — "Not yet. I have heard: when the Lord on High has not completed it, keep watch for the time to turn; one who seeks by force is unlucky. Get the time and not complete it, and you receive its calamity; lose virtue and your name is destroyed, you flee and die. There is taking back, there is giving, there is not giving. Do not plan early. Wu is Your Majesty's Wu; if you plan for it early, the matter will again be unknowable." (韋昭: 考，成也; 天時反，乃可以動; 索，求也.)
  • Source: 國語21:13 (§范蠡勸句踐無蚤圖吳), the first of four yearly "not yet" answers.
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: new (my research)

8. A fresh disaster can unite the enemy

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: When the enemy's disaster is new, its people are afraid, and everyone from the ruler down knows their resources cannot last, they will pool their strength and fight to the death, so attacking is still dangerous. Meanwhile hunt, feast and drink without excess and without forgetting the state's standing rules. Their ruler will thin his virtue, their people will use up their strength and they will look for food and not get it; then Heaven's and Earth's punishment can be brought on. Until then, wait.
  • Their words: 「今其禍新民恐，其君臣上下，皆知其資財之不足以支長久也，彼將同其力，致其死，猶尚殆。王其且馳騁弋獵， 無至禽荒；宮中之樂，無至酒荒；肆與大夫觴飲，無忘國常。彼其上將薄其德，民將盡其力，又使之望而不得食， 乃可以致天地之殛。王姑待之。」 — "Now their disaster is new and the people are afraid; ruler and ministers, high and low, all know their wealth will not hold out long. They will unite their strength and give their lives — it is still dangerous. Your Majesty, go riding and hunting for now, but not to the point of being lost in the hunt; palace pleasures, but not to drunken ruin; drink freely with the officers, but do not forget the state's standing rules. Their ruler will thin his virtue, their people will exhaust their strength, and they will look for food and not get it; then the punishment of Heaven and Earth can be brought about. For now, wait." (Why hunt: 韋昭 「示不以吳為念」 — to show Yue is not thinking of Wu. That purpose is the commentator's, not Fan Li's words.)
  • Source: 國語21:19. He said it after crabs had eaten Wu's rice (「稻蟹不遺種」) and the king angrily asked why he kept switching between 人事 and 天時. 韋昭: Yue attacked four years later.
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: new (codex "Missing" #1, checked by me against 國語21:19)

9. Disorder that has only sprouted

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: When Wu killed its remonstrating minister, he said that rebellion against order had only sprouted and Heaven and Earth had not yet shown their form. To campaign ahead of that is why a venture fails, and everyone shares the harm, so wait.
  • Their words: 「逆節萌生。天地未形，而先為之征，其事是以不成，雜受其刑。王姑待之。」 — "Rebellion against order is sprouting. Heaven and Earth have not yet taken visible form, and to campaign ahead of it — that is why the affair does not succeed, and all together suffer the harm. For now, wait." (韋昭: 萌，兆也; 形，見也，天地之占未見; 征，征伐也; 雜，猶俱也; 刑，害也.)
  • Source: 國語21:17 (§范蠡謂先為之征其事不成), on news that 申胥 (伍子胥) had been killed. 史記41:28 records the same case briefly: 「呉已殺子胥，導諛者衆，可乎？」對曰：「未可。」 At 41:30 he says 「可矣」 the next spring. In the narrator's words, Wu's crack troops had then gone north with the king, leaving only the old, the weak and the heir.
  • Words are: quoted (the conditions at 史記41:30 are attributed — narration)
  • From: top-12 #3

10. Turning at the extreme, and stopping

  • Kind: mindset
  • Rule: Those of old who used troops well took advance and retreat as the norm and the four seasons as the measure. They did not go past Heaven's limit, and they stopped when the count ran out. Yang at its height turns to yin and yin to yang; the sun at its end comes back, and the full moon begins to wane.
  • Their words: 「臣聞古之善用兵者，贏縮以為常，四時以為紀，無過天極，究數而止。天道皇皇，日月以為常，明者以為法， 微者則是行。陽至而陰，陰至而陽；日困而還，月盈而匡。古之善用兵者，因天地之常，與之俱行。」 — "I have heard that those of old who used troops well took advance and retreat as their constant and the four seasons as their rule; they did not pass Heaven's limit, and stopped when the count was exhausted. Heaven's way is bright, with sun and moon as its constant; the bright is taken as the model, the waning is acted on accordingly. When yang reaches its height it turns to yin; when yin reaches its height, to yang. The sun, exhausted, returns; the moon, full, wanes. Those of old who used troops well went by the constants of Heaven and Earth and moved with them." (韋昭 on 究數而止: 「無過天道之所至，窮其數而止也」; on 明/微: advance when sun and moon are full, withdraw when they wane.)
  • Source: 國語21:25, the opening of his 「臣聞古之善用兵者」 speech during the campaign.
  • Words are: quoted (he gives it as received teaching — 臣聞)
  • From: new (my research; split out of the passage behind top-12 #8)

11. People start it, Heaven and Earth show it, the sage completes it

  • Kind: mindset
  • Rule: With enemy states and decisions taken on the spot, go by the constants of yin and yang and the norms of Heaven and Earth: be soft without bending and strong without hardness. Heaven goes by people and the sage goes by Heaven: people bring a thing about, Heaven and Earth make it visible, and the sage completes it. So a victory is not repaid, land taken is not given back, and very little force is used.
  • Their words: 「四封之外，敵國之制，立斷之事，因陰陽之恆，順天地之常，柔而不屈，彊而不剛，德虐之行，因以為常； 死生因天地之刑，天因人，聖人因天；人自生之，天地形之，聖人因而成之。是故戰勝而不報，取地而不反，兵勝於外，福生於內， 用力甚少而名聲章明，種亦不如蠡也。」 — "Outside the four borders — dealing with enemy states, matters decided on the spot: go by the constant of yin and yang, follow the norm of Heaven and Earth; be soft but not bent, strong but not hard; let favour and severity follow these as the rule; let death and life follow the law of Heaven and Earth. Heaven goes by people; the sage goes by Heaven. People bring it about themselves, Heaven and Earth give it form, the sage goes by that and completes it. So battles are won and not repaid, land is taken and not given back, arms win outside and good fortune grows inside, very little force is used and the name shines — in this 種 is not as good as I." (韋昭: 德 = 懷柔及爵賞, 虐 = 斬伐及黜奪; 戰勝而不報 = 敵家不能報也.)
  • Source: 國語21:11, his reply when the king said 「不穀之國家，蠡之國家也，蠡其圖之！」. The king then had 種 govern the state (「令大夫種為之」; 韋昭: 為，治國也).
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: top-12 #11 (merged, widened to the whole passage)

12. When to engage

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: If they come at you, hold firm and do not engage. Engage only on a disaster from Heaven and Earth, cross-checked by whether their people are hungry or fed and worn or rested; let their yang run out and your yin fill, then take it. A side that is hard, strong and swift cannot be taken while its yang has not run out. Seeing Wu come that way, he said wait.

17. Send the one who can part with money

  1. Take away, and take away again.

    • Kind: algorithm. The words give a repeated step and its end point (損之又損，以至於無為), and nothing more.
    • Rule: Learning adds something every day; the Way takes something away every day. Take away, and take away again, until you reach not-doing (無為); with not-doing, nothing is left undone.
    • Their words: 為學日益，為道日損。損之又損，以至於無為。無為而無不為。取天下常以無事，及其有事，不足以取天下。 — "Pursuing learning, one gains daily; pursuing the Way, one loses daily. Lose and lose again, until one arrives at not-doing.   17. The use is in what is not there.

  18. When your time comes, ride out; when it does not, drift away.

  19. Where others take the front, take the rear; restraint as the rule.

    • Kind: mindset
    • Rule: Where everyone takes the front, he alone takes the rear, saying he takes on the world's reproach (variant: filth). Where everyone takes the solid, he alone takes the empty, and where everyone seeks good fortune, he alone stays bent and whole, saying "just escape blame". Everyone seeks good fortune; he alone keeps bent and whole, saying: 'Just escape blame.' He takes depth as the root and restraint as the rule, saying: 'The hard is broken; the sharp is blunted.' Final list

  1. A change that breaks what used to work for users is a kernel bug: fix it or revert it, in the kernel, and admit it.

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: If a change makes user programs break, the bug is in the kernel, not in the user program. The change must be fixed or reverted, and fixed in the kernel. When a regression happens, it is admitted and fixed instead of blamed on user space. If the kernel used to work for you, it continues to work for you. He says there have been exceptions, but "few and far between". For API breakage inside the kernel: whoever broke something fixes it too.
    • Their words: (English original)
      • "If a change results in user programs breaking, it's a bug in the kernel. We never EVER blame the user programs." … "WE DO NOT BREAK USERSPACE!"
      • "The first rule is: - we don't cause regressions and the corollary is that when regressions do occur, we admit to them and fix them, instead of blaming user space."
      • "If the kernel used to work for you, the rule is that it continues to work for you. There have been exceptions, but they are few and far between, and they generally have some major and fundamental reasons for having happened, that were basically entirely unavoidable, and people tried_hard to avoid them."
      • "But if something actually breaks, then the change must get fixed or reverted. And it gets fixed in the kernel."
      • "Nobody can say "I now broke the API you used, and now you need to fix it up". Whoever broke something gets to fix it too."

  2. Talk is cheap; show the code.

    • Kind: mindset
    • Rule: A claim that something can be built costs nothing to make; what he asks for is the code. In 2017 he did not accept a main pull request because its untested code "clearly didn't do what it claimed it did".
    • Their words: (English original)
      • "Talk is cheap. Show me the code." (his whole reply to Jamie Lokier's claim that the best of user-space and kernel threads could be combined "in less than 8kB per thread")

    • Words are: quoted

  3. What counts as a regression is a user's breakage, not an ABI, a document or a test.

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: A regression is the kernel not giving the same end result with the same user space. The rule is about breaking a user's workflow. It is not about documented behaviour, about where the code lives, or about behaviour never changing. A test that now behaves differently is a warning sign, not by itself a regression. Changing an API is fine as long as nobody notices.
    • Their words: (English original)
      • "As far as the kernel is concerned, a regressions is THE KERNEL NOT GIVING THE SAME END RESULT WITH THE SAME USER SPACE." (2017)
      • "The rules about regressions have never been about any kind of documented behavior, or where the code lives. The rules about regressions are always about "breaks user workflow". Users are literally the only thing that matters." (2020)
      • "And our regression rule has never been "behavior doesn't change". That would mean that we could never make any changes at all." (2017)
      • "But if you have some random test that now behaves differently, it's not a regression. It's a warning sign, sure: tests are useful." (2024)
      • "You can make any changes to an API you like - as long as nobody notices." (2020/2021, see Gaps)
    • Source: 2017 line: LKML 26 Oct 2017, https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/1710.3/02474.html (opened).     • Words are: quoted
    • From: new (my research)

  4. Avoid having to make the big decisions; the people who know the details should not need you to pick.

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: The aim is to avoid having to make decisions, at least the big and painful ones. If people you manage ask you to choose (a) or (b) on a technical matter, you are in trouble: they should know the details better than you, so you are not competent to decide it for them. If they don't know the details better than you, you are in the wrong job. Small, non-consequential decisions are fine. The job is to turn the big and painful ones into small things.
    • Their words: (English original) "The name of the game is to avoid having to make a decision. In particular, if somebody tells you "choose (a) or (b), we really need you to decide on this", you're in trouble as a manager. The people you manage had better know the details better than you, so if they come to you for a technical decision, you're screwed. You're clearly not competent to make that decision for them." … "So the name of the game is to avoid decisions, at least the big and painful ones. Making small and non-consequential decisions is fine, and makes you look like you know what you're doing, so what a kernel manager needs to do is to turn the big and painful ones into small things where nobody really cares."
    • Source: Documentation/process/management-style.rst §1 "Decisions", https://docs.kernel.org/process/management-style.html. The same text is in LWN, 6 Oct 2004, "Linus on kernel management style", https://lwn.net/Articles/105375/
    • Words are: quoted. Authorship is by attribution only (see Gaps).
    • From: top-12 #1 (corrected)

  5. A decision is big only if it can't be undone; avoid doing things that can't be undone.

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: What separates a big decision from a small one is whether you can fix it afterwards. Any decision can be made small by making sure that, if you were wrong (and you will be wrong), you can undo the damage by backtracking. So avoid doing things that can't be undone. Deleting the work is easy, but you may have irrevocably lost the trust of the engineer who did it, and then the decision was a big one after all.
    • Their words: (English original) "It helps to realize that the key difference between a big decision and a small one is whether you can fix your decision afterwards. Any decision can be made small by just always making sure that if you were wrong (and you will be wrong), you can always undo the damage later by backtracking." … "Thus the key to avoiding big decisions becomes to just avoiding to do things that can't be undone. Don't get ushered into a corner from which you cannot escape." … "while the actual work was easy enough to undo by just deleting it, you may have irrevocably lost the trust of that engineer. And remember: "irrevocable" was what we tried to avoid in the first place, and your decision ended up being a big one after all."
    • Source: management-style.rst §1 "Decisions"; LWN 2004 (as #4)
    • Words are: quoted. Authorship is by attribution only (see Gaps).
    • From: top-12 #2 (corrected)

  6. Let people smarter than you make the decisions; prod, don't push too hard.

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: Take advantage of smart people: they can make your decisions for you. When you find somebody smarter than you, coast along, and your job becomes saying "Sounds like a good idea - go wild" or "That sounds good, but what about xxx?". Greatness in one area does not necessarily carry over to others. You may prod people in a direction, which is not irreversible because people drift back to what they are good at, but don't push too hard.
    • Their words: (English original) "Some people react badly to smart people. Others take advantage of them. Make sure that you, as a kernel maintainer, are in the second group. Suck up to them, because they are the people who will make your job easier. In particular, they'll be able to make your decisions for you, which is what the game is all about." … "So when you find somebody smarter than you are, just coast along. Your management responsibilities largely become ones of saying "Sounds like a good idea - go wild", or "That sounds good, but what about xxx?"." … "One thing to look out for is to realize that greatness in one area does not necessarily translate to other areas. So you might prod people in specific directions, but let's face it, they might be good at what they do, and suck at everything else. The good news is that people tend to naturally gravitate back to what they are good at, so it's not like you are doing something irreversible when you do prod them in some direction, just don't push too hard."
    • Source: management-style.rst §3 "People II - the Good Kind"; LWN 2004
    • Words are: quoted. Authorship is by attribution only (see Gaps).
    • From: top-12 #4 (corrected)

  7. Say up front that you may be wrong; give people critical thinking, not confidence.

    • Kind: mindset
    • Rule: Admit up front that you don't know. Tell people ahead of time that your decision is preliminary and might be wrong, keep the right to change your mind, and make sure they know it. If the people doing the work aren't sure their work is a good idea, don't encourage them by promising it will be included; make them think twice before a big endeavour. The best thing a manager can do is give them a healthy dose of critical thinking about what they do, not confidence. And you are one of the idiots too.
    • Their words: (English original, in source order) "Happily, both of these reasons can be mitigated effectively by just admitting up-front that you don't have a friggin' clue, and telling people ahead of the fact that your decision is purely preliminary, and might be the wrong thing. You should always reserve the right to change your mind, and make people very aware of that." … "After all, if they aren't certain whether it's a good idea, you sure as hell shouldn't encourage them by promising them that what they work on will be included. Make them at least think twice before they embark on a big endeavor." … "The best thing you can do as a manager is not to instill confidence, but rather a healthy dose of critical thinking on what they do." … (§3) "While it turns out that most people are idiots, the corollary to that is sadly that you are one too"
    • Source: management-style.rst §1 "Decisions", §3 "People II - the Good Kind"; LWN 2004
    • Words are: quoted. Authorship is by attribution only (see Gaps).
    • From: top-12 #3 (corrected)

  8. When it isn't clear which of two approaches is better, ask "can't we just do both?"

    • Kind: decision rule
    • Rule: Another way to avoid a decision is to ask "can't we just do both?". If it is not clear which approach is better, the people involved will eventually figure it out. Sometimes both teams give up. He reads that as usually a sign that both projects had something wrong with them, and that they couldn't decide because both were wrong.
    • Their words: (English original) "Btw, another way to avoid a decision is to plaintively just whine "can't we just do both?" and look pitiful. Trust me, it works. If it's not clear which approach is better, they'll eventually figure it out. The answer may end up being that both teams get so frustrated by the situation that they just give up." … "That may sound like a failure, but it's usually a sign that there was something wrong with both projects, and the reason the people involved couldn't decide was that they were both wrong."

  12. Clean history: rebase your own private work, never other people's history; merge only for a reason.

1. Reckon before battle

  • Kind: algorithm
  • Rule: Before fighting, lay the war out by five matters (the Way, heaven, ground, the general, law) and compare the two sides on seven questions. Whoever comes out ahead in the reckoning before battle wins, whoever has less does not, and having none is worse still. A second passage gives the reckoning as an ordered chain: the ground gives measurement, measurement gives quantity, quantity gives number, number gives the weighing of the sides, and the weighing gives victory.
  • Their words: 「兵者，國之大事，死生之地，存亡之道，不可不察也。故經之以五事，校之以七計，而索其情：一曰道，二曰天，三曰地，四曰將，五曰法。」…「道者，令民與上同意，可與之死，可與之生，而不畏危也。」…「故校之以七計而索其情，曰：主孰有道？將孰有能？天地孰得？法令孰行？兵眾孰強？士卒孰練？賞罰孰明？吾以此知勝負矣。」…「夫未戰而廟算勝者，得算多也；未戰而廟算不勝者，得算少也。多算勝，少算不勝，而況於無算乎？」 — "War is a great matter of state, the ground of life and death, the way to survival or ruin; it must be examined. So lay it out by five matters, compare it by seven reckonings, and search out the true situation: the Way, heaven, ground, the general, law… The Way is what makes the people one in intent with their superior, so that they will die with him or live with him and not fear danger… Compare by the seven reckonings: Which ruler has the Way? Which general has ability? Who has heaven and ground? Whose laws and orders are carried out? Whose troops are stronger? Whose officers and men are better trained? Whose rewards and punishments are clearer? By this I know victory and defeat… One who wins the temple reckoning before battle has gained more 算; one who does not has gained fewer. More wins, fewer does not, how much less none at all?" 「兵法：一曰度，二曰量，三曰數，四曰稱，五曰勝。地生度，度生量，量生數，數生稱，稱生勝。故勝兵若以鎰稱銖，敗兵若以銖稱鎰。」 — "In the method of war: first measurement, second quantity, third number, fourth weighing, fifth victory. Ground gives rise to measurement, measurement to quantity, quantity to number, number to weighing, weighing to victory. So a winning army is like a yi weighed against a zhu, a losing army like a zhu against a yi."
  • Source: 《孫子兵法》始計第一 (l.12–18); 軍形第四 (l.59).

2. Make yourself unbeatable first; win, then seek battle

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: First make yourself unbeatable, then wait for the enemy to become beatable. Being unbeatable is in your own hands, being beatable is in the enemy's, and you cannot make the enemy certain to be beatable; so the winning army wins first and then seeks battle. Do not rely on the enemy not coming or not attacking; rely on having the means to meet them and on having what cannot be attacked.
  • Their words: 「昔之善戰者，先為不可勝，以待敵之可勝。不可勝在己，可勝在敵。故善戰者，能為不可勝，不能使敵之必可勝。故曰：勝可知，而不可為。」…「古之所謂善戰者，勝於易勝者也。」…「故善戰者，立於不敗之地，而不失敵之敗也。是故勝兵先勝而後求戰，敗兵先戰而後求勝。」 — "The skilled fighters of old first made themselves unbeatable and waited for the enemy to become beatable. Being unbeatable lies with oneself; being beatable lies with the enemy. So the skilled fighter can make himself unbeatable but cannot make the enemy certain to be beatable. Hence: victory can be known but cannot be made… Those the ancients called skilled fighters won where winning was easy… The skilled fighter stands where he cannot be defeated and does not miss the enemy's defeat. So the winning army wins first and then seeks battle; the losing army fights first and then seeks victory." 「故用兵之法，無恃其不來，恃吾有以待之；無恃其不攻，恃吾有所不可攻也。」 — "Do not rely on their not coming; rely on having the means to meet them. Do not rely on their not attacking; rely on having what cannot be attacked."

3. Whole over broken; strike plans first, walled cities last

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Prefer keeping things whole to breaking them, at every scale from the state to the five-man squad. Subduing the other side's army without battle is the best of the best; a hundred victories in a hundred battles is not. Rank the targets as follows: plans highest, then alliances, then armies, and attacking walled cities lowest, as the method for when there is no alternative.
  • Their words: 「凡用兵之法，全國為上，破國次之；全軍為上，破軍次之；全旅為上，破旅次之；全卒為上，破卒次之；全伍為上，破伍次之。是故百戰百勝，非善之善者也；不戰而屈人之兵，善之善者也。故上兵伐謀，其次伐交，其次伐兵，其下攻城。攻城之法，為不得已。修櫓轒轀，具器械，三月而後成；距闉，又三月而後已。將不勝其忿，而蟻附之，殺士三分之一，而城不拔者，此攻之災也。故善用兵者，屈人之兵而非戰也，拔人之城而非攻也，毀人之國而非久也，必以全爭於天下，故兵不頓而利可全，此謀攻之法也。」 — "In using troops, a whole state is best, a broken one next; a whole army best, a broken one next; and so for brigade, company and squad. So a hundred victories in a hundred battles is not the best of the best; to subdue the other's army without battle is the best of the best. So the highest use of troops strikes plans, the next strikes alliances, the next strikes troops, the lowest attacks walled cities. Attacking cities is the method for when there is no alternative: three months to make shields, assault carts and equipment, three more for the earthen mounds; if the general cannot master his anger and sends the men swarming up like ants, a third of them are killed and the city still stands — this is the calamity of attack. So the skilled user of troops subdues the other's army without battle, takes the other's city without attacking it, destroys the other's state without a long war; he contends for all under heaven by keeping things whole, so his troops are not blunted and the gain can be whole."

4. Know whether you can fight; know the other side and yourself

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule:
    • Five things tell you who will win:
      • knowing when you can and cannot fight;
      • knowing how to use large and small forces;
      • superiors and subordinates wanting the same thing;
      • being prepared and waiting for the unprepared;
      • an able general whom the ruler does not interfere with.
    • Knowing the other side and yourself, a hundred battles hold no danger. Knowing yourself but not them, you win one and lose one. Knowing neither, every battle is dangerous.
    • Knowing whether your troops can attack and whether the enemy can be attacked, but not whether the ground permits battle, is still only half a victory.
  • Their words: 「故知勝有五：知可以戰與不可以戰者勝，識衆寡之用者勝，上下同欲者勝，以虞待不虞者勝，將能而君不御者勝。此五者，知勝之道也。故曰：知彼知己，百戰不殆；不知彼而知己，一勝一負；不知彼不知己，每戰必殆。」 — "There are five ways to know victory: one who knows when he can fight and when he cannot wins; one who knows how to use many and few wins; one whose superiors and subordinates want the same thing wins; one who, prepared, waits for the unprepared wins; one whose general is able and whose ruler does not interfere wins. These five are the way to know victory. Hence: know the other and know yourself, and in a hundred battles you are not in danger; not knowing the other but knowing yourself, one win, one loss; knowing neither, in every battle you are in danger." 「知吾卒之可以擊，而不知敵之不可擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，而不知吾卒之不可以擊，勝之半也；知敵之可擊，知吾卒之可以擊，而不知地形之不可以戰，勝之半也。…故曰：知彼知己，勝乃不殆；知天知地，勝乃可全。」 — "Knowing our troops can attack but not that the enemy cannot be attacked is half a victory; knowing the enemy can be attacked but not that our troops cannot attack is half a victory; knowing the enemy can be attacked and our troops can attack, but not that the ground does not permit battle, is half a victory… Hence: know the other and yourself, and victory is not in danger; know heaven and ground, and victory can be whole."

5. Move only when it accords with advantage; never from anger

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Do not move without advantage, do not use troops without gain, do not fight unless in danger; a ruler must not raise an army out of anger, nor a general give battle out of resentment. Move when it accords with advantage and stop when it does not. Anger can turn back into joy, but a destroyed state cannot be restored and the dead cannot be brought back.
  • Their words: 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』。故曰：明主慮之，良將修之，非利不動，非得不用，非危不戰。主不可以怒而興師，將不可以慍而致戰。合於利而動，不合於利而止。怒可以復喜，慍可以復悅，亡國不可以復存，死者不可以復生。故明主慎之，良將警之，此安國全軍之道也。」 — "To win battles and take objectives without 修 the achievement is ill-omened; it is called 費留. A ruler must not raise an army out of anger; a general must not give battle out of resentment. Move when it accords with advantage; stop when it does not. Anger can become joy again, resentment can become pleasure again; a destroyed state cannot exist again, the dead cannot live again. So the enlightened ruler is careful of it and the good general is wary of it: this is the way to keep the state secure and the army whole." On 修 and 費留, the commentators split; see Changes #8. 6. In deliberating, mix harm into benefit

  • Kind: mindset

7. War is the way of deception

  • Kind: decision rule

  • Rule: War is the way of deception:

    • When able, appear unable; when using troops, appear not to; when near, appear far; when far, appear near.
    • Act on the enemy's condition: lure, take in disorder, prepare against the solid, avoid the strong, provoke the angry, make the humble arrogant, tire the rested, split the close-knit.
    • Attack where they are unprepared, and come out where they do not expect.

    War stands on deceit, moves by advantage, and changes by dividing and combining.

  • Their words: 「兵者，詭道也。故能而示之不能，用而示之不用，近而示之遠，遠而示之近。利而誘之，亂而取之，實而備之，強而避之，怒而撓之，卑而驕之，佚而勞之，親而離之。攻其無備，出其不意。此兵家之勝，不可先傳也。」 — "War is the way of deception. So when able, show inability; when using, show non-use; when near, show far; when far, show near. With advantage, lure them; in disorder, take them; solid, prepare against them; strong, avoid them; angry, stir them; humble, make them arrogant; rested, tire them; close, separate them. Attack where they are unprepared; come out where they do not expect. These are the strategist's victories; they cannot be handed down in advance." 「故兵以詐立，以利動，以分合為變者也。」 — "So war is established by deceit, moved by advantage, and varied by dividing and combining."

8. Value victory, not duration; speed; supply from the enemy

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: In war, value victory and not duration. A long campaign blunts the troops, exhausts strength and leaves the state short, and no state has ever benefited from a long war: clumsy speed is heard of, skilful prolongation has never been seen. Do not levy twice or haul grain three times; take provisions from the enemy, for one measure of his grain equals twenty of one's own.
  • Their words: 「凡用兵之法，馳車千駟，革車千乘，帶甲十萬，千里饋糧，…日費千金，然後十萬之師舉矣。其用戰也，貴勝，久則鈍兵挫銳，攻城則力屈，久暴師則國用不足。…故兵聞拙速，未睹巧之久也。夫兵久而國利者，未之有也。」…「善用兵者，役不再籍，糧不三載；取用於國，因糧於敵，故軍食可足也。」…「故智將務食於敵，食敵一鍾，當吾二十鍾。」…「故兵貴勝，不貴久。」 — "A thousand fast chariots, a thousand armoured carts, a hundred thousand armoured men, grain carried a thousand li… a thousand gold a day, and only then can an army of a hundred thousand be raised. In using it to fight, value victory: long, and it blunts the troops and dulls their edge; attacking cities, strength is spent; the army long in the field, the state's means fall short… So in war one hears of clumsy speed, but has not seen skill that is prolonged. A long war that benefits the state has never been… The skilled user of troops does not levy twice nor haul grain three times; he takes equipment from his own state and provisions from the enemy, so the army's food is enough… So the wise general strives to eat from the enemy: one zhong of the enemy's food equals twenty of mine… So in war value victory, not duration." 「故兵之情主速，乘人之不及，由不虞之道，攻其所不戒也。」 — "The nature of war is to rely on speed: take advantage of the other's not being in time, go by roads they do not anticipate, attack where they are not on guard."

9. Bring the other side to you; do not be brought

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Whoever is first on the field and awaits the enemy is fresh; whoever comes later and hurries into battle is weary. So the skilled fighter brings others and is not brought by them: he draws them with advantage and keeps them off with harm.
    • When he wants battle, he attacks what they must rescue.
    • When he does not, he diverts them from their course.
    • Against a large, ordered enemy, he first seizes what they cherish, and they comply.
  • Their words: 「凡先處戰地而待敵者佚，後處戰地而趨戰者勞。故善戰者，致人而不致於人。能使敵自至者，利之也；能使敵不得至者，害之也。」…「故我欲戰，敵雖高壘深溝，不得不與我戰者，攻其所必救也；我不欲戰，雖畫地而守之，敵不得與我戰者，乖其所之也。」 — "Whoever occupies the field first and awaits the enemy is at ease; whoever occupies it later and hurries to battle is weary. So the skilled fighter brings others and is not brought by others. What makes the enemy come of himself is advantage; what keeps him from coming is harm… When I want battle, though the enemy has high walls and deep moats, he must fight me, because I attack what he must rescue; when I do not want battle, though I only draw a line on the ground and hold it, he cannot fight me, because I divert him from where he is going." 「敢問：敵衆整而將來，待之若何？曰：先奪其所愛，則聽矣。」 — "May I ask: the enemy, many and in order, is about to come; how do I meet him? Answer: first seize what he cherishes, and he will comply."

10. Avoid the solid, strike the empty; have no constant form

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Make the enemy show its form while you show none: then you are concentrated and they are divided, and an enemy that prepares everywhere is thin everywhere. Like water, which avoids the high and runs to the low, an army avoids the solid, strikes the empty and shapes its victory by the enemy. It has no fixed 勢 and no constant form, and its victories are not repeated.
  • Their words: 「故形人而我無形，則我專而敵分。…無所不備，則無所不寡。寡者，備人者也；衆者，使人備己者也。」…「人皆知我所以勝之形，而莫知吾所以制勝之形。故其戰勝不復，而應形於無窮。」…「夫兵形象水，水之行，避高而趨下；兵之勝，避實而擊虛。水因地而制行，兵因敵而制勝。故兵無成勢，無恒形，能因敵變化而取勝者，謂之神。」 — "So I make others take form while I have none; then I am concentrated and the enemy divided… Preparing everywhere, he is few everywhere. Water shapes its course by the ground; an army shapes its victory by the enemy. So an army has no fixed 勢 and no constant form; one who can take victory by changing with the enemy is called divine."

11. Foreknowledge must come from people; pay for it

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Foreknowledge cannot be had from spirits, from likeness to past events, or from measurement; it must be got from people who know the enemy's situation. It is the height of inhumanity to keep a war going for years, at a ruinous daily cost, for a victory decided in one day, yet grudge ranks, salaries and a hundred pieces of gold and so not know the enemy's situation. Of the five kinds of spy, knowledge rests on the converted spy, so he must be treated most generously.
  • Their words: 「凡興師十萬，出征千里，百姓之費，公家之奉，日費千金，…相守數年，以爭一日之勝，而愛爵祿百金，不知敵之情者，不仁之至也，非人之將也，非主之佐也，非勝之主也。故明君賢將，所以動而勝人，成功出於眾者，先知也。先知者，不可取於鬼神，不可象於事，不可驗於度，必取於人，知敵之情者也。」…「五間之事，主必知之，知之必在於反間，故反間不可不厚也。」 — "Raising a hundred thousand and marching a thousand li, the people's spending and the state's outlay come to a thousand gold a day… To hold out for several years to contend for one day's victory, yet begrudge ranks, salaries and a hundred gold and so not know the enemy's situation, is the height of inhumanity: no general of men, no aid to a ruler, no master of victory. What lets the enlightened ruler and the wise general move and defeat others, with achievements beyond the crowd, is foreknowledge. Foreknowledge cannot be taken from ghosts and spirits, cannot be got by analogy with events, cannot be verified by measurement (度; 李筌: 「度，數也」); it must be taken from people who know the enemy's situation… The ruler must know the work of the five kinds of spy; knowing it rests on the converted spy, so the converted spy must be treated generously."

12. Seek it in 勢, made of the regular and the irregular, not from the people

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: Battle 勢 is no more than the regular (正) and the irregular (奇): engage with the regular, win with the irregular, and their combinations cannot be exhausted. The skilled fighter seeks victory in 勢 and does not demand it of his people, so he can choose his people and rely on 勢; courage and cowardice are a matter of 勢.
  • Their words: 「三軍之衆，可使必受敵而無敗者，奇正是也。」…「凡戰者，以正合，以奇勝。…戰勢不過奇正，奇正之變，不可勝窮也。奇正相生，如循環之無端，孰能窮之哉？」…「勇怯，勢也」…「故善戰者，求之於勢，不責於人，故能擇人而任勢。任勢者，其戰人也，如轉木石。木石之性，安則靜，危則動，方則止，圓則行。」 — "That the whole army can take the enemy's attack without defeat is the work of 奇 and 正… In all battle, engage with the regular and win with the irregular… The 勢 of battle are no more than irregular and regular, yet their changes cannot all be exhausted. One who relies on 勢 fights with men as one rolls logs and stones: by nature logs and stones are still on level ground and move on a slope; square, they stop; round, they roll." 勢 is defined in 始計 as 「勢者，因利而制權也」: "勢 is shaping one's leverage by advantage."

13. Not every road is taken, and not every command of the ruler is accepted

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: There are roads not to be taken, armies not to be struck, cities not to be attacked, ground not to be contested, and commands of the ruler not to be accepted. When the way of battle means sure victory and the ruler says do not fight, fighting is permissible; when it means no victory and the ruler says fight, not fighting is permissible. The general advances without seeking fame and retreats without avoiding blame, protecting only the people.
  • Their words: 「途有所不由，軍有所不擊，城有所不攻，地有所不爭，君命有所不受。」 — "There are roads one does not take, armies one does not strike, cities one does not attack, ground one does not contest, ruler's commands one does not accept." 「故戰道必勝，主曰無戰，必戰可也；戰道不勝，主曰必戰，無戰可也。是故進不求名，退不避罪，唯民是保，而利合於主，國之寶也。」 — "So when the way of battle means sure victory, though the ruler says do not fight, fighting is permissible; when the way of battle does not mean victory, though the ruler says fight, not fighting is permissible. So one who advances not seeking fame and retreats not avoiding blame, protecting only the people and whose gain accords with the ruler's, is the treasure of the state." 謀攻 names three ways the ruler brings trouble on his army: 「不知軍之不可以進而謂之進，不知軍之不可以退而謂之退，是為縻軍；不知三軍之事，而同三軍之政，則軍士惑矣；不知三軍之權，而同三軍之任，則軍士疑矣。」 — "ordering advance or retreat without knowing whether the army can, which is hobbling the army; sharing in the army's administration without knowing its affairs, which confuses the soldiers; sharing in its command without knowing its expedients, which makes them doubt." The 史記 drill story shows him acting on this: 「臣既已受命為將，將在軍，君命有所不受。」 — "Having received the command as general, while the general is with the army there are commands of the ruler he does not accept." The bamboo 〈四變〉 explains the clause. It is in English only here (Ames): "That there are commands from the ruler not to be obeyed means that if the commands of the ruler are contrary to these four contingencies, do not obey them."
  • Source: 九變第八 (l.105); 地形第十 (l.131); 謀攻第三 (l.44); 《史記》卷六十五 孫子吳起列傳 (l.13); 〈四變〉 in Ames 1993, Part II ch.2.
  • Words are: quoted (九變, 地形, 謀攻). The 史記 line is attributed (司馬遷's narrative). 〈四變〉 is attributed: Ames reads it as commentary on the core text.
  • From: top-12 #2 (the authority half, corrected); 九變, 史記 and 〈四變〉 are new (my research).

14. Choose the action by the ratio of forces

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: With ten to one, surround; five to one, attack; two to one, divide; equal, you can fight; fewer, you can hold (a variant reads "slip away"); outmatched, you can avoid. A small force that holds stubbornly is taken by a large one. Numbers are not valued for their own sake: it is enough not to advance recklessly, to concentrate strength, size up the enemy and take men, and whoever lacks forethought and slights the enemy is sure to be captured.
  • Their words: 「故用兵之法，十則圍之，五則攻之，倍則分之，敵則能戰之，少則能守之，不若則能避之。故小敵之堅，大敵之擒也。」 — "So in using troops: ten to one, surround them; five to one, attack them; double, divide them; equal, be able to fight them; fewer, be able to hold; not a match, be able to avoid them. So the stubbornness of a small force is the capture of a large one." 「故兵非貴益多也，惟無武進，足以併力、料敵、取人而已。夫惟無慮而易敵者，必擒於人。」 — "So in war more is not what is valued; only do not advance rashly; enough to combine strength, size up the enemy and take men. Only one who lacks forethought and slights the enemy is sure to be captured by others." Readings differ:
    • 十一家注 reads 「少則能逃之」.
    • On 倍則分之, 曹操 reads "divide your own force into regular and irregular" (「以二敵一，則一術爲正，一術爲奇」), 王晳 reads it as splitting into two forces so the enemy is hit front and back, and 陳皥 as sending part of the force at what the enemy must rescue, so that the enemy divides.
    • Ames 1993 (n.125) swaps the 倍 and 敵 clauses by emendation.
  • Source: 謀攻第三 (l.40); 行軍第九 (l.122); 十一家注孫子·謀攻篇 (Wikisource).
  • Words are: quoted
  • From: new (my research)

15. The general's faults: five dangers, six defeats

  • Kind: decision rule

  • Rule: Examine the general for five dangers, each a handle for the enemy:

    • one set on dying can be killed;
    • one set on living can be captured;
    • the quick-tempered can be provoked by insult;
    • the scrupulous about honour can be shamed;
    • one who loves the people can be harried.

    The six kinds of defeat (flight, slackness, sinking, collapse, disorder, rout) are not disasters from heaven but the general's faults, and the text names the cause of each.

  • Their words: 「故將有五危︰必死，可殺也﹔必生，可虜也﹔忿速，可侮也﹔廉潔，可辱也﹔愛民，可煩也。凡此五者，將之過也，用兵之災也。覆軍殺將，必以五危，不可不察也。」 — "The general has five dangers: set on dying, he can be killed; set on living, he can be captured; quick to anger, he can be insulted; scrupulous, he can be shamed; loving the people, he can be troubled. These five are the general's faults, the calamities of using troops. The overturned army and the slain general come from the five dangers; they must be examined." 「故兵有走者、有弛者、有陷者、有崩者、有亂者、有北者。凡此六者，非天之災，將之過也。夫勢均，以一擊十，曰走；卒强吏弱，曰弛；吏强卒弱，曰陷；大吏怒而不服，遇敵懟而自戰，將不知其能，曰崩；將弱不嚴，教道不明，吏卒無常，陳兵縱橫，曰亂；將不能料敵，以少合衆，以弱擊強，兵無選鋒，曰北。」 — "Armies have flight, slackness, sinking, collapse, disorder and rout. These six are not calamities from heaven but the general's faults. With 勢 equal, striking ten with one is flight; strong men and weak officers is slackness; strong officers and weak men is sinking; senior officers angry and insubordinate, fighting on their own from resentment when they meet the enemy, with the general not knowing their ability, is collapse; a weak, lax general, unclear instruction, officers and men without constancy and ranks every which way is disorder; a general who cannot size up the enemy, meets many with few and strong with weak, and has no picked vanguard, is rout."

  • Source: 九變第八 (l.111); 地形第十 (l.129).

  • Words are: quoted

  • From: top-12 #12 (the 五危 half, corrected); 六敗 is new (my research).

16. Orders clear first, then enforced; care that cannot command is useless

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: If orders are not clear and not drilled in, it is the general's fault; if they are clear and still not followed, it is the officers' fault. Punish troops before they are attached to you and they will not submit, but once they are attached, if punishment is not carried out, they cannot be used. Care without the power to direct and command makes soldiers like spoiled children: useless.
  • Their words: 「約束不明，申令不熟，將之罪也；既已明而不如法者，吏士之罪也。」 — "If the rules are not clear and the orders not made familiar, it is the general's fault; if they are already clear and are not followed as the law requires, it is the officers' fault." In the story he says the first sentence alone after the first failure, then repeats the orders before the second. The bamboo 〈見吳王〉 has the same rule. It is in English only here (Ames): "If one does not order them, or if one's orders are not understood, it is the fault of the commander. If one has already issued orders and has explained them, it is the fault of the field officers." 「卒未親附而罰之，則不服，不服則難用也。卒已親附而罰不行，則不可用也。故令之以文，齊之以武，是謂必取。」 — "Punish soldiers before they are attached and they will not submit, and if they do not submit they are hard to use. Once they are attached, if punishment is not carried out, they cannot be used. So command them with civility and keep them uniform with martial discipline: this is called sure success." 「視卒如嬰兒，故可以與之赴深溪；視卒如愛子，故可與之俱死。厚而不能使，愛而不能令，亂而不能治，譬若驕子，不可用也。」 — "Regard the soldiers as infants and they will go into deep valleys with you; regard them as beloved sons and they will die with you. Generous but unable to direct, loving but unable to command, disorderly and unable to control: they are like spoiled children, of no use."
  • Source: 《史記》卷六十五 (l.13); 〈見吳王〉 in Ames 1993, Part II ch.5; 行軍第九 (l.124); 地形第十 (l.133).
  • Words are: The 史記 and 〈見吳王〉 lines are attributed: narrated, and per Ames both versions are later than the core text. The 行軍 and 地形 lines are quoted.
  • From: new (my research; also codex "Missing" for the 行軍/地形 half)

17. Learn the enemy by probing and by signs

  • Kind: decision rule

  • Rule: Learn the enemy by acting on him:

    • reckon him (策) to know the gains and losses of his plans;
    • stir him (作) to learn the pattern of his movement and stillness;
    • make him take form (形) to know his ground of life and death;
    • test him in contact (角) to know where he has more and where less.

    Read his intent from what can be seen. Humble words with more preparation mean he will advance; strong words with a forward push mean he will withdraw; asking for peace without a pledge means a plot; half advancing and half withdrawing means a lure. The chapter lists many more signs.

  • Their words: 「故策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處。」 — "So reckon them to know the calculation of gain and loss; stir them to know the pattern of movement and stillness; give them form to know the ground of death and life; test them to know where there is surplus and shortage." 「敵近而靜者，恃其險也；遠而挑戰者，欲人之進也；…辭卑而益備者，進也；辭強而進驅者，退也；…無約而請和者，謀也；…半進半退者，誘也；杖而立者，饑也；…見利而不進者，勞也；鳥集者，虛也；夜呼者，恐也；軍擾者，將不重也；旌旗動者，亂也；吏怒者，倦也；…數賞者，窘也；數罰者，困也。」 — "Near and quiet: they rely on their strong ground. Far off and challenging: they want you to advance… Humble words and more preparation: they will advance. Strong words and pressing forward: they will withdraw… Asking for peace without a pledge: a plot… Half advancing, half withdrawing: a lure. Leaning on their weapons as they stand: hungry… Seeing advantage and not advancing: weary. Birds gathering: the camp is empty. Calling out at night: afraid. The camp in uproar: the general lacks weight. Banners moving: disorder. Officers angry: exhausted… Frequent rewards: in straits. Frequent punishments: in distress."

  • Source: 虛實第六 (l.87); 行軍第九 (l.120).

  • Words are: quoted

  • From: top-12 #11 (corrected)

18. Spirit, heart, strength and change: meet theirs with yours

  • Kind: decision rule

  • Rule: An army's spirit can be taken and a general's heart can be taken. Four management rules follow:

    • Managing spirit: avoid the enemy's spirit when it is keen, and strike when it flags and turns homeward.
    • Managing the heart: await disorder with order, and clamour with calm.
    • Managing strength: await the far-come while near, the weary while rested, the hungry while fed.
    • Managing change: do not intercept well-ordered banners or strike an imposing array.

    Eight prescriptions:

    1. Do not attack up a high hill.
    2. Do not meet head-on an enemy with a hill at its back.
    3. Do not follow a feigned flight.
    4. Do not attack crack troops.
    5. Do not take bait.
    6. Do not block an army going home.
    7. Leave a gap for a surrounded army.
    8. Do not press a desperate enemy.

  • Their words: 「三軍可奪氣，將軍可奪心。是故朝氣銳，晝氣惰，暮氣歸。故善用兵者，避其銳氣，擊其惰歸，此治氣者也；以治待亂，以靜待嘩，此治心者也；以近待遠，以佚待勞，以飽待饑，此治力者也；無邀正正之旗，無擊堂堂之陣，此治變者也。」 — "The army's spirit can be taken; the general's heart can be taken. So morning spirit is keen, midday spirit slack, evening spirit turns homeward. So the skilled user of troops avoids keen spirit and strikes the slack and homeward: this is managing spirit. With order await disorder, with stillness await clamour: this is managing the heart. Near, await the distant; at ease, await the weary; fed, await the hungry: this is managing strength. Do not intercept orderly banners, do not strike an imposing array: this is managing change." 「故用兵之法，高陵勿向，背丘勿逆，佯北勿從，銳卒勿攻，餌兵勿食，歸師勿遏，圍師必闕，窮寇勿迫，此用兵之法也。」 — "So the method of using troops: a high hill, do not face up it; a hill at their back, do not oppose; feigned flight, do not follow; crack troops, do not attack; bait troops, do not swallow; an army going home, do not block; a surrounded army, always leave a gap; a desperate enemy, do not press." The commentators read 朝/晝/暮 two ways: 杜牧 takes them as times of day, while 孟氏, 梅堯臣 and 王晳 take them as the start, middle and end of a campaign.

  • Source: 軍爭第七 (l.100, 102); 十一家注孫子·軍爭篇.

  • Words are: quoted

  • From: top-12 #9 (corrected)

19. Name the ground, act as prescribed; troops with no way out fight

  • Kind: decision rule

  • Rule: Identify the ground by the conditions the chapter defines, then act as prescribed:

    • scattered ground: do not fight;
    • light ground: do not halt;
    • contested ground: do not attack;
    • open-crossing ground: do not get cut off;
    • crossroads ground: join allies;
    • heavy ground: plunder;
    • broken ground: keep moving;
    • enclosed ground: use stratagem;
    • dead ground: fight.

    Troops deep in hostile land with nowhere to go fight to the death without being ordered. The chapter says the variations of the nine grounds and the pattern of human feelings must be examined.

  • Their words: 「是故散地則無戰，輕地則無止，爭地則無攻，交地則無絕，衢地則合交，重地則掠，圮地則行，圍地則謀，死地則戰。」 — "So on scattered ground, do not fight; on light ground, do not halt; on contested ground, do not attack; on crossing ground, do not be cut off; on crossroads ground, join in alliances; on heavy ground, plunder; on broken ground, keep going; on enclosed ground, scheme; on dead ground, fight." (Each ground is defined in l.138, e.g. 「疾戰則存，不疾戰則亡者，為死地」 — "ground where you survive if you fight fast and perish if you do not is dead ground.") 「投之無所往，死且不北。死焉不得，士人盡力。兵士甚陷則不懼，無所往則固，深入則拘，不得已則鬥。」…「投之亡地然後存，陷之死地然後生。夫衆陷於害，然後能為勝敗。」…「九地之變，屈伸之利，人情之理，不可不察也。」 — "Throw them where there is nowhere to go and they will die rather than flee; facing death, what can they not achieve? Officers and men give their full strength. Soldiers deeply trapped do not fear; with nowhere to go they hold firm; deep in, they cling together; with no choice, they fight… Throw them into ground where they would perish, and they survive; sink them into dead ground, and they live. Only when the mass has fallen into harm can it decide victory or defeat… The variations of the nine grounds, the advantage of drawing in or extending, the pattern of human feelings: these must be examined." The same chapter gives a second list for each ground of what the commander himself does (「散地，吾將一其志…死地，吾將示之以不活」, l.148).

  • Source: 九地第十一 (l.138, 142, 146, 148, 150).

  • Words are: quoted

  • From: top-12 #10 (corrected)

20. Which house falls first: judge by the land unit, the tax, and what they breed

  • Kind: decision rule
  • Rule: The King of Wu asked which of the six houses dividing Jin would fall first. Sunzi named Fan and Zhonghang first, then Zhi, then Han and Wei, while Zhao, keeping its old laws, would hold and Jin would go to it. His reason, given house by house (Ames's translation), had two sides:
    • The houses that fell: small land units plus the tax gave full coffers and many officials, so the ruler grew arrogant and the ministers wasteful, and they fought often for exploits.
    • Zhao: the largest unit and no new taxes gave empty coffers and few officials, a frugal ruler and humble ministers over a prosperous people, so it held.
  • Their words: 「吴王问孙子曰："六将军分守晋国之地，孰先亡、孰固成？"孙子曰："范、中行是(氏)先亡。""孰为之次？""智是(氏)为次。""孰为之次？""韩魏为次，赵毋失其故法，晋国归焉。"」 (simplified characters, as printed by 何炳棣) — "The King of Wu asked Master Sun: 'Of the six commanders who divided and held the land of Jin, which will perish first, and which will hold firm?' Master Sun said: 'Fan and Zhonghang will perish first.' 'Who next?' 'Zhi next.' 'Who next?' 'Han and Wei next. Zhao has not lost its old laws, and the state of Jin will go to it.'" The reasoning is in English only here (Ames): "Fan and Chung-hang took eighty square paces as a yuan, and took one hundred and sixty square paces as a chen, and then made five households their basic tax unit. The land area was small and the officials in office were many. With five households as the basic tax unit, the public coffers prospered. With the public coffers prospering and the officials in office many, the ruler became arrogant and his ministers wasteful. And in pursuit of great exploits they embarked on frequent wars. Thus I say they were the first to perish… Chao took one hundred and twenty square paces as a yuan, and took two hundred and forty square paces as a chen, and so there were no new taxes forthcoming for the public coffers. With the public coffers empty and the officials in office few, the ruler was frugal and his ministers humble in their management of what was a prosperous people. Thus I say that he held on to his lands." The closing line is the king's, not Sunzi's: 「王者之道明矣，厚爱其民者也」 — "The way of the true king is clear: it is to love his people generously."
  • Source: 銀雀山漢簡〈吳問〉. Chinese as quoted in 何炳棣〈中國現存最古的私家著述《孫子兵法》〉 (https://www.aisixiang.com/data/101665.html). English from Ames 1993, Part II ch.1 and n.230. Ames reads 伍稅 as "five households as the tax unit"; 何炳棣, in his own words rather than the slips', describes Fan and Zhonghang as 「亩最小，税最重」 (smallest unit, heaviest tax).
  • Words are: attributed (a narrated dialogue in the bamboo text)
  • From: new (my research). The identity research named 〈吳問〉 as the place to look; the top 12 could not source it.
