# 老子 (Laozi) — learn the mistake inside a success, not from total failure

## Answer

The principle holds for this harness **only in its joinable form, and R12.7 as drafted has no join**: the mistake inside a success is already written under R12.2, but as a separate record with no link to the step that would have taught the next child — so the harness would hold the lesson and never read it next to the result. Fix that, require the named measure to be able to show a difference at all, and the principle holds; if instead the measure is pass/fail, it cannot hold, because a binary outcome has no hill to climb and "2% better" is a number with nothing under it. Before that, take something away (my #1: 損之又損，以至於無為 — "Lose and lose again, until one arrives at not-doing", `quoted`).

## How I decided

1. **The principle as EJ remembers it is true, but weaker than it sounds (my reading, through my #17).** My #17 is 故有之以為利，無之以為用 — "what is there makes for benefit; what is not there makes for use" (`quoted`). A path record as R12.7 specifies it is mostly *what is there*: steps, engines, checks, measures. Its empty part — the check that failed once and passed on retry, the call that errored and was worked around, the guard that did not fire — is where the use is, and R12.7 does not ask for it. The same finding appears in the GA literature: partial fitness in the zone around a good solution (Hinton & Nowlan 1987, "constructing a large zone of increased fitness around the good net", F2, `verified`, https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/evolution.htm); a reward that only says all-correct or all-wrong gives zero advantage and zero gradient, which is why DAPO filters out prompts with accuracy 1 *and* 0 (Yu et al., F4, `verified`, https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476). The signal is not "success" and not "failure" — it is **outcomes that differ**. F3 makes the same claim for GAs but is tagged `unproven` (Wikipedia, marked [citation needed]), so I do not rest anything on it.

2. **Where it holds here, concretely.** The only lesson in today's measured data sits in exactly that shape (my reading of the brief's Facts). One agent, after a fake engine returned nothing, called the real engine binary instead. Every check logged `ok`; by R12.2 nothing failed; by R12.7 that is a success with a clean path and nothing to learn. Under R12.2 it is also not a failure, because the result passed. So the harness currently has **no place to put "the right answer obtained by the wrong road"** — the empty slot is the wrong road (my #17). That is precisely "the mistake inside a success", and R12.7 as written loses it.

3. **Where it breaks, concretely.** A child borrowing a parent path gets a recipe with no information about why it worked: R12.2's record carries no run id, no step id and no parent link, and R12.7's "every child tries to avoid the failures recorded in its parent's **insights**" routes through the insight file, which is grouped **by cause** (R12.3) and written at end of task. So the near-miss is recorded, grouped, and filed away from the step that stands next to the success. My #12 calls this 微明 — subtle clarity (`quoted`) — and the faint thing is exactly what is not joined.

4. **"Not from total failure" is wrong as a blanket rule (my reading, against the principle).** Failures teach when they are turned into a contrast or a relabelled success: STaR's rationalization raised CommonsenseQA 68.8% → 72.5% (F41, `verified`, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14465) **but did not substantially help on GSM8K, 10.1% vs 10.7%** (F42, `verified`, same) — task-dependent, not universal. NSR trains only on wrong answers and improves over the base model across Pass@k (F43, `reported`), yet on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct all RL methods including NSR underperformed the base model (F44, `reported`), and its advantage seems tied to a strong prior (F45, `unproven`). Reflexion stores verbal reflection from task feedback and reports 91% pass@1 on HumanEval (F47, `reported`). So the honest form is: **a total failure teaches least when it has no successful sibling and no relabelled goal**; R12.3's six whys is closest to Reflexion, which is fine, but it is not the whole of what is available.

5. **What the harness already is, and the dial it lacks (my reading).** Borrow a path, change one thing, keep it if better, never lose the best — that is exactly (1+1)-ES elitist selection (Glasmachers, F17, `verified`, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.02887: "the offspring must perform at least as good as the parent"). On the size of the change, the literature's answer is the 1/5 rule — keep the share of successful offspring stable, one in five leaves the step unchanged (F19, F20, `verified`, same paper) — **but I propose no number from it**: the same source and pypop (F21, `unproven`, https://pypop.readthedocs.io/en/latest/es/res.html) say the fixed 1/5 constant has been "mostly superseded"; treat it as a direction (change more if almost every child beats its parent, less if almost none), never as a threshold. My #18 agrees on timing: 良賈深藏若虛 — the good merchant stores deep as if empty (`attributed`) — do not set a rate before there is a population to count.

6. **Proposed changes, ranked. Each adds nothing until step 7 is read as a subtraction.**

   **7. (Subtract — do this first.) R12.7: drop the one-line global success log; move the first-pass result into the path step.**
   > Every step of a path records its check and whether that check passed on the first pass. The separate one-line log of every successful check is dropped; the path record is where a check's result lives, and the steps of a path are how often that check ran.
   *Check that can fail:* plant a run whose steps claim a check never ran, and whose deleted one-line log would have shown it ran. If the path still stands and no report notices, the denominator was only ever in the one-line log and the subtraction lost it.
   *Price:* you can no longer count runs of a check that left no path. My #16 — 自勝者強, "overcoming himself is strength" (`quoted`) — is why I say so out loud rather than quietly dropping it.

   **8. (The join — the change that decides whether the principle holds.) Add to R12.7: the cost of each step.**
   > *The empty part of the path.* Each step names what that step cost, by reference to the failure record ids it produced under R12.2: a check that failed once and passed on a retry, a call that errored and was worked around, an edit that had to be undone, and anything the guard did not catch that the agent did by hand. References, not copies. A path whose steps carry none of these is marked *clean*, and is marked as a path with nothing to teach.
   *Check that can fail:* take a run that has at least one R12.2 record, delete the per-step references from its path, and run the borrow step for a similar task. If the child is produced and its great-success insight comes out unchanged, the field is decorative and the rule has the sentence without the learning.
   *Cost, named:* an agent that is rewarded for writing this field can turn the path into a manual for evading the guard. My #4 — 為者敗之，執者失之, "one who acts on it spoils it; one who holds on to it loses it" (`quoted`) — is the reason this must be reviewed by EJ and never by the agent that wrote it (R12.3 already puts EJ between proposal and fix).

   **9. (A measure that can show a difference.) Add to R12.7.**
   > *The measure must be able to show a difference.* The measure named before the run is one on which two runs of the same work are known to differ — a count, a time, a number of retries, a number a previous run reported. If it can only say pass or fail, the run records its value without a comparison and the report says so in those words; the 2% aim is then not written at all.
   *Check that can fail:* plant a design naming a boolean measure ("the suite passes") and confirm the pre-run note cannot state a parent's value on it; plant a count ("number of retries") and confirm it can. If the boolean is accepted, the check fails.
   *Price:* some tasks will run with no comparison. My #2 — 知足不辱，知止不殆，可以長久, "knowing what is enough, one is not disgraced; knowing to stop, one is not endangered" (`quoted`) — says a run with no honest comparison is better than one with a fake number.

   **10. (Mourn the victory, do not praise it.) Add to R12.7's great-success insight.**
   > The insight for a great success does not describe why the path was good. It names **one thing about the path that nothing checked**, and one to three options to make that hole visible to the next child — a check that would have caught it, or the step written down so the next run can see it. An insight with no such hole is not accepted, exactly as a failure insight with no changeable source is not accepted.
   *Check that can fail:* feed the rule two planted great successes — one with a recorded retry or bypass, one genuinely clean — and it must accept one and reject the other. If it accepts both (the hole clause is soft) or rejects both (it demands a cause that does not exist), the check fails.
   *Why:* my #13 — 勝而不美，而美之者，是樂殺人 … 戰勝，以喪禮處之, "he wins and does not find it beautiful; one who finds it beautiful delights in killing people … a victory in war is handled with the rites of mourning" (`quoted`). A trigger judged after the fact ("beats the previous run", "EJ calls it great") will otherwise produce a rationalisation, and my #14 — 不自見故明, "not displaying himself, he is clear" (`quoted`) — says a display is not an explanation.

   **11. (For R12.3, one change.) The end is held to the same standard as the beginning.**
   > *The end is held to the beginning.* The six whys and the fix options are written while the run is still open — at the moment of the failure — and the final report only assembles what is already written. A task whose whys never reached a changeable source is recorded as ending open, and the report says so.
   *Check that can fail:* plant a session where the failure's evidence file is gone by report time. The check must catch that the whys were reconstructed after the fact and record the task as open, not complete.
   *Why:* my #7 — 民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。慎終如始，則無敗事, "people in their undertakings often fail when they are close to finishing; careful at the end as at the beginning, then no undertaking fails" (`quoted`). The harness guards every moment and inspects one — the last. My #6 — 輕諾必寡信, "light promises must bring little trust" (`quoted`) — is why I will not promise this is cheap.

   **12. (Deferred, and the most expensive: more than one parent per category.)** Keep the best path **and** at least one other, chosen for being different rather than second-best; a child may borrow from either and writes which before the run. Literature: high selection pressure under-explores (F11, `reported`); MAP-Elites keeps the highest performer per behaviour cell, and its authors note elites often descend from non-neighbouring cells (F30, F31, `verified`/`reported`, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1504.04909); deception means the best path can lead to a dead end (Lehman & Stanley 2011, F28, `verified`, https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Lehman_2011_EC.html); lexicase keeps specialists that fail overall but pass a rare case (F33, `verified`). My #19 fits it best — 人皆取實，己獨取虛, "everyone takes the solid; he alone takes the empty" (`attributed`) — and my #11's 正言若反, "straight words seem reversed" (`quoted`), says the thing that looks like junk is the archive's value. **I still defer it**: it contradicts "a child that improves becomes a parent" as written, it doubles what EJ must read, and it needs the measure from step 9 to be trustworthy first.

   **13. (On the guard, from today's measurement — my #5.)** 我無為而民自化, "I do not act and the people transform themselves" (`quoted`). The brief says hooks alone taught the same behaviour as written rules. That argues against adding a second rule to R12.3 to make agents do step 11, and for the guard carrying it: a stop is logged when a whys is not written. Whether it is enough is a separate question I cannot answer from n=3.

## What I need to know

1. Is the measure named before the run usually graded, or usually a check that passes or fails? Step 9 stands or falls on this and the brief does not say.
2. Does anything read the insight files after the session — a hook, EJ, or a later agent? My #17 says the empty part is the use; if nothing reads the file, the honest conclusion is that the file is a record, not a mechanism, and step 8's cost buys nothing.
3. Does the guard record a retry and a worked-around failure today, or only what surfaces as a log line? If retries are already stored keyed by run, step 8 is a pointer, not a new field.
4. Across the runs recorded so far, how many success paths contain at least one non-success entry? If none, today's tasks are too easy for this loop (Florensa et al., F8, `verified`: goals reachable with probability 0.1–0.9 only) and the measurement cannot yet judge the principle either way.
5. My #8 — 知不知上，不知知病, "knowing not-knowing: highest; not-knowing knowing: sickness" (`quoted`) — applies to the principle itself: no source uses EJ's phrasing, and which of the four candidate concepts EJ learned is unknown. If EJ's memory is of (1+1)-ES rather than of shaped fitness, steps 8 and 9 are the wrong two changes.

## Costs and risks

- **R12.7 is already nine clauses long; I have added four.** My #14 — 少則得，多則惑, "less, it is gained; more, it is confused" (`quoted`) — is why steps 7–8 are one subtraction and one pointer, and why step 12 is deferred rather than appended. If EJ adds all of it, I have argued for my own failure.
- **Dropping the one-line success log loses a denominator** for checks that appear in no path (my #16).
- **The cost-of-step field can be gamed into a loophole catalogue** and can give an agent a map of what the guard misses (my #4; my #9 — 欲先民，必以身後之, "wanting to be ahead of the people, one must in person put oneself behind them" (`quoted`) — is why the review sits with EJ).
- **Tasks will end "open" more often** once step 11 is in force, so the failure count rises while the learning rises. Read my #10's 慈故能勇,儉故能廣 — "compassionate, one is able to be brave; frugal, one is able to be broad" (`quoted`) — as the reason for that trade: a harness that punishes the report gets a silent one.
- **Step 10 makes the great-success file duller.** EJ may find it slower to write and less satisfying to read; that is the price of 戰勝以喪禮處之 (my #13).
- **The whole proposal rests on n=3, one task, one engine (claude-haiku).** My #18: 不得其時則蓬累而行, "when one does not get one's time, one drifts off" (`attributed`). Nothing here is ready to be a MUST. I would hold steps 8–11 as draft text until the checks below have run, and I would not invent a run-count for when.
- **What I could not verify.** I did no fetching in this seat; every external claim above is cited from `research/ga-findings.md` with the researcher's own label, and the primary gaps stand — HER's relabeling body text unread, Goldberg & Deb (1991) unread, Jastrebski & Arnold (2006) not obtained, F3/F21/F45 `unproven`, F1/F2 dated by year only. The four sources I lean on hardest (F4, F17, F28, F41/F42) were read from primary pages.

## How we would know I was right

1. **Join test (step 8's planted failure).** Strip the per-step references from a run's path record and borrow as normal. If nothing downstream changes, the harness holds the rule and not the learning — my reading is wrong.
2. **Spread test (step 9).** Over the recorded runs, count the named measures that could show a difference between two runs of the same task. If most cannot, the success loop has no hill (F4) and R12.7 measures nothing, whatever it says.
3. **Clean-path count.** Count success paths with at least one non-success entry. If the count is zero, the conclusion is not "the rule is wrong" but "these tasks are too easy to teach it" (F8) — and the honest move is my #18, to let it sit rather than force it.
4. **The test that can make me wrong.** If, after enough runs, children that borrowed a path *with* a recorded near-miss repeat that near-miss no less often than children that borrowed a *clean* path, then step 8 is not worth its price and the whole proposal should be dropped rather than repaired. The trigger is that comparison, not a count I would have to invent.
