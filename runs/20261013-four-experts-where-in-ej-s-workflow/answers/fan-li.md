## How I decided

1. **At 3.1 Readiness and the blueprint check:** The method requires proving tools, checking information, and settling blueprint requirements before building. Goujian asked what makes it possible to fight; my answer in my #18 was: 「審備則可以戰乎？」 — "If the preparations are examined, can we then fight?" (`quoted`). Examining defensive preparations to ensure they cannot be penetrated is the prerequisite to dispatching any campaign.
2. In the same step (3.1), my #1 states the dual-condition gate: 「夫聖人隨時以行，是謂守時。天時不作，弗為人客；人事不起，弗為之始。」 — "The sage moves with the time; this is called keeping to the time. When Heaven's time has not arisen, do not be the guest [the attacker]; when human affairs have not arisen, do not be the one who begins." (`quoted`). Furthermore, 「夫人事必將與天地相參，然後乃可以成功。」 — "Human affairs must be joined with Heaven and Earth as a three; only then can success be achieved." (`quoted`). When tool readiness (Heaven) is present but EJ's requirements (human affairs) are unconfirmed, or vice versa, my rule tells the king: 「王姑待之」 — "For now, wait." (`quoted`).
3. For incomplete or draft blueprint sections in 3.1, my #9 warns against acting on unformed signals: 「逆節萌生。天地未形，而先為之征，其事是以不成，雜受其刑。王姑待之。」 — "Rebellion against order is sprouting. Heaven and Earth have not yet taken visible form, and to campaign ahead of it — that is why the affair does not succeed, and all together suffer the harm. For now, wait." (`quoted`). Campaigning before requirements take visible form causes shared failure. My #3 reinforces this: 「時不至，不可彊生；事不究，不可彊成。」 — "When the time has not come, growth cannot be forced; when the matter has not run its course, completion cannot be forced." (`quoted`).
4. **At 3.6 The knowledge graph, contract fidelity, and the stop condition:** During node execution, workers frequently uncover edge cases, refactoring ideas, or temptations to change scope. My #5 strictly prohibits mid-flight contract drift: 「夫謀之廊廟，失之中原，其可乎？王姑勿許也。…贏縮轉化，後將悔之。天節固然，唯謀不遷。」 — "To plan it in the temple hall and lose it on the central plain — can that be right? For now, do not accept. … Advance and retreat change into each other; later you would regret it. Heaven's rhythm is like that; only the plan does not move." (`quoted`). And: 「夫十年謀之，一朝而棄之，其可乎？」 — "Ten years of planning, thrown away in one morning — can that be right?" (`quoted`). The plan agreed in council must govern the node; any out-of-scope discoveries must go to `docs/blueprint/backlog.md` rather than mutating the active run.
5. In closing the run at 3.6, my #6 governs the stop: 「今事已濟矣，蠡請從會稽之罰。」 — "Now the task is done; I ask to receive the punishment of 會稽." (`quoted`), and 「蜚鳥盡，良弓藏；狡兔死，走狗烹。」 — "When the flying birds are gone, the good bow is put away; when the cunning hare is dead, the hunting dog is cooked." (`quoted`). Once the acceptance criteria are met, the agents must be retired and the run ended immediately; they must not linger to invent extra unasked work.
6. **At 3.5 Clear pieces run as loops:** Bounding attempts is essential so a loop does not spin endlessly. In my #10, the ancient masters took advance and retreat as their constant and 「無過天極，究數而止」 — "they did not pass Heaven's limit, and stopped when the count was exhausted." (`quoted`). When the attempt limit is hit, the worker stops and escalates rather than looping forever. When the objective check does pass, my #4 commands immediate closure without slackening: 「得時無怠，時不再來，天予不取，反為之災。」 — "Having got the time, do not slacken; the time does not come again; what Heaven gives and you do not take turns into disaster." (`quoted`).
7. **Where I am NOT reliable (my reading applied to my #14):** In 3.2 (Write the algorithm) and detailed domain/schema modeling, my #14 states my boundary explicitly: 「四封之內，百姓之事，蠡不如種也。四封之外，敵國之制，立斷之事，種亦不如蠡也。」 — "Inside the four borders, the people's affairs: I am not as good as 種. Outside the four borders, dealing with enemy states, matters decided on the spot: 種 is not as good as I." (`quoted`). Domestic administration, detailed internal state mechanics, and code algorithms belong inside the borders; 文種 (Wen Zhong) handles those, not I. My rules are strategic decision rules and phase judgments, not procedural step-by-step algorithms.

3. **Unreliability in micro-step algorithm design (my #14):** Asking me to write the fine-grained algorithmic steps inside a piece violates my #14 (「四封之內，百姓之事，蠡不如種也」). My list contains no algorithmic steps or data structures; forcing me to write code procedures would produce invented advice.
4. **Unreliability in workload sizing (3.4):** My list has no rules for estimating LLM token counts, context window limits, or wall-clock timeouts. Sizing prompts from my statecraft rules would be an invented calculation (my reading).
