# 孫子 (Sun Tzu) — will an agent follow this memory design?

## Answer

**An agent will not follow this design as it stands**, and by my #16 the fault is the design's, not the agent's: the YAML tells a reader that a clause's parent "text governs" but stores no parent text, most judged records say `check: reviewer` with `who_decides: none` while the header says the project owner names the reviewer, and `K4.1` and `K1.1` each demand something the design's own `K4.3` forbids. **If EJ will let the project owner be the default decider and the reviewer-of-record**, then the fix is structural — store or drop the parent text, give `retired` its own rule, spell out the supersession step, define what a failed MUST stops — and the tool-checkable MUSTs become followable; **if not**, leave `W3.6` and `Rd3.1a` unassigned, and the design must say plainly that every `check: reviewer` record and the `Rd3.1a` MUST are then unverifiable.

## How I decided

**1. I fixed what "follow it" can mean here (my #16, my #17).**
My #16, `quoted`: *"If the rules are not clear and the orders not made familiar, it is the general's fault; if they are already clear and are not followed as the law requires, it is the officers' fault."* The given Facts say the test of whether agents follow the YAML better than the Markdown was **skipped** and is *not measured*. So the honest first answer is: whether an agent follows it is **not measured**; what I can do is read the text for the places a follower would break. My #17, `quoted`: *"So reckon them to know the calculation of gain and loss; stir them to know the pattern of movement and stillness; give them form to know the ground of death and life; test them to know where there is surplus and shortage."* The build script reckons shape only; there has been no *作* (stirring) and no *角* (contact test). Everything below is my reading of the two files, checked against an item where one applies.

**2. Q1 — what would make an agent misread.**
- **A1 (my reading; level/links).** `part_of` names W1.1, W1.2, W1.3, W2.1, W2.2, W3.3, W3.5, W4.1, W5.3, W6.1, W6.3, K6.1, K6.3, Rd2.3, Rd3.1, Rd4.5 — **none of these parent records exists in the file**, and `how_to_read` says *"When a clause and its requirement seem to differ, text governs."* There is no parent text to govern. An agent looking for the requirement reads only the clause.
- **A2 (my reading; one thing).** Three counts: the prose says **46 requirements**, `how_to_read` says *"Each record is one requirement, or one clause of one"*, and the file holds **64 records**. No count is in the YAML.
- **A3 (my reading; who decides).** `who_decides` is not one vocabulary: bare `none`, bare `project owner`, and three free sentences — `unassigned: whether a procedure can be a script`, `unassigned: which agents work on the project, and so the strictest limit`, `the agent doing the read: what is worth keeping`, `the project's workflow: when work goes the other way`. `how_to_read` defines the bare token `unassigned`; the records carry `unassigned: <question>`, so an exact match fails.
- **A4 (my reading; links).** The prose cites ids that are not records — `W1.2`, `W5.3`, `K6.3`, `W6.3`, `Rd2.3`, `Rd3`, `Rd4`, `Rd4.5` — mixed with real ones (`K4.3`, `Rd5.3`). An agent that resolves `W1.2` in the YAML finds only `W1.2a`/`W1.2b`.
- **A5 (my reading; unit/basis).** `basis` is group-only, and the header never says so; `v` is bare in some groups (`'v; d: What it is for'`) and sectioned in others (`'v: nothing lost; ...'`). A record carries no basis at all.
- **A6 (my reading).** `check: tool; git` needs an undefined `;` split; `checks:` documents each label alone, never the composition, and `none` doubles as "guidance" and as a field value.

**3. Q1 — what makes records contradict each other.**
- **B1 (my reading; who decides).** `checks.reviewer` says *"The project owner names who reviews."* Every reviewer record says `who_decides: none`, which `how_to_read` defines as *"the record leaves no choice open."* A record-level reader concludes the choice is closed; a header-level reader concludes it is open. Nobody is named anywhere.
- **B2 (my reading).** `roles:` says the project owner decides *"the open choices named in who_decides"* — yet `W3.6` and `Rd3.1a` say `unassigned`. Direct contradiction; the same one question 4 is about.
- **B3 (my reading; one thing).** `K4.1` bundles both statuses under one required `superseded_by` link; a `retired` entry has nothing replacing it. See Q5a.
- **B4 (my reading; unit/weak words).** `K1.1`'s `fails_if` says *"no change to that entry"*; `K4.3` says the claim is never rewritten and the change is a **new** entry. A literal follower edits the old claim — precisely what `K4.3` forbids. See Q5b.
- **B5 (my reading; level).** `W1.2a` (MUST) still carries the sentence *"Each field is one `key: value` line"*, which the same split moved to `W1.2b` (SHOULD). The MUST fails only on the `---` lines, so a follower cannot tell which level the sentence is.
- **B6 (my reading).** `K5.1` says journals are *"appended to and never edited"* and fails a file *"edited after it is written"* — every append is an edit after writing.
- **B7 (my reading; one thing/links).** `W6.3b` and `K6.3b` are the same rule twice, in two groups; `W6.3b` defers with `fails_if: as K6.3b`; `K6.3b`'s `links` contains `K6.3b`, itself.
- **B8 (my reading; can it fail).** `Rd4.1` is MUST, but it depends on `covers`, which `W1.2b` makes SHOULD (skippable with a reason). A compliant entry may have no `covers`, and the MUST is then unsatisfiable.
- **B9 (my reading).** `W3.2` is MUST, `check: secret scanner; reviewer`, and the Facts say **no secret scanner exists**; `checks:` never names one.

**4. Q1 — what would make an agent skip.**
- **C1 (my #2, `quoted`: *"Do not rely on their not coming; rely on having the means to meet them... Do not rely on their not attacking; rely on having what cannot be attacked."*).** The design relies on the reviewer appearing; no structure produces them. My #12, `quoted`: *"the skilled fighter seeks it in 勢 and does not demand it of people; so he can choose people and rely on 勢."* The reviewer count is `責於人`, not 勢.
- **C2 (my #2).** *"with no written reason"* is the escape on **every** SHOULD, and the design never says where the reason is written or how a later reader finds it. Any SHOULD can be passed on an unrecorded word.
- **C3 (my reading).** `Rd2.1` itself records that *"Agents skip optional reads"*, and the session-start reads are SHOULD; the fallback is one sentence in `AGENTS.md`. A session can do all its work and meet only the tool MUSTs, which check shape, not meaning.
- **C4 (my #7).** My #7's method is to act on the condition, and it names that the form can be produced: *"These are the strategist's victories; they cannot be handed down in advance"* (`quoted`). A `**Why:**` line and a claim are signs; the tool checks the signs, so an agent learns to satisfy the sign. The design should not read a passing tool as a compliant entry.

**5. Q2 — the wording the COO wrote, item by item.**
- **`fails_if` for W5.2 — change.** Keep the field; *"what it learned unwritten"* has no unit. Anchor it to the `K5.2` journal-read log or the task's end state (my #16, my reading).
- **`fails_if` for W6.1a — keep.** Observable in git and matches the text.
- **`fails_if` for K5.1 — change.** *"a journal file is edited after it is written"* contradicts *"appended to"* (B6). Say *"a journal file's earlier lines are changed or removed."*
- **`fails_if` for Rd1.1 — keep.** Layout is observable; just note `.cache/` is optional and gitignored.
- **`fails_if` for Rd5.2 — keep.** Observable by report.
- **"with no written reason" on every SHOULD — keep the phrase, change the design.** Name where the reason goes and make it findable from the change; otherwise the default is unenforceable (my #2).
- **W1.2 split — keep the split, change both halves.** Remove the layout sentence from `W1.2a` (B5), and in `W1.2b` name the keys other MUSTs already require (`id`, `kind`, `status`) so SHOULD is not read as "keys optional."
- **W1.1b "No two entries share an id" — keep**; the tool needs it. **Rd5.3 naming Rd5.4's two cases — keep**; it prevents false mismatches. **Rd2.3b letting a link-reached superseded entry pass — keep**; it matches the prose.
- **`who_decides` assignments — keep the project owner on W4.1c, W5.3b, W6.3a, Rd5.3 and the agent on K5.2; change K1.1.** `the project's workflow` is not in `roles:`, and `who_decides` must name a decider, not a process. **Change the `unassigned: <question>` format** to the bare token `unassigned` with the question in `text`, or a tool can never match it.
- **Header keys — keep `what`, `levels`, `checks`, `roles`, `basis`; change `how_to_read`** (drop or implement *"text governs"*; fix the count); **change `retired_ids`** from a sentence to an id list, or move it to a comment — K2/K3 are group numbers, not records; **add `categories`**, which is in the file and not in the list.

**6. Q3 — who reviews the judged checks (my #11, my #16).**
My #11, `quoted`: *"Foreknowledge cannot be taken from ghosts and spirits, cannot be got by analogy with events, cannot be verified by measurement; it must be taken from people who know the enemy's situation."* The reviewer is the *人* here; a tool cannot be it. My recommendation: add a **`reviewer`** entry to `roles:` — *"the person or agent the project owner names to judge `check: reviewer` records for this project"* — and make the owner name them **once**, in `AGENTS.md` or a `D-` entry, both of which are read. Then a reviewer record's `who_decides` is `none` only when no judgement is open, and `project owner` when the naming itself is the open choice. If EJ will not name a default, the design should say so and mark those records unenforceable — not leave the header claiming an owner who does not exist (my #12: fix the 勢, not the agent).

**7. Q4 — the two open `who_decides` (my #1, my #4, my #11, my #14).**
My #1, `quoted`: *"Ground gives rise to measurement, measurement to quantity, quantity to number, number to weighing, weighing to victory."* **Rd3.1a splits cleanly by that chain**: *which agents work on the project* is a choice — assign it to the **project owner**; *the strictest limit* is a measurement — the tool derives it from the named agents plus `EXECUTOR_KINDS`, so it is never a `who_decides` at all. **W3.6** (*whether a procedure can be a script*) is a project-level call about where a thing lives; the roles header already gives such calls to the **project owner**, so assign it there. My #4, `quoted`: *"one who knows when he can fight and when he cannot wins"* — an `unassigned` MUST is one that cannot be fought or won. My #14, `quoted`: *"in war more is not what is valued; only do not advance rashly; enough to combine strength, size up the enemy and take men."* Do not add a new role to settle one decision; use the role the design already has, and only if EJ agrees.

**8. Q5a — may an entry be retired with nothing replacing it (my #5, my #15).**
**Yes, and `K4.1` must say so.** As written, `retired` and `superseded` are indistinguishable: both are non-active, both are never loaded except by link, both need a link — two names for one state (one thing), and a writer with nothing to link must invent one. My #5, `quoted`: *"do not move without advantage, do not use troops without gain, do not fight unless in danger"* — inventing a successor to satisfy a link is a move with no gain. **Change `K4.1`** so `superseded` requires `superseded_by`, and `retired` requires a written reason and no link; change the prose line the same way. **Cost:** a reader who lands on a retired entry has no successor, so the old body can be misread as live; the mitigation is that `status: retired` is in the header and `Rd2.3b` does not load it. If EJ wants no such case, the simpler fix is to **drop `retired`** and keep one status.

**9. Q5b — is the supersession step clear enough (my #16, my #4).**
**No.** `K1.1` says *"change the entry first"*, which the follower reads as *edit the decision*; `K4.3` says the claim is never rewritten and the change is a **new** entry. My #16 says unclear orders are the general's fault. **Change `K1.1`'s text and `fails_if`** to name both paths: if the decision stands, no entry change is owed; if the decision changes, write the superseding entry (`K4.3`) and then the code, and the failure is *no new or superseding entry in the same commit or an earlier one*. **Cost:** the wording is longer and repeats `K4.3`; the alternative is the one violation the design most needs to prevent.

**10. Q5c — what stops when a MUST fails (my #13, my #18, my #19, my #6).**
`levels` says *"the work it governs stops"*; that phrase is the whole question and is undefined. **Recommend: the part the record governs, not the whole task** — for a tool-checked record, the commit or merge it blocks; for the rest, the named work the record's `covers`/text points at. My #13, `quoted`: *"There are roads one does not take, armies one does not strike, cities one does not attack, ground one does not contest, ruler's commands one does not accept"* — the reader's standing instruction to carry on is a command not to accept where accepting it destroys the mission. My #18, `quoted`: *"a surrounded army, always leave a gap."* My #19, `quoted`: *"Throw them into ground where they would perish, and they survive; sink them into dead ground, and they live."* A follower with no way out does not stop cleanly; it routes around the design or writes a reason that is not true. My #6, `quoted`: *"Whoever does not fully know the harm of using troops cannot fully know its benefit."* Define the harm the level does, in `levels` and `how_to_read`. **Cost:** a local stop can let a real problem sit while other work continues; the mitigation is to require the failure be reported (as `Rd5.3` already requires for mismatches) and to keep the repo-wide tool gates, whose governed work *is* the commit.

**11. What to do first (my #3, my #10, my #20).** My #3, `quoted`: *"the highest use of troops strikes plans, the next strikes alliances, the next strikes troops, the lowest attacks walled cities."* Fix the plan — the YAML's structure — not each agent. My #10, `quoted`: *"an army in its victory avoids the solid and strikes the empty"* — the tool already covers shape; the empty ground is the named reviewer, the parent text, and the MUST's scope. My #20, `quoted` (`attributed`): *"Zhao has not lost its old laws, and the state of Jin will go to it."* Keep the old laws: the existing `docs/`, `tools/memory_index.py`, and the requirement-check criteria. Do not add rows for every defect; the design's own five dangers are that a design which loves completeness and scruples can be shamed into more rules (my #15).

## What I need to know

1. Does EJ accept the **project owner** as the default decider and the reviewer-of-record, or must a different role be named? (This decides Q3 and Q4.)
2. Is `retired` meant to be distinct from `superseded`? If not, drop it. (Q5a.)
3. When a MUST fails, does EJ intend the **whole task** to stop, or only the governed work? The file now says neither. (Q5c.)
4. Is a secret scanner available in the target projects, or should `W3.2` drop that check label and rely on `reviewer`? (Q2.)
5. Where is a SHOULD's **written reason** recorded so a later reader finds it — the `changed:` line, the commit message, or `state.md`? (Q2.)
6. Should the parent requirement text (`W1.1`, `W1.2`, …) be stored in the YAML, or is `part_of` only a grouping label? (Q1, A1.) If it is only a label, `how_to_read`'s *"text governs"* line must go.

## Costs and risks

- Naming one default reviewer makes the project owner a bottleneck and invites rubber-stamping; that is the half-victory of knowing your own side but not the judged one (**my #4**, **my #16**).
- Storing parent text adds a second source that can drift from the clauses, against speed and against the single copy (**my #8**, **my #6**).
- Allowing `retired` with no link leaves the reader no successor and lets the old body be misread as live (**my #13**, **my #6**).
- Scoping a failed MUST to the governed work can leave a real problem standing while work continues; the gap must be paired with a required report (**my #18**, **my #6**).
- Fixing the YAML without probing leaves *"will an agent follow it"* still unmeasured — the shape can pass while the meaning fails (**my #17**, **my #7**).
- Naming the owner on `W3.6` and `Rd3.1a` is a decision I am recommending, not one I can make; if EJ does not make it, those records should stay `unassigned` and the design should say the MUST is unverifiable (**my #13**, house rule: never fill a missing value with an invented one).

## How we would know I was right

Run the probe that was skipped, with the two signs named: give **2 agent engines × 3 tasks** a fresh clone and a task id under this memory, and count — not judge — (a) how often the agent cites a level that matches the record, (b) how often it finds and names a reviewer for a `check: reviewer` record, (c) how often it edits an entry's claim instead of superseding it (`git` shows this), (d) how often it skips a session-start read. My #17 is the test: *reckon, stir, give form, test in contact.* The measure is the count against the current text; if the counts do not drop after the structural changes above, I was wrong. One check I would write is a script that fails when any `check: reviewer` record's `who_decides` does not resolve to a name in `roles:`, and when `tools/memory_index.py --check` passes an entry whose claim changed after first commit. **Name who writes that script** — the project owner or the COO, not me — and **name what it does not establish**: it shows the fields resolve and the claim is unchanged, not that the requirements' content is right, and not that an agent follows them.
