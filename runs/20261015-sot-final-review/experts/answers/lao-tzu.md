# 老子 (Laozi) — Will an agent follow the memory design, and who decides what it leaves open

## Answer

An agent will mostly follow it — one requirement per record, one `fails_if` per record, and the two rounds of expert levels have produced real gates — but four things will stop a careful one: W1.2a (MUST) and W1.2b (SHOULD) contradict each other; 7 of the ids the prose cites name no record, and 16 of the 46 requirements have no record of their own; every SHOULD's escape ("with no written reason") has no place named to write the reason; and W3.2 is a MUST whose first check does not exist.

Keep the COO's wording with three changes (drop the key-layout sentence out of W1.2a, sharpen W6.1a's `fails_if`, move the open choice out of `who_decides` into `text`), make the reviewer **the reader the prose already names** rather than a new role anyone must appoint, give W3.6 to the project owner by way of `AGENTS.md` and Rd3.1a to whoever *measures* rather than whoever decides — and answer the three behaviour questions: (a) no, not without a replacement; (b) no, K1.1 never mentions supersession and links nothing; (c) the part the record governs, which is what Rd5.3 already says and what `levels.MUST` contradicts.

## How I decided

### 1. Will an agent follow it?

**1. The arithmetic holds, so the file's own checks pass.** I counted the pasted YAML: 34 clause records under 16 parents, 30 requirements with a record of their own, 16 parents named only by their clauses — 64 records, 46 requirements. That matches the Facts. My #7 (慎終如始，則無敗事 `quoted`) — the thing to check first is the end of the counting, and it lands.

**2. But 16 requirements are named and never written down:** W1.1, W1.2, W1.3, W2.1, W2.2, W3.3, W3.5, W4.1, W5.3, W6.1, W6.3, K6.1, K6.3, Rd2.3, Rd3.1, Rd4.5. The prose cites seven ids that lead to no record at all: W1.2, W5.3, K6.3, Rd4.5 (parents), K6, Rd3, Rd4 (groups). W1.1b's `fails_if` — "an id is not in the W1.1 form" — points the reader at a parent that has no record, so the only place the form is written is the sentence above it in the same record. My #8 (知不知上，不知知病 `quoted`): a reference that answers nothing is the sickness this rule was written to catch. One sentence in `how_to_read` — "a citation to a requirement id names its clause records; a citation to a group id names the group" — closes all seven, and costs nothing.

**3. `how_to_read`'s "When a clause and its requirement seem to differ, text governs" is unusable as written.** Which text: the record's own `text` over its `fails_if`, or `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` over the YAML? And a requirement with no record has no text to govern with. My reading: it is meant to say the record's `text` governs its own `fails_if`, `check` and `who_decides`; write it out, because the second reading is worse than none — an agent that believes the prose wins will drop YAML rules the prose never stated (see step 4).

**4. The prose carries rules no requirement states, which under that second reading become optional.** Three examples: "What was learned while running one is a lesson" (W3.6 says procedures are not entries; the lesson half has no id); "Edits to different lines of one file merge without warning even if they contradict each other, so a clean merge does not mean the file is consistent"; "A decision about this project is a project entry, whoever made it, the project owner included". Worse, the middle one is *under-covered*: W6.2's `fails_if` is "an edit to an entry is lost without a conflict", and a clean-but-contradicting merge loses no edit, so W6.2 passes exactly the case the prose warns about. My #1 (損之又損，以至於無為 `quoted`): either these three sentences get ids or they go. Keeping the third one is cheapest — add "…even when both are active" to W6.2's `fails_if`.

**5. W1.2a and W1.2b contradict each other.** W1.2a (MUST): "Each field is one `key: value` line." W1.2b (SHOULD): "A field with several values (`covers`, the links, `changed:`) holds them on indented lines below its key." An agent writing three paths under `covers:` breaks the MUST. W1.2a's `fails_if` covers only the `---` envelope, so the tool never sees the sentence that conflicts. My #1 again — take the sentence out of the MUST and put it in the SHOULD it belongs to; the split itself is right, because the `---` lines are what the tool fails and the layout is what a reviewer judges.

**6. The SHOULD escape has nowhere to go.** Thirty-three records end their `fails_if` with "with no written reason", and no record says where the reason is written. K5.2 shows the pattern the design already owns: a journal-read is logged in the task's `state.md`. Name that one place, once, in `checks` or `how_to_read`. My #17 (故有之以為利，無之以為用 `quoted`): the use of this design is in the empty places — the written reason, the unnamed reviewer, the open `who_decides` — and an empty place with no name is a place no one writes to.

**7. Three failures happen on events nothing records.** Rd2.2's, Rd2.3a's and Rd2.3b's `fails_if` all turn on what a session *loaded*; `check: reviewer`. A reviewer cannot see it unless the session said what it read. The same fix as step 6 — one line in `state.md` at session start — covers all three, and it costs one sentence per record.

**8. W3.2 is a MUST standing on a tool that does not exist.** `check: secret scanner; reviewer`, and the Facts say no secret scanner exists. `checks.secret scanner` describes a scanner without saying it is absent; `checks.none` at least says "guidance: nothing to check". An agent that looks for the scanner, finds none, and concludes the record is uncheckable will skip a MUST about credentials. Add to that key: "not built yet; until it is, the reviewer is the only check, and the entry is unjudged rather than passed."

**9. `none` means two different things in one file.** `who_decides: none` = the record leaves no choice open. `check: none` = nothing to check. An agent reading eleven guidance records with `check: none` and then reading `who_decides: none` on W1.1c can reasonably conclude nobody is judging that record — which is true — or that nobody needs to — which is false. Rename the guidance check to `not applicable`. My #2 (知足不辱，知止不殆 `quoted`): know where one word ends before you reuse it.

**10. `W4.1b`'s `fails_if` lets a missing field through.** "an entry's `status` is any other value" — absence is not a value, so an entry with no `status:` passes W4.1b while the tool fails it (the tool checks status is one of three). W1.2b, a SHOULD, lists `status` among the keys, so the same entry only breaks a SHOULD. Change to "missing, or any other value". My reading — this is the same shape as step 5, and both come from treating the tool's checks and the records' `fails_if` as two lists written by different hands.

**11. The prose hides the one exception to W4.1a.** "Anyone may change any entry, and every change is logged… there is no approval gate" is in *Who it is for*; the exception (W4.1c: a reader who finds a mismatch reports and does not settle) is a MUST buried in *Keeping it current*. The Facts list W4.1c as a MUST, so the design intends the gate to bind. One clause in the prose fixes it.

**12. W6.3b and K6.3b are one rule written twice.** W6.3b (Write) says an `INDEX.md` conflict is resolved by regenerating; K6.3b (Keep) says `INDEX.md` is never merged by hand. Both cite K6.3. W6.3b's `fails_if` — "as K6.3b." — points at a record one letter away from itself, so an agent cannot tell whether it has satisfied W6.3b or K6.3b. And W6.3b is the one SHOULD whose `fails_if` does not carry "with no written reason". My #3 (功遂身退，天之道 `quoted`): one rule, one id, cited twice; the duplicate is what the next reader has to withdraw.

**13. Three external documents are required and never provisioned.** `tools/memory_index.py` (`checks.tool`, K6.2), `docs/index-md/` (K6.2), `CACHES.md` (Rd3.1b, Rd4.2). The design's *Where it is put* lists neither `tools/` nor those files, and the design is for any project. An agent introducing this into a fresh clone hits a MUST whose check is a script that is not there. Either name them in *Where it is put*, or say in `how_to_read` that a design adopted into a project must bring these three. My #6 (為之於未有，治之於未亂 `quoted`): the cheap moment to fix this is before the project exists.

**14. Rd3.1b's `basis` names no section.** It says `d: the size check`, and the design's sections are *Where it is put*, *When it will be used*, *When it will not be used*. The sentence it means is the last one of *When it will not be used*. Every other `d:` names a real heading; this one does not.

### 3. Who reviews the judged checks

**Counted from the file: 37 of 64 records name `reviewer`, and 12 of the 25 MUSTs have no other check** — W4.1c, W5.3b, W3.3a, W3.5a, Rd1.2, Rd2.3a, Rd4.1, Rd5.1, Rd5.2, Rd5.3, Rd6.1, plus W3.2 whose secret scanner does not exist. Seven more records are checked only by `git`, which is a record, not a judge. So the design's gates are, in the main, one unnamed person.

Do not appoint a reviewer role — that would be inventing a value, and the house rule forbids it. The prose already has the actor: Rd5.3's "the reader", and *Keeping it current*'s "whoever reads a decision or fact checks it against the code". So:

- `checks.reviewer` should read: "whoever reads or reviews the work; the project owner may name someone else. If nobody reviews it, the record is **unjudged**, not passed."
- `roles:` gains **the reader** — a role the prose already uses, so this is a transcription, not an invention.
- The writer checks their own entry against the reviewer clauses before committing. My #16 (知人者智，自知者明；勝人者有力，自勝者強 `quoted`): judge yourself first; of the reviewer clauses, most are cheapest at the moment of writing.
- `check: git` needs the same treatment: the git check is run by whoever reviews the diff.

My #9 (江海所以能為百谷王者，以其善下之 `quoted`): the reviewer stands behind the work and below it in rank; the design should say so rather than leave a name-shaped hole. My #19 (人皆取先，己獨取後 `attributed`) for the same reason — the person behind is the one who checks, not the one who wrote.

### 4. The two open `who_decides`

**W3.6 (whether a procedure can be a script) — assign, do not leave unassigned: the project owner, by way of what `AGENTS.md` names.** W3.6 already requires `AGENTS.md` to name each script one line each, so the decision has a home; the memory only inherits it. Give it a default so the record is judgeable before the owner answers: until then, a procedure is a page in `docs/`, and it becomes a script only when it is run repeatedly and mechanically. My #6 and my #1 — the smaller artefact is the default, and making the script is the subtraction you have already earned. My #4 (為者敗之，執者失之 `quoted`) for the principle: a script acts on the repo, and memory should not act. **Cost:** procedures stay prose longer than necessary, and every project inherits one default it may have to undo.

**Rd3.1a (which agents work on the project, so the strictest limit) — assign to whoever measures; it is not a decision.** Nobody settles this by opinion. `how_to_read` already says "There are no fixed numbers: where a limit applies, it is a comparison that measurement decides". So `who_decides` should read "the project owner, by measurement: the smallest documented start-file limit among the agents in use, written as one line in `AGENTS.md`". Two consequences the design must accept out loud: until it is measured, Rd3.1a is **unjudged** and `INDEX.md` grows (the prose already accepts that entries go stale); and the number must live in `AGENTS.md` or a tool argument, not hard-coded in `tools/memory_index.py`, or a project with a different strictest limit cannot comply without changing code. My #16 (知足者富；不失其所者久 `quoted`) — the number is enough when it is measured, and it lasts only while the roster is watched. My #7: `INDEX.md` grows at the *end* of a project's life, which is where things fail, so the limit has to be lowered as agents are added, not fixed once.

### 5. The three behaviour questions

**(a) May an entry be retired with nothing replacing it? Not under K4.1 as written — and the design should say so, or change K4.1.** K4.1's `fails_if` is "a `superseded` or `retired` entry with no link", `check: git`, so retirement with nothing to point at fails a MUST today. But `retired` is a legal `status` (W4.1b) that no requirement ever produces, and W4.1a lets anyone write it.
