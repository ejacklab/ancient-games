# 老子 (Laozi) — final list

## Changes from the top 12

1. **#1 (ch. 48, subtraction) — corrected → final #1.** Removed "add to your knowledge, then subtract" and "every act of doing is a loss": the words describe what learning and the Way do; they give no two-stage order (codex row 1, agreed). Silk chapter is 11, not 48 (codex). Also wrong in the top 12, and not caught by codex: "the silk carries 無為而無不為 verbatim". Silk B breaks off after 以至於無, and silk A is lost here. `silk.txt:117` prints the formula as a gap-fill: the 校勘版 is "甲乙本合并補缺". The formula is attested in Guodian 乙 instead (亡為而亡不為).
2. **#2 (ch. 13, 為吾有身) — dropped.** Codex row 2 is right that "find your own stake before you trust your advice" is a modern application. I go further than codex, which said change. Stated plainly, the words say "my great trouble comes from having a self". But the same chapter ends by entrusting the world to one who *values* his self (貴以身為天下，若可寄天下), so a one-line rule misleads whichever half it keeps. It is listed in Gaps.
3. **#3 (ch. 29, 天下神器) — corrected → final #4.** Removed "aim at a part" and "never" (codex row 3, agreed). Joined with ch. 64's identical 為者敗之，執者失之 because that passage *is* in Guodian (甲 and 丙), while ch. 29 is not.
4. **#4 (ch. 22 + 24) — corrected → final #14.** Removed "in this order". Removed "don't look at yourself": 自視 in the silk corresponds to WB 自是 "affirm oneself", not self-examination (codex row 4, agreed). The silk keeps ch. 22 (silk 67) and ch. 24 (silk 66) apart, so "the joining is the manuscript's" is false (codex claims bullet). I cite both as separate passages.
5. **#5 (ch. 63 + 64) — corrected and split → final #6 (act early, treat as difficult) and #7 (careful at the end).** Removed "the end is cheap, the middle expensive" and "the final step" (codex row 5, agreed). Also wrong: the top 12 says Guodian 甲 has 圖難於其易，為大於其細. The Guodian transcription has only 為亡為，事亡事，味亡味。大小之多易必多難。是以聖人猶難之，故終亡難.
6. **#6 (ch. 41, 明道若昧) — dropped.** Codex row 6 said change; I drop it, for three reasons. As a mindset it overlaps final #11 and #14. Its decision use needs the "judge by outcome" test that codex rightly says the words lack. Its best-known image differs across the witnesses: Guodian 大器曼成, silk B 大器免成, WB 大器晚成. It is listed in Gaps.
7. **#7 (ch. 9 + 2) — corrected → final #3.** Removed "running without you" and "stop attending to it" (codex row 7, agreed). Added ch. 9's own first line, 持而盈之，不如其已. The earlier claim "遂 is an editorial emendation for 述 'recounts'" has no support in these sources. Guodian's 攻述 is transcribed 攻（功）述（遂）, and the raw silk transcription has 功遂 in both A and B (only the 校勘版 prints 述).
8. **#8 (ch. 36, reversal pairs) — corrected → final #12.** Removed "then stop and let the thing move against you". The pairs now follow WB (廢/興) and silk (去/與) separately; both have four pairs (codex row 8 and claims bullet, agreed). The rule is no longer joined to ch. 78.
9. **#9 (ch. 71, 知不知) — corrected → final #8.** Removed "the cure is the same move as the disease" and the "performing knowledge" reading given as fact (codex row 9, agreed). Not caught by codex: "the silk reads 不知不知" is true of silk A only. Silk B reads 不知﹦ = 不知知, the same as WB. The 校勘版 chose A.
10. **#10 (ch. 33) — corrected → final #16.** These are parallel statements, not a ladder, and the text never says "harder" (codex row 10, agreed). All eight clauses are now quoted; the top 12 dropped 死而不亡者壽.
11. **#11 (ch. 8, water) — corrected → final #15.** Seven parallel clauses with no order, so it is a mindset, not "eight checks in order" (codex row 11, agreed). Also wrong, in both earlier files: "in the earliest complete witness the virtue is stillness (上善治水…有靜)". That is silk A only. Silk A writes 靜 for 爭 even in its closing 夫唯不靜，故無尤, and silk B reads 上善**如**水…而有**爭**.
12. **#12 (Shiji counsel) — corrected → final #18.** Removed "before anything else", "give one short answer" and "show less appetite than you feel" (codex row 12, agreed: 去 means remove, not hide). The three parts of the speech are now stated plainly. Added the narrator's line on his departure (codex claims bullet), labelled as the narrator's voice.

## Final list

1. **Take away, and take away again.**
   - **Kind:** algorithm. The words give a repeated step and its end point (損之又損，以至於無為), and nothing more.
   - **Rule:** Learning adds something every day; the Way takes something away every day. Take away, and take away again, until you reach not-doing (無為); with not-doing, nothing is left undone.
   - **Their words:** 為學日益，為道日損。損之又損，以至於無為。無為而無不為。取天下常以無事，及其有事，不足以取天下。 — "Pursuing learning, one gains daily; pursuing the Way, one loses daily. Lose and lose again, until one arrives at not-doing. Not doing, and nothing is left undone. The world is always taken by having no business; once one has business, one is not enough to take the world." Guodian 乙: 学者日益，为道者日员（损）。员（损）之或员（损），以至亡为也，亡为而亡不为。 — "The learner gains daily; the one who does the Way loses daily. Lose and again lose, until not-doing; not-doing, and nothing not done."
   - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 48 (王弼本, `sources/lao-tzu/道德經__王弼本_.txt`); 郭店 乙 slips 3–4 ([zh.wikisource 郭店楚墓竹簡](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/郭店楚墓竹簡)). Silk B ([zh.wikisource 老子 (帛書本)](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/老子_(帛書本)), received ch. 48) has 為學者日益，聞道者日云﹦之有云，以至於無□□□□□□□□□□取天下恆無事… — "聞道者" (one who *hears* the Way), and a lacuna where the formula would be. The 取天下 sentence is in WB and silk B but not in Guodian, whose slip runs straight on to 絕學亡憂.
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** top-12 #1

2. **Know what is enough; know to stop.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** Know what is enough and you are not disgraced; know to stop and you are not endangered, and can last long. No calamity is greater than not knowing what is enough.
   - **Their words:** 名與身孰親？身與貨孰多？得與亡孰病？是故甚愛必大費，多藏必厚亡，知足不辱，知止不殆，可以長久。 — "Name or self: which is dearer? Self or goods: which counts for more? Gaining or losing: which is the sickness? So great fondness must mean great expense, and much hoarding must mean heavy loss. Knowing what is enough, one is not disgraced; knowing to stop, one is not endangered; one can last long." (ch. 44) · 禍莫大於不知足；咎莫大於欲得。故知足之足，常足矣。 — "No calamity is greater than not knowing what is enough; no fault is greater than wanting to get. So the enough of knowing enough is a lasting enough." (ch. 46) · 始制有名，名亦既有，夫亦將知止，知止所以不殆。 — "When ordering begins there are names; once there are names, one will also know to stop; knowing to stop is how one is not endangered." (ch. 32) Guodian 甲: 古（故）智（知）足不辱，智（知）止不怠（殆），可以长旧（久）。 (ch. 44) · 化（祸）莫大○（乎）不智（知）足。智（知）足之为足，此亘（恒）足矣。 (ch. 46) · 夫亦○（将）智（知）止，智（知）止所以不○（殆）。 (ch. 32)
   - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 44, 46, 32 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 35–37 (ch. 44), 5–6 (ch. 46), 19–20 (ch. 32); silk ch. 7 = received 44 (`silk.txt:87`; silk A also preserves the line).
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** new (your research; codex "Missing")

3. **Stop before it is full; when the work is done, withdraw.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** Holding something and filling it to the brim is not as good as stopping. When the work is done, withdraw yourself; that is the Way of heaven. Accomplish and do not settle in it; because one does not settle in it, it does not leave.
   - **Their words:** 持而盈之，不如其已；揣而梲之，不可長保。金玉滿堂，莫之能守；富貴而驕，自遺其咎。功遂身退，天之道。 — "To hold it and fill it is not as good as stopping. Hammer it to a point and it cannot be kept long. Gold and jade fill the hall and none can guard them. Rich, high and arrogant, one brings blame on oneself. The work done, the self withdraws: this is the Way of heaven." (ch. 9) · 功成而弗居。夫唯弗居，是以不去。 — "The work is achieved and he does not dwell in it. Just because he does not dwell in it, it does not leave." (ch. 2) Guodian 甲: ○而○（盈）之，不不若已。…攻（功）述（遂）身退，天之道也。 (ch. 9) · 成而弗居。天〈夫〉唯弗居也，是以弗去也。 (ch. 2)
   - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 9 and 2 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 37–39 (ch. 9), 17–18 (ch. 2). Silk B has 功遂身退，天之道也 and silk A has 功遂身芮 (帛書本 page); the 校勘版 prints 功述身芮 (`silk.txt:567`).
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** top-12 #7

4. **Acting on it ruins it; gripping it loses it.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** Whoever acts on it ruins it and whoever grips it loses it, so the sage does not act and does not ruin, does not grip and does not lose. The world is a sacred vessel that cannot be acted on, so the sage drops the extreme, the extravagant and the excessive.
   - **Their words:** 為者敗之，執者失之。是以聖人無為故無敗，無執故無失。 — "One who acts on it spoils it; one who holds on to it loses it. So the sage does not act, and therefore does not spoil; does not hold, and therefore does not lose." (ch. 64) · 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已。天下神器，不可為也，為者敗之，執者失之。…是以聖人去甚，去奢，去泰。 — "One who wants to take the world and act on it: I see that he will not get it. The world is a sacred vessel; it cannot be acted on. One who acts on it spoils it; one who holds it loses it. … So the sage discards the extreme, the extravagant, the excessive." (ch. 29) Guodian 甲: 为之者败之，执之者远之。是以圣人亡为古（故）亡败；亡执古（故）亡○（失）。 ("one who holds it pushes it away") · Guodian 丙: 为之者败之，执之者○（失）之。
   - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 64 and 29 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 10–11, 丙 11 (ch. 64; ch. 29 is not in Guodian). Silk ch. 73 = received 29 (`silk.txt:795–801`) has 夫天下 神器也 非可為者也 … 去甚 去大 去楮 (silk B: 去諸).
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** top-12 #3

5. **I do not act, and the people transform by themselves.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** Govern a state with the straight, use troops with the unexpected, and take the world by having no business. The sage says: I do not act and the people transform by themselves; I like stillness and the people set themselves right; I have no business and the people grow rich by themselves; I have no desires (Guodian and silk: "I want not to want") and the people become simple by themselves.
   - **Their words:** 以正治國，以奇用兵，以無事取天下。…故聖人云：「我無為而民自化，我好靜而民自正，我無事而民自富，我無欲而民自樸。」 — "Govern the state with the straight, use troops with the unexpected, take the world with no business. … So the sage says: 'I do not act, and the people transform themselves; I love stillness, and the people correct themselves; I have no business, and the people enrich themselves; I have no desire, and the people become plain by themselves.'" (ch. 57) · 道常無為而無不為。侯王若能守之，萬物將自化。 — "The Way constantly does not act, and yet nothing is left undone. If lords and kings can keep to it, the ten thousand things will transform by themselves." (ch. 37) Guodian 甲: 以正之（治）邦，以○（奇）○（用）兵，以亡事取天下。…是以圣人之言曰：我无事而民自○（富）。我亡为而民自○（化）。我好青（静）而民自正。我谷（欲）不谷（欲）而民自朴。 (ch. 57) · ○（道）亘（恒）亡为也，侯王能守之，而万勿（物）○（将）自○（化）。 (ch. 37)
   - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 57 and 37 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 29–32 (ch. 57), 13 (ch. 37); silk ch. 20 = received 57 (`silk.txt:216–220`). At ch. 37 Guodian has 道恆亡為也 with no 而無不為, and both silk copies have 道恆無名 with no 無為 (帛書本 page; `silk.txt:891`).
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** new (your research; restores the ch. 37 strand of identity #1)

6. **Act while it is small and easy; treat it as difficult.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** What is at rest is easy to hold and what has shown no sign is easy to plan for, so act on a thing before it exists and put it in order before it is in disorder. Taking many things as easy brings many difficulties, so the sage still treats them as difficult and in the end has no difficulty.
   - **Their words:** 其安易持，其未兆易謀。其脆易泮，其微易散。為之於未有，治之於未亂。合抱之木，生於毫末；九層之臺，起於累土；千里之行，始於足下。 — "What is at rest is easy to hold; what has not yet shown a sign is easy to plan for. What is brittle is easy to split; what is minute is easy to scatter. Act on it before it exists; order it before it is in disorder. A tree a man's arms can circle grows from a hair-tip; a nine-storey terrace rises from piled earth; a thousand-li journey begins under the feet." (ch. 64) · 圖難於其易，為大於其細；天下難事必作於易，天下大事必作於細。是以聖人終不為大，故能成其大。夫輕諾必寡信，多易必多難。是以聖人猶難之，故終無難矣 — "Plan for the difficult while it is easy; do the great while it is small. The world's difficult affairs must arise from the easy, its great affairs from the small. So the sage never does the great, and therefore can achieve the great. Light promises must bring little trust; much taken as easy must bring much difficulty. So the sage still treats things as difficult, and therefore in the end has no difficulty." (ch. 63) Guodian 甲: 其安也，易○也。其未○（兆）也，易○（谋）也。其○（脆）也，易畔（判）也。其几也，易○（散）也。为之于其亡又（有）也。○（治）之于其未乱。合□□□□□□□，九成之台甲□□□□□□□□□足下。 (ch. 64) · 为亡为，事亡事，未（味）亡未（味）。大少（小）之多惕（易）必多○（难）。是以圣人猷（犹）○（难）之，古（故）终亡○（难）。 (ch. 63)
   - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 64 and 63 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 25–27 (ch. 64), 14–15 (ch. 63). Guodian's ch. 63 lacks 圖難於其易, 天下難事必作於易 and 輕諾必寡信; silk A has them (`silk.txt:284–290`). The images differ by witness. WB has 九層之臺，起於累土；千里之行. Silk A has 九成之臺作於羸土，百仁（仞）之高台（始）於…, and silk B …作於欙土，百千之高始於足[下]. Guodian is broken at that point. "A journey of a thousand li" appears in WB only among these witnesses.
   - **Words are:** `quoted`
   - **From:** top-12 #5 (first half)

7. **Be as careful at the end as at the beginning.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** People in their affairs often fail when the work is nearly done; be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and no affair fails.

Guodian 丙: ○（慎）终若○（始），则无败事喜（矣）。人之败也，亘（恒）于其○（且）成也败之。 — "…People's failing always comes when they are about to succeed."
   - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 64 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 11, 丙 12; silk ch. 27 (`silk.txt:299`).

8. **Know that you do not know.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** To know that you do not know is best.    - **Their words:** 知不知上，不知知病。夫唯病病，是以不病。聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病。 — "Knowing not-knowing: highest. Not-knowing knowing: sickness. Only by treating sickness as sickness is one not sick. 9. **To be above, put yourself below; to be in front, put yourself behind.**
   - **Kind:** decision rule
   - **Rule:** If you want to be above the people, you must put yourself below them in your words; if you want to be in front of them, you must put yourself behind them in your person. Then they do not feel your weight above them or harm from you in front, and because you do not contend, no one in the world can contend with you.
   - **Their words:** 江海所以能為百谷王者，以其善下之，故能為百谷王。是以欲上民，必以言下之。欲先民，必以身後之。是以聖人處上而民不重，處前而民不害。是以天下樂推而不厭，以其不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。 — "Rivers and seas can be kings of the hundred valleys because they are good at being below them. So, wanting to be above the people, one must in speech put oneself below them; wanting to be ahead of the people, one must in person put oneself behind them. So the sage is above and the people do not feel the weight; he is in front and the people are not harmed. So the world gladly pushes him forward and does not tire of it. Because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him." Guodian 甲: 圣人之才（在）民前也，以身后之；其才（在）民上也，以言下之。其才（在）民上也，民弗厚也；其才（在）民前也，民弗害也。天下乐进而弗○（厌）。以其不静（争）也，古（故）天下莫能与之静（争）。
   - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 66 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 2–5; silk ch. 29 (`silk.txt:319`; A and B both). Guodian states it as what the sage does ("when he is in front of the people, he puts himself behind them"), without the 欲…必 "if you want…, you must" form that silk and WB have.

10. **Three treasures: compassion, frugality, not daring to be first.**
    - **Kind:** decision rule
    - **Rule:** Hold on to three treasures: compassion, frugality, and not daring to be first in the world. Compassion makes one able to be brave, frugality makes one able to be broad, and not daring to be first makes one able to become head of the vessels. To give these up and still be brave, broad and first is death.
    - **Their words:** 我有三寶，持而保之。一曰慈，二曰儉，三曰不敢為天下先。慈故能勇，儉故能廣，不敢為天下先，故能成器長。今舍慈且勇，舍儉且廣，舍後且先，死矣！夫慈以戰則勝，以守則固。天將救之，以慈衛之。 — "I have three treasures; I hold and keep them.     - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 67 (王弼本); silk ch. 32 (`silk.txt:344–352`: 一曰茲 二曰檢 … 天將建之 如以茲垣之). Not in Guodian.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** new (your research; codex "Missing"; it was identity #10)

11. **The weak and soft overcome the hard and strong.**
    - **Kind:** mindset
    - **Rule:** Reversal is how the Way moves; weakness is how the Way is used. The hard and strong go with death and the soft and weak with life. Nothing is softer than water, yet nothing does better at attacking the hard: everyone knows the weak beats the strong, and no one can practise it.
    - **Their words:** 反者道之動，弱者道之用。 — "Reversal is the movement of the Way; weakness is the use of the Way." (ch. 40) · 故堅強者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。是以兵強則不勝… 強大處下，柔弱處上。 — "So the hard and strong are companions of death, the soft and weak companions of life. So when an army is strong it does not win… The strong and great sit below; the soft and weak sit above." (ch. 76) · 天下莫柔弱於水，而攻堅強者，莫之能勝，其無以易之。弱之勝強，柔之勝剛，天下莫不知莫能行。是以聖人云：「受國之垢，是謂社稷主；受國不祥，是為天下王。」正言若反。 — "Nothing in the world is softer and weaker than water, yet for attacking the hard and strong nothing surpasses it; nothing can take its place. That the weak beats the strong and the soft beats the hard, everyone in the world knows and no one can practise. So the sage says: 'One who takes on the state's filth is called lord of its altars; one who takes on the state's misfortune is king of the world.' Straight words seem reversed." (ch. 78) Guodian 甲: 返也者，道○（动）也。溺（弱）也者，道之○（用）也。 (ch. 40)
    - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 40, 76, 78 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 37 (ch. 40 only; chs. 76 and 78 are not in Guodian). Silk ch. 4 (`silk.txt:54`), ch. 41 (`:435`), and ch. 43 (`:455–457`: 水之勝剛 弱之勝強 天下莫弗知 而莫能行也).

12. **What is to be shrunk must first be stretched.**
    - **Kind:** decision rule
    - **Rule:** What is to be shrunk must first be stretched; what is to be weakened must first be strengthened. What is to be discarded must first be raised (WB 廢/興; silk 去/與, "given to"), and what is to be taken must first be given. This is called subtle clarity (微明). The words do not say who does the stretching or what follows.
    - **Their words:** 將欲歙之，必固張之；將欲弱之，必固強之；將欲廢之，必固興之；將欲奪之，必固與之。是謂微明。柔弱勝剛強。魚不可脫於淵，國之利器不可以示人。 — "About to shrink it, one must first stretch it; about to weaken it, one must first strengthen it; about to abolish it, one must first raise it; about to seize it, one must first give to it. This is called subtle clarity. The soft and weak beat the hard and strong. Fish must not leave the deep; the state's sharp instruments must not be shown to people." Silk A: 將欲拾之，必古張之；將欲弱之，□□強之；將欲去之，必古與之；將欲奪之，必古予之。是胃微明。柔弱勝強。魚不脫於瀟，邦利器不可以視人。
    - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 36 (王弼本); silk ch. 80 (`silk.txt:882–886`; A as quoted, from the 帛書本 page). Not in Guodian. 韓非子·喻老 reads it as strategy: 越王入宦於吳…故曰：「將欲翕之，必固張之」 and 晉獻公將欲襲虞，遺之以璧馬…故曰：「將欲取之，必固與之。」 That is Han Fei's application, not the text's words.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** top-12 #8

13. **Achieve the result and stop; use weapons when it cannot be avoided; mourn the victory.**
    - **Kind:** decision rule
    - **Rule:** One who helps a ruler by the Way does not use troops to force the world. The good one achieves the result and stops, and does not use it to take strength. He achieves it without vaunting, boasting or arrogance, and as something he could not avoid. Weapons are instruments of ill omen, used when there is no alternative; calm indifference is best, victory is not made beautiful, and a victory is treated with the rites of mourning.
    - **Their words:** 以道佐人主者，不以兵強天下。其事好還。…善有果而已，不敢以取強。果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而勿驕。果而不得已，果而勿強。 — "One who assists a ruler with the Way does not force the world with troops; such affairs tend to come back. … The good one has a result and that is all; he does not dare use it to take strength. Result, and no vaunting; result, and no boasting; result, and no arrogance; result, as something unavoidable; result, and no forcing." (ch. 30) · 兵者不祥之器，非君子之器，不得已而用之，恬淡為上。勝而不美，而美之者，是樂殺人。…戰勝，以喪禮處之。 — "Weapons are instruments of ill omen, not the gentleman's instruments; when it cannot be avoided he uses them, and calm indifference is best. He wins and does not find it beautiful; one who finds it beautiful delights in killing people. … A victory in war is handled with the rites of mourning." (ch. 31) Guodian 甲: 以○（道）差（佐）人○（主）者，不谷（欲）以兵强于天下。善者果而已，不以取强。果而弗发（伐），果而弗乔（骄），果而弗○（矜），是胃（谓）果而不强。 (ch. 30) · Guodian 丙: …得已而甬（用）之。○○为上，弗○（美）也。○〈美〉之，是乐杀人。…战○（胜）则以丧豊（礼）居之。 (ch. 31)
    - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 30, 31 (王弼本); 郭店 甲 6–8 (ch. 30), 丙 6–10 (ch. 31); silk chs. 74, 75 (`silk.txt:806–833`). WB has no commentary on ch. 31. 晁說之's postface in the sources file reports that Wang Bi took 佳兵者不祥之器…以喪禮處之 as 非老子之言, yet Guodian 丙 and both silk copies carry it.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** new (your research; codex "Missing")

14. **Do not display yourself; do not contend.**
    - **Kind:** decision rule (four parallel restraints, no order)
    - **Rule:** Bent, then whole; crooked, then straight; hollow, then full; worn, then new; little, then gaining; much, then confused. The sage does not display himself and so is clear, does not affirm himself and so stands out, does not boast and so has merit, and does not vaunt himself and so lasts; because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him.
    - **Their words:** 曲則全，枉則直，窪則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑。是以聖人抱一為天下式。不自見故明，不自是故彰，不自伐故有功，不自矜故長。夫唯不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。 — "Bent, then whole; crooked, then straight; hollow, then full; worn, then new; little, then gained; much, then confused. So the sage embraces the one and is the world's model. Not displaying himself, he is clear; not affirming himself, he is distinguished; not boasting, he has merit; not vaunting, he lasts. Just because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him." (ch. 22) · 企者不立，跨者不行，自見者不明，自是者不彰，自伐者無功，自矜者不長。其在道也，曰餘食贅行。物或惡之，故有道者不處。 — "On tiptoe one does not stand; striding, one does not walk. Who displays himself is not clear; who affirms himself is not distinguished; who boasts has no merit; who vaunts does not last. In the Way these are called leftover food and superfluous action. Things may loathe them, so one who has the Way does not dwell in them." (ch. 24)
    - **Source:** 道德經 chs. 22, 24 (王弼本). Silk ch. 67 = received 22 (`silk.txt:715–721`, 執一以為天下牧) and silk ch. 66 = received 24 (`:708–710`, 炊者不立). The 明/彰 pairing swaps between silk A (不□視故明，不自見故章, one graph lost) and silk B (不自視故章，不自見也故明). Neither chapter is in Guodian.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** top-12 #4

15. **The highest good is like water.**
    - **Kind:** mindset (seven parallel clauses, no order)
    - **Rule:** The highest good is like water: water benefits all things and does not contend (WB), and it settles where people dislike to be, so it is near the Way. Good in dwelling is the ground, in the heart depth, in giving benevolence (silk B: heaven), in speech trust, in governing order, in affairs ability, and in moving timing; because it does not contend, there is no blame.
    - **Their words:** 上善若水。水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。居善地，心善淵，與善仁，言善信，正善治，事善能，動善時。夫唯不爭，故無尤。 — "The highest good is like water. Water is good at benefiting the ten thousand things and does not contend; it settles where the crowd dislikes, and so it is near the Way. Dwelling: good is the ground. Heart: good is depth. Giving: good is benevolence. Speech: good is trust. Governing: good is order. Affairs: good is ability. Moving: good is timing. Just because it does not contend, there is no blame." Silk A: 上善治水﹦善利萬物而有靜…夫唯不靜，故無尤. Silk B: 上善如水﹦善利萬物而有爭…予善天，言善信…夫唯不爭，故無尤.
    - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 8 (王弼本); silk ch. 52 (`silk.txt:550–554`, a composite of A and B); 帛書本 page for A and B. Not in Guodian. Where WB has 而不爭, both silk copies have 而有—. A uses 靜 also for the closing 不爭, so the silk's sense at this point is unresolved (my reading).
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** top-12 #11

16. **Knowing yourself is clarity; overcoming yourself is strength.**
    - **Kind:** mindset (eight parallel statements, not a ladder)
    - **Rule:** Knowing others is cleverness; knowing yourself is clarity. Overcoming others takes force and overcoming yourself is strength; knowing what is enough is wealth, pressing on is will, not losing your place is lasting, and dying without perishing is long life.
    - **Their words:** 知人者智，自知者明。勝人者有力，自勝者強。知足者富。強行者有志。不失其所者久。死而不亡者壽。 — "One who knows others is clever; one who knows himself is clear. One who overcomes others has force; one who overcomes himself is strong. One who knows what is enough is rich. One who presses on has will. One who does not lose his place lasts. One who dies and does not perish is long-lived."
    - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 33 (王弼本); silk ch. 77 (`silk.txt:851–857`: 知人者 知也 … 死不忘者 壽也; silk B 死而不忘者壽也, "dies and is not forgotten"). Not in Guodian.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** top-12 #10

17. **The use is in what is not there.**
    - **Kind:** mindset
    - **Rule:** The cart's use is in the empty space at the hub, the vessel's use in its hollow, and the room's use in the openings. So what is there gives benefit, and what is not there gives use. The words do not say to remove anything; that would be an application.
    - **Their words:** 三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用。埏埴以為器，當其無，有器之用。鑿戶牖以為室，當其無，有室之用。故有之以為利，無之以為用。 — "Thirty spokes share one hub; in its nothing lies the cart's use. Clay is moulded into a vessel; in its nothing lies the vessel's use. Doors and windows are cut to make a room; in its nothing lies the room's use. So what is there makes for benefit; what is not there makes for use."
    - **Source:** 道德經 ch. 11 (王弼本); silk ch. 55 (`silk.txt:583–589`). Not in Guodian.
    - **Words are:** `quoted`
    - **From:** new (your research; codex "Missing")

18. **When your time comes, ride out; when it does not, drift away.**
    - **Kind:** decision rule
    - **Rule:** A gentleman who gets his time rides out, and one who does not drifts away. The good merchant hides his goods deep as if he had none, and the gentleman of full virtue looks like a fool. Confucius is told to put away his arrogance and many desires, his airs and excessive aims, which do his person no good. Separately, as narrator, Sima Qian writes that Laozi's learning made hiding himself and namelessness its task, and that, seeing Zhou decline, he left.
    - **Their words:** 老子曰：「子所言者，其人與骨皆已朽矣，獨其言在耳。且君子得其時則駕，不得其時則蓬累而行。吾聞之，良賈深藏若虛，君子盛德容貌若愚。去子之驕氣與多欲，態色與淫志，是皆無益於子之身。吾所以告子，若是而已。」 — "Laozi said: 'The men you speak of have rotted, they and their bones; only their words remain. And a gentleman, when he gets his time, rides out; when he does not get his time, he goes off like drifting tumbleweed. I have heard that a good merchant stores deep as if empty, and a gentleman of abundant virtue looks like a fool. Get rid of your arrogance and many desires, your airs and wanton ambition; none of these benefits your person. What I have to tell you is this, and only this.'" · Narrator: 老子修道德，其學以自隱無名為務。居周久之，見周之衰，乃遂去。 — "Laozi cultivated the Way and virtue; his learning made hiding himself and having no name its task. He lived long in Zhou; seeing Zhou's decline, he then left."
    - **Source:** 史記 卷63 老子韓非列傳 (`sources/lao-tzu/史記_卷063.txt` lines 13, 15). Line 21 is Sima Qian on the identification: 世莫知其然否.
    - **Words are:** `attributed`. The speech is Sima Qian's report of Laozi speaking to Confucius; the second part is the narrator's voice.
    - **From:** top-12 #12

19. **Where others take the front, take the rear; restraint as the rule.**
    - **Kind:** mindset
    - **Rule:** Where everyone takes the front, he alone takes the rear, saying he takes on the world's reproach (variant: filth). Where everyone takes the solid, he alone takes the empty, and where everyone seeks good fortune, he alone stays bent and whole, saying "just escape blame". He takes depth as the root and restraint (約) as the rule, saying "the hard is broken, the sharp is blunted".
    - **Their words:** 老耼曰：「知其雄，守其雌，爲天下谿。知其白，守其辱，爲天下谷。」人皆取先，己獨取後，曰：「受天下之詬（一作垢）。」人皆取實，己獨取虛。无藏也，故有餘，巋然而有餘。其行身也，徐而不費，无爲也而笑巧。人皆求福，己獨曲全，曰：「苟免於咎。」以深爲根，以約爲紀，曰：「堅則毀矣，銳則挫矣。」常寬容於物，不削於人。 — "Lao Dan said: 'Know the male, keep to the female, and be the world's ravine; know the white, keep to the disgraced, and be the world's valley.' Everyone takes the front; he alone takes the rear, saying: 'Take on the world's reproach' (variant: filth). Everyone takes the solid; he alone takes the empty. Hoarding nothing, he has more than enough, abundantly. In conducting himself he is slow and not wasteful; he does nothing and laughs at cleverness. Everyone seeks good fortune; he alone keeps bent and whole, saying: 'Just escape blame.' He takes depth as the root and restraint as the rule, saying: 'The hard is broken; the sharp is blunted.' He is always tolerant of things and does not pare away at people."
    - **Source:** 莊子·天下, the paragraph on 關尹 and 老耼 ([zh.wikisource 莊子/天下](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/天下)).
    - **Words are:** `attributed`. This is the 天下 author's description, with sayings he attributes to Lao Dan. The paragraph opens with 關尹 and 老耼 together; the lines after 老耼曰 are read as about Lao Dan (my reading of the paragraph's order). 知其雄…為天下谷 matches 道德經 ch. 28; 受…詬/垢 echoes ch. 78's 受國之垢; 曲全 echoes ch. 22.
    - **From:** new (your research)

## Gaps

- **What `quoted` means here.** It means "the words of the 老子 text". Authorship by one person called 老子 is not established: Sima Qian names three candidates and writes 世莫知其然否 (`史記_卷063.txt:21`). Guodian carries only about a third of the chapters. Items 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17 have no Guodian witness, and item 11 has Guodian only for ch. 40.
- **Sources I used beyond the run folder, and who made them.**
  - Guodian: an anonymous user transcription on zh.wikisource ([郭店楚墓竹簡](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/郭店楚墓竹簡), section 老子（甲乙丙）, last edited 2019). It is in simplified characters, with ○ for graphs that cannot be typed, slip numbers inline, and no apparatus. I did not check it against the 1998 文物 edition or photos. My Guodian readings are only as good as this transcription.
  - Silk A/B: the zh.wikisource raw transcription [老子 (帛書本)](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/老子_(帛書本)), which is also unattributed. `sources/lao-tzu/silk.txt` is the *校勘版*, which that page itself describes as "甲乙本合并補缺" (A and B merged, gaps filled). It is a composite edition, not a witness. Where A and B differ (chs. 8, 9, 71) or the silk is broken (ch. 48), the composite hides it.
  - Other texts: 史記 卷47, 禮記·曾子問, 莊子·天下, 韓非子·解老 and 韓非子·喻老, all as Wikisource raw wikitext.
  - These fetched copies are in my scratchpad, not in the run. My brief allowed one file. If EJ agrees, `runs/20261013-four-seats/sources/lao-tzu/` is the place to save them so the checks can be repeated.
- **Not opened or not used.** ctext.org's Guodian page asks automated tools not to scrape it, so I did not use it. The IHP Sinica Guodian comparison page refused the connection. I also did not consult the 北大漢簡 Laozi, 高明's 帛書老子校注, or 劉笑敢's 老子古今, so variants they record are not reflected above.
- **Left out because it cannot be stated without adding to the words.**
  - **ch. 60 治大國若烹小鮮.** The words give only the simile: "governing a large state is like cooking small fish". The usual meaning, "don't stir", comes from commentators. 韓非子·解老 has 烹小鮮而數撓之，則賊其澤；治大國而數變法，則民苦之, and 王弼 has 不擾也. The seat brief (`briefs/lao-tzu.md`) cites ch. 60, so if it is used it should carry Han Fei's or Wang Bi's name.
  - **ch. 13** (dropped top-12 #2). The words are 寵辱若驚…吾所以有大患者，為吾有身，及吾無身，吾有何患, but the chapter ends 故貴以身為天下，若可寄天下, so either half alone misleads. Guodian 乙 5–8 has the chapter.
- **Found, verified, and left out at the cap** (not important enough for how he decided, or not separable from items above):
  - **禮記·曾子問.** This is the only recorded live decision by 老聃, reported by Confucius. During a funeral procession the sun was eclipsed, and 老聃 said: 「丘！止柩就道右，止哭以聽變。」 When Confucius asked why not go on, he answered: 「…見星而行者，唯罪人與奔父母之喪者乎！日有食之，安知其不見星也。且君子行禮，不以人之親痁患。」 It is a ruling on a rite. Making it a general rule ("when you cannot know, stop and wait") would add to the words. Whether this 老聃 is the book's 老子 is not established.
  - **史記 卷47 孔子世家.** Laozi's parting words to Confucius: 「聰明深察而近於死者，好議人者也。博辯廣大危其身者，發人之惡者也。為人子者毋以有己，為人臣者毋以有己。」 They are attributed, and they overlap item 18.
