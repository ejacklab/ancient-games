# 孫子 (Sun Tzu) — what the 80% is counted over

## Answer

EJ's process holds in three places — a plan per requirement, old use cases hunted first, and 80% as a target rather than a gate — and it breaks where the denominator is an AI-generated list that was never fixed: you are weighing before you have measured, so the same requirement yields different "80%" on different runs. My reading of the fix: fix the list to requirement-derived units *before* generating, mark basic/rare against a written criterion by the plan's author, let an independent reviewer who can return fail check the marks, and leave EJ owning the criterion and the uncovered (rare) list — and if any of this touches a safety bound, then EJ owns that set as a MUST, not a SHOULD.

## How I decided

1. My #1 gives an order, and it is not the order being used. The list quotes the chain 「地生度，度生量，量生數，數生稱，稱生勝」 (`quoted`, my #1): ground → measure → quantity → number → weighing → victory. EJ's steps go from ground (the requirement) to number (the AI's list) to weighing (80%) with no fixed measure in between. So the percentage is computed before the count is stable. My reading: nothing in my #1 lets you weigh a quantity you have not first fixed. This is the root break; the other three questions follow from it.

2. **What holds.** (a) A plan per requirement is my #1's 「不可不察也」 (`quoted`, my #1) — the matter is examined before anyone moves. (b) Hunting old use cases first is my #8's 「役不再籍，糧不三載」 and 「因糧於敵」 (`quoted`, my #8) — do not levy the same work twice while supply already exists. (c) 80% as a target and not a gate is my #8's 「兵貴勝，不貴久」 (`quoted`) and my #14's 「兵非貴益多也」 (`quoted`) — value the win, not exhaustive duration; do not prize the count for its own sake. I would keep these three.

3. **How the list of "all possibilities" is produced — where it breaks (a).** One AI pass is my #11's `不可象於事` — analogy with what the generator has seen. My #11 (`quoted`) says foreknowledge 「不可取於鬼神，不可象於事，不可驗於度，必取於人，知敵之情者也」 — it must come from people who know the situation. The generated list is a draft, not foreknowledge. Concrete break: prompt A returns 15 cases, prompt B returns 60 for the same requirement; 80% is 12 in one run and 48 in the other, and both are reported as "80%". A second concrete break: the generator pads the list with small variants of one basic case, 80% is met, and the changed behaviour is never touched. My #20 (`attributed`, the bamboo 〈吳問〉) is the mechanism: Fan and Zhonghang counted in small units with a heavy tax and 「范、中行是(氏)先亡」, while Zhao, with the larger unit and no new tax, held. My reading of that for this question: the size of the case unit sets the count and the fate of the suite; define the unit (a behaviour class, not an input value) before generating, or the generator defines it for you.

4. **What the 80% is counted over (a).** My #9 says 「故善戰者，致人而不致於人」 (`quoted`, my #9) — bring the other side, do not be brought; here the generator sets the terms whenever the denominator moves with it. My #10 says 「故兵無成勢，無恒形」 (`quoted`, my #10) — no fixed 勢 and no constant form; one flat 80% across every requirement is a constant form, and my #10's point is to shape the response by the enemy (here, the requirement), not by a template. My reading: count the 80% over a fixed partition of the requirement's acceptance criteria, with every generated case attached to exactly one unit as evidence. The cases are the evidence; the units are the denominator. That is my #1's 度 before 稱. The 80% stays EJ's number — what changes is what it is measured against.

5. **Which cases may be left untested, and who decides (a, c).** My #13 is written for exactly this: 「途有所不由，軍有所不擊，城有所不攻，地有所不爭，君命有所不受」 (`quoted`, my #13) — there are roads not taken, armies not struck, cities not attacked, ground not contested, and commands of the ruler not accepted. So a class of cases may be left untested, and the standing order ("cover 80%") is not binding against a case-level judgement. The decider in the same passage is the one who 「進不求名，退不避罪，唯民是保」 (`quoted`, my #13) — who advances without seeking fame and retreats without avoiding blame. My reading: the person who owns the requirement's risk decides the skip and answers for it; the generator proposes. My #18 gives the shape of the gap: 「圍師必闕」 (`quoted`, my #18) — always leave a gap for a surrounded army; the uncovered set must be a named, deliberate gap, not the remainder.

6. **How old use cases are reused (a).** My #8 prices reuse: 「食敵一鍾，當吾二十鍾」 (`quoted`, my #8) — one measure of the enemy's grain equals twenty of mine; an old case is far cheaper than a new one, so hunting them first holds. But my #11 blocks the inference: `不可象於事` means an old case cannot establish the new requirement, because the situation it knew has changed. My #5 names the failure: 「夫戰勝攻取，而不修其功者凶，命曰『費留』」 (`quoted`, my #5; the list keeps the phrase's meaning disputed) — winning and not finishing is 費留. Concrete break: an old green case still passes, the requirement's changed behaviour is untouched, and the plan counts that green as coverage — that is 費留.

7. **My better approach (b),** built from the above, as defaults (SHOULD at most; MUST only for a safety bound):
   - **(i)** Before any generation, write the requirement's acceptance criteria as a fixed list of behaviour units and count them (my #1's 度 and 數). Generated cases attach to a unit; the count is over units, not over generated text.
   - **(ii)** Grow the list by probe, not only by imagination: my #17's four moves 「策之而知得失之計，作之而知動靜之理，形之而知死生之地，角之而知有餘不足之處」 (`quoted`, my #17) — reckon, stir, make take form, test in contact — plus my #11's sourcing from a person who knows the situation. Each probe that reveals a new behaviour adds a unit.
   - **(iii)** Mark basic/rare against a written criterion, by the plan's author. My #16 says 「約束不明，申令不熟，將之罪也；既已明而不如法者，吏士之罪也」 (`quoted`, my #16) — unclear criterion is the author's fault; a clear criterion not followed is the officers' fault. The independent reviewer checks the marks and can fail (the judge rule already agreed).
   - **(iv)** Do not carry the marking on individual judgement alone: my #12 says 「故善戰者，求之於勢，不責於人」 (`quoted`, my #12) — seek it in the 勢, do not demand it of people. Make the criterion mechanical (a case is basic if removing the behaviour turns a test red), so the mark follows from the case and not from the marker's mood.
   - **(v)** Reuse old cases as supply (my #8) but re-probe each against the changed requirement (my #11, my #17); an old case counts only while it can still fail.
   - **(vi)** Keep the 80% over the fixed units, and keep "every uncovered case is a rare one" — that clause is consistent with my #13's deliberate exception, and the uncovered list is EJ's to own.
   - **(vii)** If a unit is a safety bound, then it is a MUST and EJ marks that set.

8. **The open question — who marks basic or rare.** Default: the plan's author marks each case against the written criterion; an independent reviewer checks the marks and may return fail; EJ owns the criterion and reviews the uncovered (rare) list, not every mark. Reasons: my #16 puts the fault on the author when the criterion is unclear and on the officers when a clear one is not followed, so the marker is the author; my #13 puts the exception with the accountable owner, so the skip list is EJ's; my #11 says the knowledge must come from someone who knows the situation and must be paid for, so the criterion comes from the domain owner and the marking/review is budgeted work, not a free afterthought; my #12 says shape the conditions rather than demand judgement at scale. The reviewer's power is to fail, not to re-mark. **Conditional:** if the plan's author has no knowledge of the requirement's situation (a pure generator), its marks are not foreknowledge under my #11 and the domain owner must supply the criterion or mark; if the requirement carries a safety bound, EJ marks that set as a MUST. My #15 also warns that the marker's own virtues are handles — 「廉潔，可辱也﹔愛民，可煩也」 (`quoted`, my #15) — a marker scrupulous about coverage can be shamed into testing everything, and one who loves the work can be harried into over-testing; the written criterion defends the marker from that.

## What I need to know

1. Whose requirement is it, and who owns its risk? My #11 says foreknowledge must come from a person who knows the situation; I cannot name the marker without knowing who that is.
2. What is a "possibility" a possibility *of* — an input value, an input class, a behaviour, or a user journey? My #20 says the unit sets the count and the fate of the suite, and step 7(i) cannot be built without it.
3. Is any part of this requirement a safety bound? That is the one place the house rule allows MUST, and the one place I would move the marking to EJ.
4. Are the acceptance criteria written and countable before generation? If yes, the denominator can be fixed first. If no, that gap is repaired ahead of the 80%.
5. What number of probes or rounds closes the list? I will not invent it (the house rule); if EJ sets it, the stop condition in step 7(ii) becomes checkable.

## Costs and risks

- Fixing the unit and the count before generating costs time up front and may miss a novel behaviour the generator would have found; if the unit is drawn too large, the coverage number hides real gaps (my #1, my #20).
- A written criterion makes the marking mechanical but can be gamed: a case can be made "rare" by never writing the test that would make its removal go red (my #16, my #13).
- The independent reviewer adds a second reader and a delay, and can be captured by the plan's framing; my #15's five dangers apply to the reviewer too — the guard is the house rule that a judge counts only if it can return fail.
- Reusing old cases is cheap (my #8) but can leave the plan reported as covered while nothing new is tested — 費留 (my #5); re-probing reduces that at the cost of one run per old case.
- A single 80% for every requirement is a constant form; safety-bounded requirements, and requirements whose ground is novel, need a different shape (my #10, my #19).
- Naming the skip list and its owner puts blame on a person; that is the point of my #13, and it is a social cost, not a technical one.

## How we would know I was right

Primary test: run the generator twice on the same requirement with two different prompt phrasings. My prediction — under the current process the denominator moves (the main agent's 15 vs 60 gives 12 vs 48 as "80%"); under my proposal the unit-level denominator is identical across both runs, and only the evidence cases differ. If the unit-level denominator also moves, my proposal fails. This test can fail, and it is run on a planted wrong input (the same requirement, two phrasings).

Supporting test 1 (marking): take a case whose removal turns the suite red, mark it rare in the plan, and hand it to the independent reviewer; the reviewer must return fail. If it passes, the marking scheme is not a check. This check is mine; it tests the marking, not the coverage.

Supporting test 2 (can-it-fail): remove the code that meets the requirement; at least one case must go red. If all stay green, the 80% is counting the generator, not the product. This check is mine, and it establishes only that the cases can fail — it does not establish that 80% is enough.

Supporting test 3 (reuse): keep an old case's input, change the requirement's behaviour, and rerun; if the plan still reports coverage from the old green, that is the 費留 failure (my #5).
