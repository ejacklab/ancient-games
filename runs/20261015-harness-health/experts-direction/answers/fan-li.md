# 范蠡 (Fan Li) — use cases, requirements, and vision

## Answer
EJ's direction holds when the user's basic use cases anchor the work in concrete reality and the AI merely completes details, but it breaks if the AI is allowed to plan early by generating unconstrained use cases or premature requirements that inflate the scope before affairs have run their course. If use cases are used without a formal requirements document, coverage must count over fixed units extracted from the user's accepted cases, and once a requirement is settled at court, it must be strictly followed in execution without shifting.

## How I decided
1. **Verdict — what holds:** EJ's direction to start with basic use cases from the user grounds the process in living human reality, which matches my #11: 「人自生之，天地形之，聖人因而成之」 (`quoted`: "People bring it about themselves, Heaven and Earth give it form, the sage goes by that and completes it"). The user originates the human condition; the AI responds and completes it. Furthermore, using use cases to ground requirements accords with my #2: 「節事者與地」 (`quoted`: "regulating affairs goes with Earth") and my #3: 「待其來者而正之，因時之所宜而定之」 (`quoted`: "wait for what comes and set it right; settle it by what the time makes fitting"). Grounding details in concrete situations as they arise is proper regulation.
2. **Verdict — where it breaks (with a concrete case):** The direction breaks when an AI generates extensive use cases or requirements ahead of time, which amounts to forcing growth and planning early. My #7 warns: 「未可也。蠡聞之，上帝不考，時反是守，彊索者不祥。… 王無蚤圖。夫吳，君王之吳也，王若蚤圖之，其事又將未可知也。」 (`quoted`: "Not yet. I have heard: when the Lord on High has not completed it, keep watch for the time to turn; one who seeks by force is unlucky. … Do not plan early. Wu is Your Majesty's Wu; if you plan for it early, the matter will again be unknowable") and my #3 states: 「時不至，不可彊生；事不究，不可彊成。」 (`quoted`: "When the time has not come, growth cannot be forced; when the matter has not run its course, completion cannot be forced").
   - *Concrete case:* In the n=1 trial, asking the AI to "brainstorm every scenario" generated 200 cases, which surfaced contradictory scenarios against settled decisions and invented clauses with no basis in reality, dropping raw test coverage from 46% to 20%. Forcing speculative scenarios before code or concrete need exists is planning early (蚤圖) and violates my #10: 「無過天極，究數而止」 (`quoted`: "they did not pass Heaven's limit, and stopped when the count was exhausted").
3. **Changes I would make, with their checks:**
   - *Change 1:* Split AI suggestions and accepted use cases into two separate artifacts, and place a strict bound on AI suggestions before asking EJ (my #10: 「無過天極，究數而止」 `quoted` and my #14: 「四封之內，百姓之事，蠡不如種也」 `quoted`). The AI proposes; only EJ accepts. *Check:* An intake gate fails on planted input if an AI-suggested case is directly added to the active test denominator without an explicit acceptance mark by EJ.
   - *Change 2:* Clarify that creating a formal requirement document is optional (accord with the ground and need, my #3: 「因時之所宜而定之」 `quoted`), but any requirement once recorded must be verified as a binding check before execution finishes (my #18: 「審備則可以戰乎？」 `quoted`). *Check:* A check fails on planted input if a plan modifies or skips an agreed requirement during implementation without returning to court for re-approval.
4. **Answer to Tension 1 (Must vs. Should):** "Requirements are SHOULD have" and "requirements are must follow" are not a contradiction; they govern two different moments (my reading).
   - Whether to write a formal requirement document is a matter of regulating affairs by what is fitting: it is "SHOULD have" (optional based on whether the use case needs detail), per my #3: 「時不至，不可彊生；事不究，不可彊成…因時之所宜而定之」 (`quoted`). Do not force unnecessary requirements if use cases are already self-evident.
   - Once a requirement is accepted and established, obeying it is binding during execution: it "must be followed", per my #5: 「夫謀之廊廟，失之中原，其可乎？… 天節固然，唯謀不遷。」 (`quoted`: "To plan it in the temple hall and lose it on the central plain — can that be right? … Heaven's rhythm is like that; only the plan does not move").
   - Under house rules, method rules are SHOULD, while safety invariants are MUST (my reading). Thus, "must follow" in workflow design means an agreed requirement is a binding contract of the plan that cannot be dropped on the battlefield.
5. **Answer to Tension 2 (What the 80% counts over):** R11.1 requires fixed units. If requirements are optional, coverage cannot count over a raw, fluctuating list of use cases, because an AI generator can produce 28 or 200 cases at whim.
   - Per my #3: 「必有以知天地之恆制，乃可以有天下之成利」 (`quoted`: "one must have the means to know the constant measures of Heaven and Earth; then one can have the world's full benefit") and my #10: 「無過天極，究數而止」 (`quoted`).
   - The measure must be constant (恆制). When a piece of work has use cases and no formal requirement document, the denominator must be fixed behavioral units derived from EJ's accepted use cases before test generation begins (my reading). AI-generated cases attach only as evidence to those fixed units and never expand the denominator.
6. **Answer to Tension 3 (Who makes what):** "Suggested by AI, accepted by the user" cannot be a single shared form without violating the one-maker rule (my reading).
   - Per my #14: 「四封之內，百姓之事，蠡不如種也。四封之外，敵國之制，立斷之事，種亦不如蠡也。」 (`quoted`: "Inside the four borders, the people's affairs: I am not as good as 種. Outside the four borders, dealing with enemy states, matters decided on the spot: 種 is not as good as I"). The user owns the people's affairs (the vision, needs, and final verdict); the AI operates on external formulation and suggestion.
   - Per my #11: 「人自生之，天地形之，聖人因而成之」 (`quoted`). They are two artifacts: an AI Suggestion Docket (Maker: AI), and an Accepted Scope Register (Maker: EJ). EJ pulls accepted items into their register. One person/agent per artifact.

## Costs and risks
- **Cost of planning early (my #7):** If the AI suggests dozens of edge-case requirements before concrete usage shows they are needed, effort is wasted evaluating imaginary problems (「彊索者不祥…王無蚤圖」 `quoted`).
- **Risk of unstopped generation (my #10):** Without a set stopping count for AI suggestions, the volume exceeds the user's capacity to review attentively, leading to rubber-stamping or exhaustion (「無過天極，究數而止」 `quoted`).
- **Risk of shifting the plan in execution (my #5):** If requirements are treated as merely optional during coding, decisions made at court are lost in the field, causing regressions and broken contracts (「夫謀之廊廟，失之中原，其可乎？」 `quoted`).
- **Risk of blurred maker roles (my #14):** Allowing the AI to formulate and validate its own use cases erodes the distinction between the sovereign's intent and the advisor's execution (「四封之內，百姓之事，蠡不如種也」 `quoted`).

## How we would know I was right
- **Observable test:** In a task with a fixed set of user use cases, run the AI generator under two conditions: one prompting for 5 edge cases and one prompting for 50 scenarios. If my reading holds, extracting fixed units from the user-accepted cases produces an identical denominator in both runs, and coverage stability is preserved without denominator drift.
- **Planted failure test:** Supply a planted input where an AI-suggested use case automatically enters the test execution denominator without an explicit acceptance mark by EJ; the intake gate must detect the missing maker sign-off and fail.
