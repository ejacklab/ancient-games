# 老子 (Laozi) — eleven harness requirements, verdict per requirement, and what is missing

## Answer

Of the eleven, only R11.2 should carry the list — it is the only one that can fail a result before the result is accepted, and all seven recorded failures have that shape; R11.1's 80% should be reported as a count and kept out of the gates until it has a sample, and R1–R10 stay as they are with their readiness-checkable half separated from the half that can only be judged on a result. If EJ wants one requirement added, it is the one that judges the call itself, because nothing in the eleven looks at what the harness returned.

## How I decided

1. **The list is a list of things that can fail, not a list of things that are written.** My #4: 為者敗之，執者失之 — "One who acts on it spoils it; one who holds on to it loses it" (`quoted`). A health check that reads `~/.claude/settings.json` and reports "deny rules: absent" is holding the whole world in its grip (my #4 also quotes 天下神器，不可為也). It is a valid reading of ten rows; it is not a verdict on the harness. The header the built check already prints — "configured is not enforced" — is the honest boundary, and my answer is that the eleven must not be allowed to blur past it.

2. **The backtest settles the order of the list.** My #7: 民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。慎終如始，則無敗事 — "People, in their undertakings, often fail when they are close to finishing. Careful at the end as at the beginning: then no undertaking fails" (`quoted`). The seven failures are all a call that ended as if it had succeeded: exit 0 with no work, exit 0 with empty output, a hang that never reached an end. They are the shape my #7 names — failure at the end, where nothing was looking. 0 of 7 flagged is therefore not a defect in the thresholds; it is the shape of the instrument.

3. **R11.2 is the only requirement that can fail a result on its own, so it should be the spine and the rest should hang off it.** My #6: 為之於未有，治之於未亂 — "Act on it before it exists; order it before it is in disorder" (`quoted`), and the same item's 猶難之, 故終無難矣 — "the sage still treats things as difficult, and therefore in the end has no difficulty" (`quoted`). R11.2 says the judge must exist before the prompt goes out; that is "acting on it before it exists". R1–R10 are what a judge needs around it; none of them is a judge.

4. **The verdict per requirement.** "Readiness?" means provable before any work is sent out, at no quota.

5. **R11.1: change, and the change is to make the number a report, not a gate.** My #2 again — knowing what is enough is knowing where to stop, and a threshold nobody has measured is a threshold that will be argued about instead of used. If the check cannot say pass or fail on 80%, it may say the count and the ratio and stop: "how many of this design's prompts have no judge" is a fact, "the design fails R11.1" is a verdict on a number with no sample behind it (n=0). My #12 as my reading: 將欲歙之，必固張之 — "About to shrink it, one must first stretch it" (`quoted`) — the list must show what the 80% looks like on real designs *before* anyone shrinks or enforces anything to it; the words do not say who does the stretching, and I do not add that.

6. **Q1 — count over acceptance criteria, not over prompts.** The result is what a judge sees, and a criterion is what the work was wanted for; the prompt is only the vessel that carries it (my #17: 三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用 — "Thirty spokes share one hub; in its nothing lies the cart's use" — and 故有之以為利，無之以為用 — "what is there makes for benefit; what is not there makes for use", `quoted`). Counting over prompts rewards a design that writes many small easily-judged prompts, which is the shape my #14 warns against: 少則得，多則惑 — "little, then gained; much, then confused" (`quoted`). Counting over criteria also shows the real gap: one prompt serving four criteria is one result judged four ways, and three prompts serving one criterion is one gap, visible. If EJ's designs have more prompts than criteria, the two ratios move in opposite directions and the choice matters — which is a question, not a fact I was given.

7. **Q2 — a design standard counts as a judge only when the design names what result it judges and what a pass looks like.** A standard that says "when you are corrected, trace the cause" is a rule, not a judge: it tells a person what to do and cannot return pass or fail on one output on its own. In readiness's own vocabulary it is `reported` — a local file claims it (my #8). My #18, which is `attributed`: 吾所以告子，若是而已 — "What I have to tell you is this, and only this" (`attributed`) — that is the standard for what a standard is worth: it instructs the one who applies it, which is the builder.

8. **The plant for the new checks already exists and costs nothing.** The house rule already says every check is shown failing once on a planted wrong input. The seven recorded failures *are* that plant, with their captured output as the wrong input, and replaying them costs no quota and no canary. The seven must not be hardcoded in the skill, because the skill runs in other projects — they are this run's evidence, and the rule that generalises is "each new check is proved against a real recorded failure of this project".

9. **What the doctor must keep not doing.** My #13: 善有果而已，不敢以取強。果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而勿驕 — "The good one has a result and that is all; he does not dare use it to take strength. Result, and no vaunting; result, and no boasting; result, and no arrogance" (`quoted`) — advice and a snippet, never an edit, and the check does not grow into a dispatcher that fixes what it finds. My #3: 功遂身退，天之道 — "The work done, the self withdraws: this is the Way of heaven" (`quoted`) — when the run ends, the doctor leaves. And no total: a "harness healthy" roll-up containing four `unknown` harnesses is a claim nobody should print (my #14: 不自見故明 — "Not displaying himself, he is clear", `quoted`; my #2).

10. **The cost that decides whether any of this survives: a check that costs more than the work gets skipped.** My #11: 天下莫柔弱於水 — "Nothing in the world is softer and weaker than water" (`quoted`) — a heavy judge is not run, and an unrun judge is the same as no judge. This is why EJ's "fast" is a comparison and not a number, and why the readiness step must stay cheap: my #5, 我無為而民自化 — "I do not act, and the people transform by themselves" (`quoted`) — readiness proves what is installed and gets out of the way.

## What I need to know

Nothing in the eleven judges the dispatch; every judge in the list judges the *work*. My reading: this is the largest hole in the list, and it costs one assertion per call.

## Costs and risks

- **The list grows, and a growing list is a list nobody reads.** My #14 (少則得，多則惑) and my #18 (`attributed`, 吾所以告子，若是而已): four N-items is already more than I would add to a list of eleven without dropping one. My price for N1–N3 is that one existing row must go, and I do not get to choose which.
- **Judges at dispatch are where the quota goes.** Every N1/N2 check is a call. My #11 is the risk named plainly: once the first signal costs real time, it gets skipped, and a skipped judge is worse than a named gap because the design will claim coverage.
- **R5's evaluator agent is the same harness grading itself.** My #16. The cost of keeping it as written is that R11.1's count can be satisfied by judges that share the builder's blind spots — the four failures that "looked like success" are what a self-built judge calls done.
- **Promoting R11.2 to the spine slows the first hour of every run.** My #6 says the difficult work belongs at the easy stage, so the cost is paid on purpose; it must be measured on one real run rather than assumed.
- **A check on config can be confidently wrong after a harness upgrade.** My #8: only by calling the sickness a sickness is one not sick. So a row may not say `verified` unless it carries the version it was verified against; after an upgrade its rows are expected to go to `unknown`.
- **Promoting the 80% to a gate before there is a sample is inventing a number by another route.** My #2 and the house rule: the threshold is EJ's, the sample is EJ's, and until both exist the ratio is reported and stops there.

## How we would know I was right

- **The seven, replayed.** Each recorded failure's captured output is fed to the R11.2 first signal. Seven must fail. If any one passes, the check is not the thing I say it is, and the number to watch is not 80% but 7 of 7.
- **The four agy/opencode failures come from harnesses the check reports `unknown` for.** If N3 is accepted, the report must name them in the same sentence. If a report can say nothing about agy while three of the seven failures are agy's, my answer is wrong.
- **Failure 5 (a plan where an answer was asked) is the sharpest single test** of N2: non-empty output, exit 0, wrong form. If a first signal passes that output, form is not being judged and N2 has not been built.
- **No config row changes the outcome.** The config-only rows (R6, R8, R10) may be shown to fail against the seven and must still fail to catch them. A report that claims otherwise is the failure my #4 predicts.
- **The sample for the 80%.** After three real designs are counted, the ratios are recorded. If they cluster far from 80% in the same direction each time, EJ's number was a guess and the gate would have been wrong — one observed instance is the test.
