# 老子 — who they are and how they decided

## Who they are

Almost nothing about the historical person is known, and the earliest source says so itself. Sima Qian's 《史記·老子韓非列傳》 (vol. 63, c. 1st century BCE — roughly 500 years after the period in question) is the first biography: 老子者，楚苦縣厲鄉曲仁里人也，姓李氏，名耳，字耼，周守藏室之史也 — a keeper of the Zhou royal archive. His entire recorded life is two episodes: Confucius, then51, travelling to Zhou to ask him about ritual, and his own departure west when he saw Zhou declining, writing two parts of a little over 5,000 characters at the gatekeeper Yin Xi's request. **Sima Qian does not even claim to have the right man**: he offers three candidates (李耳, 老萊子, 太史儋) and writes 或曰儋即老子，或曰非也，世莫知其然否 — "or it was him, or it was not; the world does not know whether it is so." The ages he reports (百有六十餘歲，或言二百餘歲) are plainly legendary, and his ending is 莫知其所終 — nobody knows where he ended. No inscription, letter, state record or contemporary text of Laozi survives. The book is worse attested than the man: Sima Qian describes it as two undivided parts with no chapters, and the 81-chapter divisions and chapter titles are a later editorial layer (Wang Bi, 3rd century CE, and the 河上公 tradition) that scholars still argue about — 姚鼐 argued in 1783 that they were arbitrary cuts. The excavated manuscripts show the text still growing: Guodian (c. 300 BCE) preserves only about 31 of the received 81 chapters, in a different order, with none of chapters 70–81; the earliest complete version is the Mawangdui silk manuscript (c. 168 BCE). So the decision rules below are, strictly, the rules of a text of uncertain authorship compiled over centuries — with one exception, Rule 2, the only words anywhere recorded as Laozi actually advising someone.

## Top 10 decision rules

1. **Rule (plain English):** When you must produce an outcome, act so it does not depend on your forcing it — remove the blockage, then leave it alone rather than push.
   - **Their words:** 道常無為而無不為。侯王若能守之，萬物將自化。 — "The Way constantly acts-without-acting, and nothing is left undone. If those in authority keep to it, all things transform of themselves." And 三章: 為無為，則無不治。 — "In acting without acting, nothing is left ungoverned."
   - **Source:** 道德經 三十七章 and 三章 (王弼 received text), wikisource 道德經 (王弼本).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: you own a result and the obvious move is to apply more pressure. → Choice: make the smallest change that lets the situation move by itself, stop there, and accept an outcome you did not design.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian *does* preserve ch. 37 (bundle 甲 unit 1) *and* ch. 32, so the received split of these two passages is early. But the 馬王堆 silk text (帛書 ch. 81, titled 無名) reads 道恆无名，侯王若守之，萬物將自化 — it does **not** have 無為而無不為 at this point; that formula is a received-text feature. Silk also reads 吾將貞之以无名之樸…夫將不辱，不辱以靜，天地將自正 against Wang Bi's 吾將鎮之以無名之樸…夫亦將無欲，不欲以靜，天下將自定. **This matters:** the famous "act by not acting" formula is later than the manuscript that contains this passage.

2. **Rule (plain English):** Advise or act only when the timing is right; when it is not, withdraw rather than press — and show less appetite than you feel.
   - **Their words:** 且君子得其時則駕，不得其時則蓬累而行。吾聞之，良賈深藏若虛，君子盛德容貌若愚。去子之驕氣與多欲，態色與淫志，是皆無益於子之身。吾所以告子，若是而已。 — "A gentleman, when his time comes, drives out; when it does not, he wanders and goes. I have heard that the skilled merchant hides his treasure deep as if his store were empty, and a man of great virtue wears the face of a fool. Take away your arrogance and your many desires, your proud bearing and your wanton aims — none of these will serve you. This is all I have to tell you."
   - **Source:** 史記 卷六十三 老子韓非列傳 (Sima Qian, c. 1st c. BCE), wikisource 史記/卷063. This is Sima Qian's report of Laozi speaking to Confucius, not a text in the 道德經.
   - **How it decides:** Situation: someone asks you a question or wants your involvement, and you have leverage. → Choice: check the timing first — if it is wrong, decline and go; if you do speak, strip your own agenda out of it and give one short answer.
   - **Label:** `attributed`
   - **Manuscripts:** none — this is not part of the book, so there is nothing to collate. It is also the only place in the record where Laozi is shown *deciding* rather than instructing in the abstract, which is why it is ranked this high despite being the least securely his words.

3. **Rule (plain English):** Prefer the smaller, lower, less-travelled option to the bigger, higher, more-travelled one.
   - **Their words:** 曲則全，枉則直，窪則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑。 — "Bent, it is whole; strained, it is straight; hollow, it is full; worn-out, it is new. Less, it is gained; more, it is confused."
   - **Source:** 道德經 二十二章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: two courses are available, one modest and unpromising, one larger and already validated by everyone else. → Choice: take the smaller one. The rule treats scale itself as a source of error, independent of merit.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian is silent — ch. 22 is not among its 31 chapters. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 67, 執一) has 曲則全，枉則**正**，洼則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑 — 直 → 正 in the second pair. The advice is unchanged; the wording of the second couplet is not fixed.

4. **Rule (plain English):** Take the position everyone else rejects, and do the thing that benefits everyone without claiming the credit.
   - **Their words:** 上善若水。水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。…夫唯不爭，故無尤。 — "The highest good is like water. Water benefits all things and does not contend; it dwells where the crowd of people finds distasteful — that is close to the Way… Because it does not contend, it is without blame."
   - **Source:** 道德經 八章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: you can pick the attractive, credited position or the useful, unccredited one. → Choice: take the low, useful one; the value is in the benefit delivered, not in the rank.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** **Guodian is silent — ch. 8 is not in the Guodian corpus at all**, so this is one of the received text's additions as far as the earliest witness is concerned. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 52, 治水) reads 上善**治水**，水善利萬物而**有靜** — "the highest good governs water; water benefits all things *and has stillness*" — not 不爭, and not 上善若水. 不爭 survives only in the chapter's closing line. **This matters:** in the earliest complete witness the virtue is being still, not being non-competing; the non-competing reading sharpens a general principle into a specific competitive one.

5. **Rule (plain English):** Once the work is finished, leave and hand it back — do not sit on it.
   - **Their words:** 功遂身退，天之道。 — "When the work is accomplished, withdraw: that is the Way of heaven." And 二章: 功成而弗居。夫唯弗居，是以不去。 — "He accomplishes and does not dwell on it. Precisely because he does not dwell on it, it does not depart."
   - **Source:** 道德經 九章, with 二章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: a thing you started is now working without you. → Choice: stop attending to it, do not take ongoing credit, and let the result stand on its own.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian **does** preserve ch. 9 (bundle 甲 unit 5). 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 53, 持盈) reads 功**述**身**芮** — i.e. 功述身退, not 功遂身退. **This matters:** 遂 ("succeeds") is an editorial emendation for 述 ("recounts"). Both readings tell you to withdraw, but the received text makes withdrawal follow success, where the silk manuscript ties it to *describing* the result rather than owning it.

6. **Rule (plain English):** Fix it before it breaks, start before you feel ready, and hold the last step to the same standard as the first.
   - **Their words:** 為之於未有，治之於未亂。合抱之木，生於毫末；九層之臺，起於累土；千里之行，始於足下。…民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。慎終如始，則無敗事。 — "Do the thing before it comes to be; govern it before it gets out of order. A tree as big as a man's embrace grows from the tip of a hair; a nine-storey terrace rises from clods of earth; a journey of a thousand li begins with a single step… People in the middle of a task most often fail as it is nearly finished. Be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and nothing will go wrong."
   - **Source:** 道德經 六十四章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: a problem is visible but not yet urgent, or a large task is waiting on a "ready" moment. → Choice: act at the cheap, unignorable stage rather than the dramatic one, and spend your vigilance on the final step rather than the middle.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian preserves ch. 64 **only in fragments — its beginning and its end, not the middle**, so the famous middle section is exactly what Guodian does not attest. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 27, 輔物) opens identically (為之於亓未有也，治之於亓未亂也) but gives 九成之臺，作於**羸土**；**百仞之高**，始於足下 — "bare earth" not "layered soil," and "a hundred *ren* high," not "a thousand li." **This matters:** the received text's two most-quoted images, 千里之行 and 起於累土, are both received-text recastings; Guodian does not cover them, and the silk manuscript neither supports 千里之行 nor the word 累.

7. **Rule (plain English):** Do not try to take hold of the whole situation — the whole situation is not something to be gripped.
   - **Their words:** 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已。天下神器，不可為也，為者敗之，執者失之。 — "Whoever wants to seize the world and act upon it — I see him unable to attain it. The world is a sacred vessel; it may not be acted upon. To act on it is to ruin it; to grip it is to lose it."
   - **Source:** 道德經 二十九章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: someone proposes to control an entire system — a market, a company, a course of events — rather than influence a part of it. → Choice: refuse the grip. Aim at the local, and accept that the total outcome is not yours to hold.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian is silent — ch. 29 is not in its corpus. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 73, 自然) reads 夫天下神器也，**非可為者也** — "is not one who may act upon it" — a sharper prohibition. Its closing prescription also differs: 聖人去甚，去**大**，去**楮**, where Wang Bi has 去甚，去**奢**，去**泰**. **This matters:** the silk manuscript does not merely warn against grasping the world, it specifies that the world is not an agent you may act through at all.

8. **Rule (plain English):** Judge your own performance before you judge anyone else's.
   - **Their words:** 知人者智，自知者明。勝人者有力，自勝者強。 — "Understanding others is knowledge; understanding yourself is clarity. Overcoming others shows strength; overcoming yourself shows real strength."
   - **Source:** 道德經 三十三章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: you have evidence about a colleague, a rival, or a market, and a position on your own conduct. → Choice: treat the second as the harder and more valuable measurement; self-criticism outranks external assessment.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian is silent — ch. 33 is not in its corpus. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 77, 盡己) reads 知人者**知**也，自知者明也 — 知 "knowing" rather than 智 "wisdom." **This matters:** the point is not that self-knowledge is wiser but that it is *knowledge*, the same category as understanding others, only clearer.

9. **Rule (plain English):** Prefer the yielding, unforced position to the aggressive one — aggression loses even when it wins the exchange.
   - **Their words:** 天下莫柔弱於水，而攻堅強者，莫之能勝，其無以易之。弱之勝強，柔之勝剛，天下莫不知莫能行。 — "Nothing in the world is weaker than water, yet nothing can withstand it — there is nothing that can equal it. The weak overcomes the strong, the soft overcomes the hard; all in the world know it, none can practise it."
   - **Source:** 道德經 七十八章 (王弼 received text).
   - **How it decides:** Situation: a conflict where you could escalate or stand down. → Choice: stand down and give way. The claim is specifically that winning the exchange is not the win — which is also the reason it is a decision rule and not a tactic.
   - **Label:** `quoted`
   - **Manuscripts:** Guodian is silent — ch. 78 is not in its corpus, and the received text's own chapter titles note that 70–81 may not have existed when Guodian was copied. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 43, 水德) is nearly identical: 天下莫柔弱於水，而攻堅強者莫之能勝也，以亓无以易之. 陸德明's 經典釋文 (embedded in the 王弼本 Wikisource page) records that 河上公 reads 天下柔弱**莫過於**水. So the rule is stable in the earliest full witness; only its phrasing was ever contested.

10. **Rule (plain English):** Hold to three things — compassion, frugality, and never being the first — and expect no support when you do.
    - **Their words:** 我有三寶，持而保之。一曰慈，二曰儉，三曰不敢為天下先。慈故能勇，儉故能廣，不敢為天下先，故能成器長。…夫慈以戰則勝，以守則固。 — "I have three treasures, and hold them fast. The first is compassion; the second is frugality; the third is not to be first in the world. Compassion makes one able to be courageous; frugality makes one able to have breadth; not being first in the world makes one able to be the chief of craftsmen."
    - **Source:** 道德經 六十七章 (王弼 received text).
    - **How it decides:** Situation: you must choose which qualities to spend on when you cannot keep all three. → Choice: keep compassion and frugality and step back from priority. The chapter explicitly predicts the cost — 今捨慈且勇，舍儉且廣，舍後且先，死矣 — give them up and you die.
    - **Label:** `quoted`
    - **Manuscripts:** Guodian is silent — ch. 67 is not in its corpus. 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 32, 三寶) writes 一曰**茲**、二曰**檢** — phonetic loans for 慈 and 儉, so the three treasures are recoverable but the received characters are editorial restorations — and closes 天將**建**之，如以茲垣之, "Heaven will build him up as if walling him in compassion," against Wang Bi's 天將**救**之，以慈衛之, "Heaven will save him, guarding him with compassion." **This matters:** in the silk manuscript Heaven is a builder acting on you; in the received text it is a rescuer. One rewards you for compounding compassion, the other protects you for having it.

## Gaps

- **The historical person is essentially unattested, and I did not manufacture a date.** No contemporary document names him. Everything above rests on Sima Qian writing roughly500 years after the fact, and Sima Qian himself names three candidates and refuses to choose. Guessing a birth year (571 BCE is tradition, not evidence) would have been the easy way to fill the "Who they are" section and I have not done it.
- **Guodian is silent on 7 of my 10 rules.** Its Laozi corpus covers about 31 of the received 81 chapters; of the chapters I quote (3, 8, 9, 22, 29, 33, 37, 64, 67, 78), only 9, 37 and 64 are present, and 64 only in fragments. This is a finding, not an omission, but it means most of the received text's decisive rules have **no** fourth-century-BCE attestation at all.
- **I could not verify a single word of Guodian's own text.** Wikisource has no Guodian transcription — the 老子 (郭店楚簡本) link on the道德經 version page is a red link. My Guodian statements are therefore limited to (a) which chapters are present, from the Murphy-derived list on English Wikipedia, and (b) the one ch. 19 opening quoted in the Chinese 郭店楚簡 article. I did not infer Guodian readings from memory, and I have not attributed 陸德明's 簡文 glosses to Guodian (see below).
- **A strong rule had to be cut, and it has a textual crux worth flagging.** 道德經 七十一章 — 知不知上，不知知病 (王弼) — is arguably a top-three rule: treat knowing you don't know as good, and pretending to know as the illness. I cut it to make room for Rule 2. Anyone building this seat should decide deliberately, because 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 36, 知病) reads 不知**不知**，病矣 — a **doubled** negation. The two witnesses do not differ in wording; they differ in sense: "not knowing that you know" versus "not knowing that you don't know" point opposite directions about epistemic humility.
- **One variant that would have changed a rule I did not use.** 馬王堆 (帛書 ch. 49) reads 多**聞**數窮 ("much listening exhausts") where Wang Bi has 多**言**數窮 ("much talking exhausts"), ch. 5. If a "say less" rule is ever wanted, the two witnesses recommend different disciplines.
- **陸德明《經典釋文》 has a note at ch. 37 — 簡文作不 — but I do not know what he means by 簡文.** It could be a bamboo witness he saw or a 說文-derived supplement; it predates the Guodian excavation by two millennia and cannot simply be equated with Guodian. I therefore left it out of the rule notes.
- **The 匯校版 base text is not identical to Wang Bi's**, and I quote Wang Bi throughout as instructed. For instance the 匯校版 prints ch. 29 with 不可執也 added, ch. 48 with 王弼's 為道日損 where the silk manuscript has 聞道者日損, and ch. 22 as a quoted block. I checked every quotation above against the 王弼本 page, not against the 匯校版.
- **I did not date the text.** Guodian c. 300 BCE sets a terminus ante quem for parts of it and Mawangdui c. 168 BCE a terminus for the whole, but the received text is demonstrably later and re-edited than both, and I saw no source that lets me place its composition more precisely than "compiled over centuries."
- **No third collation.** Without ctext.org I have Wang Bi, the 帛書校勘版, and the 匯校版's inline notes. A published critical apparatus (高明's 帛書老子校注, 裘錫圭's, or the 郭店楚簡校讀記) would likely surface further variants in exactly the lines I quoted.
