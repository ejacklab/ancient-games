## Costs and risks

- **Four categories against a plan that says six** (my #11). The report and `plan.md` will disagree with the index, and the record must stand alone: cost is EJ reading a plan that promises six and finding four plus two flags. The fix is to write the change into the plan, not to invent two buckets.
- **A wrong category label drops the relevant insight with no check** (my #18, U2). (a) alone and (b) alone share this hole; (c) makes it visible in the citations but does not close it. On today's corpus the hole is biggest exactly where the vision points: `F-05a1b440` and `F-4cbb2f4b` are the same root cause and both are `instruction`, so a question labelled `check` will never see them.
- **Selection off by default** (my #13). With 45%+ of the corpus one topic, a future Dave that loads everything gets a prompt diluted by repetition; the risk is dilution (R5), not overflow — and the switch does not exist yet on the day it is needed, ~7 working days out.
- **The engine decision is only reversible if it is written in one place** (my #5). Changing Dave's engine in the same trial that adds the insight load makes the n=1 unreadable — the same class of confound the Dave review already paid for once. Cost of getting it wrong: the run's headline number means nothing, and it costs a whole trial.
- **Flag-based `engine`/`unconfirmed` will be re-litigated** (my #16, bluntness: politeness ends up hiding the problem). Say in the index that they are flags, in the same sentence, or someone will read them as categories and the counts will disagree.
- **The count discrepancy is real and unowned** (my #1). 62 rows / 64 ids / 58 sections / 4 dangling. If the collector's definition is not written down, the Round-3 test "the index holds every section once" is a coin flip.
- **Haiku is the cheapest and it is a cliff** (R3). If someone picks the cheap tier for the price, the load crosses 100k in about a week and the price goes up 5x on the *whole request* — the cheapest option becomes the most expensive one within the month, in the direction nobody watches.

3. **U2, the honest measure** (my #3: a warning sign is not the thing itself — a test that behaves differently is "a *warning* sign, sure"). Not "Dave looked right", not "Dave mentioned them" — the repeated failure itself is the measurement, and `F-05a1b440`'s own wording ("a second time") is the shape of the thing to count.
4. **U3, the trigger is a number in the state file, not a feeling**: the date the index passes 400 KB / 100k tokens (≈7 working days at the measured rate), and at that date run the same question with and without category selection and compare cited-id accuracy. 5. **The engine choice is falsifiable in one run**: if Dave's citations are wrong or absent while Dario's answer on the same engine is unaffected, the categories are the suspect, not the model; if both degrade with the load and neither without it, it is context rot (R5) and the selection switch was late, not the engine wrong.
6. **The feedback loop closes**: at the end of the run, each of the four advisory points above is marked right or wrong with a pointer. If the pattern has no such record today (my reading: it does not), that absence is the finding, and it is what makes the next run's advice worth more than this one.
