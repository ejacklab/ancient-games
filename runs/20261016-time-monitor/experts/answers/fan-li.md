# 范蠡 (Fan Li) — Time guard reminder frequency

## Answer
Adopt (B): inject a concise one-line status of elapsed and remaining time into the agent's context after every tool call (≈20 tokens), paired with a fuller block reflecting workers' progress at the 50%, 80%, and 100% marks. If context size or token budget is strictly constrained, fall back conditionally to (A), but (B) is the only option grounded in measured research and aligned with pacing against limits.

## How I decided
1. **The agent cannot sense the passage of time without external feedback (my reading).** Research findings F5 and F6 show that language models struggle to internally track elapsed time or count tokens; a budget stated only in the prompt gave 40% adherence versus 100% with external forcing (*quoted* F5, *verified* F6). Under **my #10**, those who used troops well "did not pass Heaven's limit, and stopped when the count was exhausted" (「無過天極，究數而止」, `quoted`). To stop before the limit is exceeded, the agent must know the count as it runs (*my reading*).
2. **Apply what has been tested and proved effective elsewhere (my #16).** In **my #16**, I said: "Ji Ran's policies were seven; Yue used five of them and got what it wanted. Since they have been applied to the state, I want to use them for the household" (「計然之策七，越用其五而得意。既已施於國，吾欲用之家」, `quoted`). In the research, every tested system with measured benefits—BATS (F1, F2), Anthropic context awareness (F3), Anthropic task budgets (F4), and negotiation pacing studies (F6)—injects budget updates continuously or after every step/tool call (*verified* F1–F4, F6). Neither sparse threshold-only marks (A) nor arbitrary step intervals (C) have been measured in the literature (*not measured*).
3. **Join Heaven's time with human affairs (my #1).** In **my #1**, I taught: "Human affairs must be joined with Heaven and Earth as a three; only then can success be achieved" (「夫人事必將與天地相參，然後乃可以成功」, `quoted`). Heaven's time is EJ's deadline; human affairs are the workers' progress on small pieces. Option (B) matches this rule directly: the one-line status keeps the agent continually attuned to Heaven's time, while the richer progress block at 50%, 80%, and 100% joins that timeline with human progress to cross-check whether the work is keeping pace (*my reading*).

Under option (A), an agent that bogs down between 0% and 49% receives no reminder and cannot adjust its pace or shed non-essential work until half the time is already spent; per-call feedback enables timely pacing before the window closes (*my reading*).

## What I need to know
1. **Context and token budget constraints:** If the run's context window is extremely tight or tool calling volume is unusually large (e.g., hundreds of calls where ~20 tokens per call would cause context truncation or excessive inference cost), then conditionally fall back to (A) to preserve space (*my reading*).
2. **Worker reporting volume:** If background workers write progress updates at a very high frequency, injecting full worker status lines on every single tool call would cause prompt clutter, confirming the necessity of (B)'s distinction between a minimal one-line clock tick on every call and the fuller worker report at major marks (*my reading*).

## Costs and risks
1. **Context accumulation cost (from my #11):** In **my #11**, the objective is that "very little force is used" (「用力甚少」, `quoted`). Injecting ~20 tokens on every tool call accumulates tokens over long trajectories (e.g., 50 calls add ~1,000 tokens), slightly increasing token consumption (*my reading*).
2. **Risk of premature truncation or pacing panic (from my #7):** In **my #7**, I cautioned that "one who seeks by force is unlucky" (「彊索者不祥」, `quoted`). As noted in finding F4, continuously reminding an agent of a dwindling budget can cause it to panic, scope down prematurely, or refuse tasks if time runs low (*my reading*).
3. **Risk of arbitrary parameters (from my #18):** Adopting (C) incurs the risk of unexamined preparations (my #18, `quoted`), because picking an unmeasured $N$ introduces untested failure modes (*my reading*).
