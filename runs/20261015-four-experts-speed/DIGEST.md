# Digest — four-experts panel speed, personas, calibration (2026-10-15)
Full findings: research.md (same folder). Status: open.

**Answer.** (1) Output length drives latency: OpenAI's guide says halving output roughly halves latency, while halving the prompt gains 1–5% (*quoted* F1; vendor rule of thumb, `unproven`). In the papers, adding agents gives less than it seems: majority voting explains most of debate's gain (*quoted* F11), and a strong single prompt nearly matches debate (F12). Voting accuracy can rise and then fall as calls increase (F9), which conflicts with F7 (gains up to 40 agents). Mixing different LLMs often lowers ensemble quality (F14), which conflicts with F15. (2) Measured cuts: adaptive or early stopping, up to 7.9x fewer samples (F18, F19); confidence-weighted votes, 40% fewer samples (F20); cascades and routers, up to 98% and 2x cost cuts (F22, F23); sparse topology (F24); terse drafts at 7.6% of the tokens (F30); a budget the agent can see beats a larger tool budget (F31). Prompt caching cuts input cost, but neither vendor gives a latency figure (F25–F27). (3) Expert personas did not improve factual accuracy (F33, F34). One 2023 study found that role-play helped reasoning (F35). (4) Two papers report that RLHF makes models overconfident (F38, F39), and models' wording does not match their internal uncertainty (F41). Remedies with evidence: verbalized confidence (F40), verifying claims independently (F42), and confidence-weighted voting (F20).
**Confidence.** 43 rows: 6 verified (vendor docs about their own products, plus what a guide says), 31 reported (arXiv abstracts read, tables not inspected), 6 unproven (vendor blog and guide claims with no data). No row is unknown. Four disagreements are kept as separate rows.
**Cautions.** All paper rows rest on abstracts read through the fetch tool. Most early-stop results test same-model sampling, not 5 different engines. None was measured on this panel.
