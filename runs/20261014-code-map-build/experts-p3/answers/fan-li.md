# 范蠡 (Fan Li) — bounding attempts and prompt closure

## Answer
P3: not loop-ready (the exit condition moves to the next requirement when one is blocked rather than halting the loop, causing up to 21 subsequent wasted attempts because an unresolved R1 fault causes the evaluator to mark every subsequent requirement NOT MET, while R1 itself cannot pass until unbuilt commands from later rounds exist). To become loop-ready, the loop must halt and escalate immediately when any requirement exhausts its count, reorder R1 after its command dependencies, and close each round promptly once tests and evaluator checks pass without allowing review to shift acceptance criteria.

## How I decided
1. **Bounding the attempts and loop exit (my #10):** Those of old took advance and retreat as the constant, did not pass Heaven's limit, and 「無過天極，究數而止」 — "stopped when the count was exhausted" (`quoted`). The attempt limit of 3 per requirement sets a count, but the exit rule ("the requirement goes to EJ as blocked and the loop moves to the next requirement") violates stopping when the count runs out. When an upstream prerequisite fails, moving to the next requirement does not bound the loop; it continues blindly into failure.
2. **Premature execution and circular coupling (my #3):** When the time has not come and the matter has not run its course, 「時不至，不可彊生；事不究，不可彊成」 — "growth cannot be forced; completion cannot be forced" (`quoted`). The build order places R1 first, but R1's evaluator cases run `map`, `chain`, `tests` and `callers`, and mark R1 NOT MET if any answer is wrong. Forcing R1 to complete before those commands are built in later rounds (A1, A2, A3) is forcing completion before the matter has run its course.
3. **Examining defensive preparations (my #18):** When asked what would make it possible to fight, I asked 「審備則可以戰乎？」 — "If the preparations are examined, can we then fight?" (`quoted`). The evaluator's coupling means an R1 or R3 fault marks every requirement NOT MET. Entering the build loop with R1 coupled to unbuilt tools means preparations have not been examined and the loop's defenses are breached before the first attempt.
4. **Seeking an outcome by force (my #7):** When the Lord on High has not completed it, keep watch for the time to turn, for 「彊索者不祥。…王無蚤圖」 — "one who seeks by force is unlucky. ... Do not plan early" (`quoted`). If R1 fails after 3 attempts, allowing the loop to proceed to R2 through R3 is seeking by force. Because R1 NOT MET poisons every subsequent requirement's evaluator run, burning 3 attempts on each remaining requirement guarantees up to 21 further doomed attempts.
5. **Prompt closure upon passing (my #4):** Following the moment is like fighting a fire: 「臣聞從時者，猶救火、追亡人也，蹶而趨之，唯恐弗及」 — "run and hurry, fearing only not to get there" (`quoted`), and 「得時無怠，時不再來，天予不取，反為之災」 — "Having got the time, do not slacken; the time does not come again; what Heaven gives and you do not take turns into disaster" (`quoted`). When opencode passes the frozen `bun test` suite, passes `bunx tsgo --noEmit`, and the evaluator reports MET with no regressions, the time has arrived: the round must close immediately without delay or slackening.
6. **The plan does not shift during review (my #5):** A plan made in the temple hall must not be lost on the central plain: 「夫謀之廊廟，失之中原，其可乎？…天節固然，唯謀不遷」 — "Advance and retreat change into each other; later you would regret it. Heaven's rhythm is like that; only the plan does not move" (`quoted`). In round step 3, reviewer feedback must not shift the plan. Reviewers must be restricted strictly to APPROVE or a CHANGE tied directly to an unmet spec requirement line, preventing subjective taste from delaying prompt closure.
7. **Aligning external and human conditions before starting (my #1):** Success requires human affairs joined with Heaven and Earth: 「天時不作，弗為人客；人事不起，弗為之始」 — "When Heaven's time has not arisen, do not be the guest [the attacker]; when human affairs have not arisen, do not be the one who begins" (`quoted`), and 「夫人事必將與天地相參，然後乃可以成功」 (`quoted`). Launching the loop while the evaluator checks are coupled to unbuilt commands starts when conditions have not arisen.
8. **Loop-readiness determination (my reading):** Because the exit rule fails to halt on blocking failure and R1 is deadlocked by circular dependency, P3 is not loop-ready as written.

## Costs and risks
- **Halting on block (my #10):** Halting the loop immediately upon an unpassed requirement stops wasteful attempts, but costs EJ's immediate attention and interrupts automated progress.
- **Examining preparations and reordering (my #18, my #3):** Decoupling evaluator cases or reordering R1 after its command dependencies costs setup time and delays starting the P3 build.
- **Strict prompt closure against review creep (my #4, my #5):** Forcing prompt closure as soon as automated checks pass and barring reviewers from shifting criteria risks accepting code that passes fixtures but carries architectural awkwardness or subtle technical debt.
